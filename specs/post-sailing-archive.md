# post-sailing-archive—the Event lifecycle: active, closing, archived, and the record it freezes

The first three children of epic [#134](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/134): [#1149](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1149), the lifecycle; [#1150](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1150), the server-writer compliance; and [#1151](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1151), the durable record. The presentation ([#1152](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1152)) and the media residuals ([#1153](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1153)) follow.

Honor-system-consistent throughout ([ADR 0001](../docs/adr/0001-honor-system-trust-model.md)). Nothing here recomputes, re-derives or "verifies" a Player-written stat—it stops them moving. Archiving is a per-Event state transition, not a migration: the data model is already event-scoped ([ADR 0003](../docs/adr/0003-pool-is-pre-cruise.md)), a future occasion is a NEW Event document, and an archived Event is never reset or reused.

**What these two tickets deliberately do NOT do is render the record.** The freeze carries `status: 'archived'`, `archivedAt`, `archivedUnder` and the `archive` record itself, and the archived surfaces that read it—the frozen Leaderboard, the hall of fame, the Share Card, the listener shutdown—are [#1152](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1152). See § "What the freeze leaves open" for exactly what that means for a Player who opens an archived Event today.

## Contract

### Three states, five fields

`src/domainTypes.d.ts` gains `archivedAt?: number` (the freeze stamp), `archiving?: boolean` (the quiescing phase), `archiveToken?: number` (WHICH quiescing phase—a monotonic generation counter, see § "The quiesce protocol"), `archivedUnder?: number` (the generation the flip named, written by the flip alone) and `archive?: EventArchive` (the durable record, § "The frozen record"), alongside the `status` field that already existed. A sixth field lands beside them but is not part of the lifecycle: `finaleCompletedAt?: number`, the server-written marker recording that every required FINALE beat has landed, which the pre-flip acknowledgement is decided on (§ "The pending-claim drain gate"). All five are ABSENT until the Event is archived or mid-archive, and pass through `eventConverter` untouched exactly as `frozenAt` and `mostLovedPhoto` do—inventing a default could only ever shut an Event nobody shut, or invent a record nobody took.

| State | `status` | `archiving` | Gameplay writes | Reversible |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Active | `'active'`, or absent | `false`, or absent | Allowed | — |
| Closing | `'active'` | `true` | **Denied** | Yes—`abandonArchive` |
| Archived | `'archived'` | `false` | **Denied** | No, not from a client |

`archivedAt` is named at arm's length from `frozenAt` for the reason `standingsFreezeAt` is ([ADR 0011](../docs/adr/0011-scoring-policy-stated-not-inferred.md)): `frozenAt` stamps the FINALE—competitive scoring stops, the podium computes, the closing Day opens, and the Event keeps running. This stamps the END. Two different events, hours or weeks apart.

`status: 'archived'` is not a new field, and it already has one consumer: the admin-alert queue settles or discards uncommitted work when an Event archives ([ADR 0013](../docs/adr/0013-admin-alert-archive-lifecycle.md), `specs/admin-notification-emails.md`). That behaviour is unchanged here—this ticket gives the same flip a second consumer, the rules boundary, and an Admin-facing way to take it.

### The quiesce protocol—`archiving`, then the flip

**The archive is TWO writes, in order, and the order is the guarantee.** A Firestore transaction serializes only against the documents it READS, and the flip reads one document and writes one document—so a Player's Board write, a stat write or a Claim create, each landing in a different collection, is not ordered against it at all. Without a quiesce, a Mark could commit in the same instant as the freeze that makes it the last one, and a Claim could go pending one instant before the freeze that makes it unresolvable. No client-side latch closes that: `hasServerData` and `serverLoaded` say the server has SPOKEN, never that it has STOPPED speaking.

1. **`beginArchive` shuts the Event, and identifies the shut.** One admin-only update setting `archiving: true` and `archiveToken`—the next value of a MONOTONIC GENERATION COUNTER, minted as `stored + 1` inside the transaction that reads the stored one. `eventClosedToPlay` in `firestore.rules`—and its mirror in `storage.rules`—treats that state exactly as it treats `status: 'archived'` for every gameplay write, so from the moment it commits the roster, the Day honours and the Claim queue cannot move again. Admins are bound by it too: a quiescing phase its own organiser can mark through quiesces nothing. The rules ENFORCE the fresh generation (Codex P1, PR #1157 round 6): the shut rides a dedicated arm that requires an `archiveToken` STRICTLY GREATER than whatever the document still carries and touches nothing else, and the general admin arm accepts `archiving` only as `false`—so a reopened Event cannot be shut again under an old generation by an Admin or a stale pre-token client, and a stale `archiveEvent(A)` whose record predates the reopen can never pass `boundToStoredQuiesce`. The shut arm sits AFTER the general arm because that arm is at Firestore's 1000-expression cap and an `allow` placed ahead of it is paid for by every ordinary admin write.
2. **`archiveEvent(token)` flips the Event, bound to that quiesce, carrying the record.** One `runTransaction` update writing `status: 'archived'`, `archivedAt`, `archivedUnder`, the frozen `archive` and `archiving: false` together, after re-reading the Event inside the transaction and confirming it still carries the SAME generation. Its inputs—the roster, the Day honour pins and the Claim queue—are re-read FROM THE SERVER between the two writes, which is the whole point of the ordering (§ "The snapshot").

**The flip is bound to the quiesce it was taken under, because being shut is not the same as being THAT shut** (Codex P1, PR #1139). `archiving: true` says the Event is closed; it cannot say WHICH closing. Between a caller taking a quiesce and its flip committing, an Admin can reopen play (`abandonArchive`), gameplay can resume—Marks, Claims, honours—and a second archive can begin, and the document the transaction then reads carries an `archiving: true` indistinguishable from the first. So `beginArchive` mints a new generation per quiesce, `archiveEvent` requires the transaction's fresh read to carry the one it was handed, and the rules require the flip write to name it in `archivedUnder` (`boundToStoredQuiesce`)—so a direct SDK write carrying a superseded generation is denied at the boundary too, not only by the client that happens to check.

**The generation is a MONOTONIC COUNTER, not a fresh opaque id** (Phase 4b P1, PR #1157 run 4). "Fresh" is a property of history, and the rules have no history: a rule sees the write in front of it and the ONE value the stored document still carries, and nothing else. Comparing the incoming generation against that one value therefore proved only that it was not the immediately preceding generation—it could still be one that had been in force two quiesces ago. Shut under A, reopen, shut under B, reopen, and a delayed SDK shut carrying A passed again (A ≠ B), putting a dead generation back in force; an `archiveEvent(A)` still outstanding from the first quiesce, or a direct flip restating `archivedUnder: A`, then archived a closing state neither had been taken against—the exact ABA the binding exists to refuse, one step further out. A positive integer that must strictly INCREASE on every shut closes it without the rules remembering anything: the stored value is the high-water mark, so every generation at or below it is dead for the life of the Event. `usableArchiveToken` is "an integer greater than zero" on both sides of the boundary, `beginArchive` mints `stored + 1` inside the transaction that read `stored` (so two Admins closing at once serialize rather than writing the same counter twice), and the repair arm below may install a generation only when the stored one is unusable AND may not reset the counter beneath a number the document already carries.

The generation is PRESERVED when `beginArchive` is called on an Event already closing, because that call is idempotent and takes nothing new: re-minting there would abort an in-flight freeze that is still perfectly valid. A closing state carrying no usable generation is refused rather than bound to by guesswork—an unidentified quiesce is exactly the state the binding exists to tell apart from another—and `beginArchive` mints one, so reopening and archiving again is the way through. That state can only come from a hand edit or an Event shut by a build older than this field, which carries a STRING there and is unusable for exactly that reason.

**And `beginArchive` says whether it CREATED the quiesce or JOINED one** ([#1142](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1142) item 6). Because the call is idempotent, a caller that merely joined another Admin's in-flight quiesce comes back holding a token that matches perfectly—so a cleanup keyed on the token alone would succeed at exactly the write the binding exists to refuse, clearing a closing state it never took. `BeginArchiveOutcome` therefore carries `created` beside `token`, and only the creator may reopen automatically. The two conditions are complementary rather than redundant: `created` says this caller owns the quiesce, and `expectedToken` says the quiesce it owns is still the one in force. The field and both semantics ship here, with the quiesce they describe; their one CONSUMER—the automatic reopen after a refused flip—rides with the console's Archive action in [#1151](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1151), because there is no flip to fail from the console on this child.

**The closing state is REVERSIBLE until the flip commits, and inert afterwards.** `abandonArchive` clears the flag, and `archiving` is deliberately outside the write-once clause: the first write shuts gameplay for everyone, so a failed second write, a tab closed mid-flight, or an Admin who changed their mind must not leave a live Event permanently unplayable. That is safe precisely because `status` is NOT clearable—once the flip lands, the freeze is carried by the half that cannot be moved, and clearing `archiving` reopens nothing.

`abandonArchive` deliberately LEAVES `archiveToken` in place rather than clearing it. It only ever means anything while `archiving` is true, and it is what the next `beginArchive` counts up from—clearing it would restart the counter at 1 and hand every superseded generation back. A freeze still in flight under an old generation sees a number that has moved past it and aborts, which is the whole point, so a stale generation cannot be laundered into a match.

**The flip is refused from anywhere else.** `status` may only become `'archived'` from a stored document already carrying `archiving: true`, so a direct SDK write cannot skip the window in which gameplay was already shut—the ordering is a rules invariant, not a convention one client happens to follow.

`archiveEvent` re-reads the Event inside its own transaction and returns `already-archived` rather than re-flipping, so a double tap—or a second Admin's tap—can never restamp the freeze, and `not-closing` if the quiesce was lifted underneath it.

### The frozen record

`EventDoc.archive` is an `EventArchive`: the final Leaderboard standings, the Event-wide First to BINGO with the holder's own kept row, the per-Day honours, the resolved Standings Freeze the whole thing cut on, and the Event's own name. It is written ONCE, by the flip, and locked by `firestore.rules` with `status`, `archivedAt` and `archivedUnder` from that instant. That lock is what "the final Leaderboard and hall of fame persist unchanged" means at the boundary: a second archive pass cannot overwrite it with a later roster, post-freeze moderation cannot edit it, and un-archiving is not a client operation.

**Every number in it is COPIED off a Player-written row, never recomputed** ([ADR 0001](../docs/adr/0001-honor-system-trust-model.md)). `bingoCount`, `squaresMarked`, `blackout` and `firstBingoAt` are taken verbatim from `players/{uid}`, including where a row's root totals disagree with the sum of its own `dayStats`—the Player wrote both, and the archive records what the Leaderboard showed rather than adjudicating between them. The only derivations `src/data/eventArchive.ts` performs are the ORDER (`sortPlayers`) and the honour SELECTIONS (`eventFirstBingoWinner`, `pinnedOrDerivedDailyHonors`), and both are the very selectors the live Leaderboard already renders with, reused rather than restated: the frozen record must say what the last live Leaderboard said.

`standings` retains a bounded prefix in rank order (`MAX_ARCHIVED_STANDING_ROWS`, 200) and `playerCount` records the complete ban-filtered cardinality, mirroring `MostLovedPhotoAward`'s `winners`/`winnerCount` pair. The Event document has one 1 MiB budget that `days`, `bannedUids` and `mostLovedPhoto` already draw on, so an unbounded roster copy is the one field that could make the document unwritable. Both live Events have two-figure rosters, so in practice nothing is ever dropped.

**And the two are ONE contract at the boundary, not two typed fields** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). `standings.size() == min(playerCount, 200)` is the whole relationship, and until this it was asserted nowhere: `completeArchiveRecord` typed the list and the count separately, so an authorized admin flipping DIRECTLY rather than through `draftEventArchive` could freeze `playerCount: 0` beside a non-empty list, or a list past the bound the document's budget depends on. Both are permanent, because the flip is irreversible and locks `archive`, and both are silent, because every archived surface reads the count as the roster size and the list as its rank-ordered prefix. Rules cannot walk a list, but a list's SIZE is one expression, so the rows stay the stated residual while the COUNT of them no longer is (`standingsSizeMatches`, the flip arm's own helper family). The writer produces exactly that pairing—`ranked.slice(0, 200)` beside `ranked.length`—and `writableArchiveRecord` asks the same question on the near side of the quiesce.

**A ban HIDES, it never reassigns.** The headline honour is selected over the FULL roster and then dropped to `null` if its holder is banned, so a ban can never promote a later Player into an honour that already happened—the same rule the live pin applies (`specs/w2-ban-console.md` § Leaderboard), made permanent. The holder's own standings row rides beside the honour as `firstBingoRow`, with the rank they held in the COMPLETE standings, because the two bounds are independent: the prefix is cut by RANK and the honour is decided by who bingoed EARLIEST, so on a large roster the holder can fall outside the retained rows entirely and a card that names them would have no row to print (Codex P2, PR #1139). The pair is `null` together or a matching pair, never one of each, and `firestore.rules` refuses every other shape.

**A pinned honour is hidden by the BAN LIST and by nothing else** ([#1146](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1146), [#1142](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1142) item 8). `pinnedOrDerivedDailyHonors` used to drop a day-meta pin whose holder was absent from the supplied roster, which read roster ABSENCE as a ban—so an Admin deleting a Player row left that Player's honour on the live Leaderboard strip (which checks `bannedUids` explicitly) and dropped it from the record, permanently. A pin is a write-once day-meta document carrying its own name and instant; it needs no Player row to render, and the only reason to hide one is the ban policy. The list is now passed explicitly, and the live strip, the frozen podium and the record all resolve the precedence through that one helper.

**Two names are frozen rather than read live, because the freeze deliberately leaves both editable.** `EventArchive.eventName` copies `EventDoc.name`, and `ArchivedDayHonor.dayLabel` stores the chip label `dayHonorChipLabel` rendered from the schedule the record was taken against. The write-once clause protects `status`, `archivedAt`, `archivedUnder` and `archive` and nothing else, so an Admin renaming the Event or re-theming a Day afterwards would silently re-title a frozen card or re-label a frozen honour—and two people sharing the same standings a week apart would get two different images of them, which is the one thing a permanent record promises it cannot do (Codex P2, PR #1139).

### The snapshot

**The record is built from reads taken AFTER the close, from the server.** That is the whole point of the two-write ordering. A passive listener re-delivers only when its documents CHANGE, so "wait until the roster subscription is server-confirmed again" would deadlock in precisely the case the quiesce creates—nothing moving. An explicit `getDocsFromServer` issued once the closing write is acknowledged returns the state of a collection that can no longer change: the strongest form of "confirmed after the close" available to a client, rather than the weakest. It is a READ, not a recompute—every number still comes verbatim off the Player-written row, through the same converters the live Leaderboard reads; only the freshness differs.

The Event document itself is re-read RAW inside the transaction (the `setDayTheme` / `confirmClaim` discipline), so the ban roster, the schedule and the name the record freezes against are the stored ones. Its `days` are normalized through `migrateDayFields` for the DERIVATIONS only, because `dayHonorChipLabel` resolves a Theme's emoji and every live surface hands it Days the converter has already normalized—an unknown or off-Edition persisted theme would otherwise freeze a label the last live strip never showed.

**The CONFIGURATION the snapshot is defined by is held across the reads** (Codex P2, PR #1139). The quiesce shuts gameplay, not administration, so an Admin can still edit the Event between the pre-read and the commit. `archiveSnapshotFingerprint` captures `claimMode`, `days`, `standingsFreezeAt` and `frozenAt`—the four fields that decide which Day pins were fetched, which Days are Tutorial, where a missing freeze is derived from, whether the drain gate applies at all, and where the honour cutoff falls—and the transaction refuses with `config-changed` rather than combining reads taken for one configuration with a record built for another. It sorts object keys, because `JSON.stringify` follows insertion order and the SDK promises nothing about reproducing it across two decodes; a comparison that could report a spurious change would abort archives at random. `bannedUids` is deliberately OUT: moderation stays open across the quiesce on purpose, and a ban is applied to the rows the record keeps rather than deciding which rows were read, so a ban landing mid-snapshot changes what the record CONTAINS in exactly the way it should. `finaleCompletedAt` is OUT too, even though it is what the finale gate reads (Codex P1, PR #1162): that gate is evaluated against the TRANSACTIONAL read—the state the flip actually lands on—and the marker only ever moves from absent to stamped, so a finale finishing mid-snapshot can only make the archive more permitted and needs no abort.

**And the canonicaliser is FIRESTORE-AWARE and CYCLE-SAFE, because it is handed RAW documents** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). It fingerprints `EventDoc.days`, which no rules arm validates, so an Admin-SDK repair or a console hand edit can leave a native Firestore value sitting in it—and a plain recursive walk enumerated those as ordinary objects. A `DocumentReference` carries an ENUMERABLE `firestore` back-reference pointing at a graph that contains the reference again, so the walk cycled and threw `RangeError: Maximum call stack size exceeded`. That throw landed after play had closed and OUTSIDE every `archiveRead` wrapper, so `archiveEvent` rejected instead of returning a refusal, the console's automatic reopen never ran, and the Event was left stuck closing—the one outcome the two-write protocol exists to make impossible. The four Firestore types are now reduced to the scalar the SDK itself round-trips them by (`Timestamp.toMillis()`, `GeoPoint`'s latitude/longitude, a reference's `path`, `Bytes.toBase64()`), recognised by SHAPE rather than by `instanceof`—this module is Firestore-free on purpose, and duck-typing also survives two copies of the SDK in one bundle—while a genuine back-edge and a primitive JSON cannot represent are tagged with a token no other branch can emit. The tracker holds the current PATH rather than every node visited, so a subobject repeated in a TREE still serialises identically both times and only a real cycle is tagged.

**And every one of those four shapes is discriminated by a FUNCTION, so a plain map is never mistaken for one** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162), round 8). The reduction is deliberately lossy—the whole value collapses to one scalar—so a shape that also matches an ordinary stored map drops every other field from the fingerprint, and two reads that differ would then compare EQUAL: `archiveEvent` skips its `config-changed` abort and freezes honour pins fetched for a schedule that no longer exists, on the one write that can never be amended. The reference branch asked only for a string `path` beside a map `firestore`, and both are shapes a Day can hold, so a stored map carrying those two field names was reduced to its `path` and its `index`, `theme` and `unlockAt` stopped being compared at all. Each branch now requires a marker Firestore's own data model cannot express: `toMillis` for a `Timestamp`, `toBase64` for `Bytes`, `isEqual` beside the coordinates for a `GeoPoint`, and `withConverter` or `isEqual` beside the `path` for a reference—all FUNCTIONS, and a value decoded from Firestore is built out of Firestore's types, none of which is one. Two markers are accepted for a reference because the SDKs disagree: the modular client's carries `withConverter` and dropped `isEqual` at v9, the Admin SDK's carries both. `firestore` is no longer asked for at all—it named the cycle the branch exists to avoid, never the type. A plain map is walked normally, which is the answer that keeps every field it holds inside the comparison.

**And a document that cannot be fingerprinted at ALL is a refusal, not a throw** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). The braces above close the concrete cycle; this is the belt beside them, and it is there because of WHERE the fingerprint runs rather than because of any value in particular. Both calls are taken after `beginArchive` has shut the Event, one of them outside every `archiveRead` wrapper and the other inside a transaction whose rejection is otherwise read as a failed COMMIT—the one outcome that must keep surfacing as a thrown failure. So a throw from either becomes `config-unreadable`, which writes nothing and joins the refusals the console reopens play after. Anything that can still throw in there is a property of the STORED document—a throwing getter, a Proxy that refuses enumeration, a `toMillis` that is not the SDK's—so a second attempt would meet it again, and the copy says so rather than naming a lever that does not exist.

**Every read names the Event explicitly** ([#1142](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1142) item 7). `EVENT_ID` is a live binding that a hostname change reassigns, and the freeze takes four awaited reads before it writes—so a path helper resolving the binding per read could freeze one Event's roster and honours onto another, with the generation and configuration checks validating only the first. The Event is captured once, up front, threaded through `evt` / `claimsRaw` / `playersCol` / `dayMetaRef`, and reported by `beginArchive` so the console's own shut → freeze → cleanup sequence names one Event too.

**The flip is bound to the quiesce BEFORE the reads as well as inside the transaction.** A caller holding a generation the document has already moved past spends four round trips building a record the commit must refuse anyway; the pre-check saves them and reports `quiesce-changed` from the Event read it already took.

**A read that does not ANSWER is a refusal too, and it says which read** (CodeRabbit Major, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). Every one of these reads is taken after the closing write has already landed, so a rejection thrown out of `archiveEvent` is not a failed call—it is a LIVE Event shut to gameplay, with no record, and nothing for the console to clean up after. That is precisely the outcome the two-write ordering exists to make impossible, and it was the ordinary consequence of an offline tab or a dropped connection: `getDocFromServer` has no cache to fall back on by design, the throw went past every branch of the console's `runArchive`, the automatic reopen never ran, and the Admin was left with the generic `AsyncButton` failure pill over an Event nobody could play on. So each read reports `read-failed:<stage>`—`event`, `claims`, `roster` or `day-meta`—which the console treats exactly as it treats the four refusals below: it writes nothing, it is stated in the status line, and an open-phase **Archive** that created the quiesce puts play back. The four stages are named separately because they fail for different reasons an Admin can act on: the Event and the roster are what a connection drop takes out, the Claim queue is what an Admin who has just lost their admin claim is denied, and a Day's honour pin is what a hand-edited schedule can point at a path that is not there. `'event'` covers both Event reads—the pre-read and the transaction's own—because they are the same document read twice and the remedy is identical.

**And a stored SCHEDULE the raw read cannot use is a refusal too** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). `EventDoc.days` is admin-written and its rules arm validates no entry, so an older seed, an Admin-SDK repair or a console hand edit can leave a `null` in the list—and `eventConverter` TOLERATES exactly that, because `migrateDayFields` reads a nullish entry as `{}`. So the Admin console renders, previews and ARMS the archive over such an Event perfectly happily, and then the freeze's raw post-quiesce read dereferenced `d.index` on that entry and threw: after `beginArchive` had closed play, and outside every `archiveRead` wrapper, so `archiveEvent` rejected instead of returning a refusal, the console's automatic reopen never ran, and the Admin was left with the generic failure pill over an Event nobody could play on. The raw read now normalizes its entries through the converter's OWN helper before it reads an index off one, so the two halves of the console agree about what a Day is, and an entry that still has no usable index is refused as `schedule-unusable` rather than addressed. `index` is the one field `migrateDayFields` does not default, because there is nothing to default a Day's identity to: it is the `days/{dayIndex}` path segment every honour pin is fetched by, so a missing or fractional one would read a document at `days/undefined` and freeze whatever it found there as that Day's honour. It joins the refusals the console reopens play after, with the one repair message here an Admin can act on from the surface this control already sits at the bottom of.

**And a schedule that names the same Day TWICE is refused by the same clause** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). A repeated integer index is perfectly READABLE—both entries address a real document—which is exactly why the unusable-index check let it through, but the two entries are not two Days. Every Day-keyed structure downstream keys on the index rather than on the position: the freeze's own `Map<number, DayMetaDoc>` collapses the two reads onto one entry, while `pinnedOrDerivedDailyHonors` flat-maps over the schedule ENTRIES and emits that one Day's honour once per entry. The record would then carry the same `dayIndex` twice, permanently, against a `dailyHonors` contract that is one honour per Day—and `completeArchiveRecord` cannot look inside a list to refuse it. The refusal is `schedule-unusable` for the same reasons the unreadable index is: it writes nothing, the console reopens play, and the repair is on the surface the control already sits at the bottom of. A UNIQUE non-contiguous schedule stays perfectly usable, which is the whole point of keying on `DayDef.index`—a one-Day Event at index 4 is a schedule the freeze reads correctly, not a broken one. Both shapes are asked through ONE predicate (`usableDayIndexes` in `src/data/eventArchive.ts`), which is what stops the freeze and the console drifting apart about which schedules are usable. `draftEventArchive` also dedupes its carried honours by Day index, keeping the FIRST entry—the same one `dayMetas.get(index)` answered with, so the deduped record is exactly the one a schedule naming that Day once would have produced. That is defence in depth rather than the fix: the builder is called from surfaces that never went through the freeze's gate (the console preview, the tests, any later caller), and the record is the one write that can never be amended.

**And the frozen honours are ordered by DAY INDEX, whatever order the schedule lists its Days in** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162), round 8). `EventArchive.dailyHonors` declares itself ordered by Day index (`src/domainTypes.d.ts`) and every archived surface renders it straight through, so the order is part of the permanent record rather than a rendering preference. `pinnedOrDerivedDailyHonors` flat-maps over the schedule's ENTRIES, and a schedule listing `[{index: 4}, {index: 1}]` is a legitimate one—the indexes are unique, `usableDayIndexes` accepts it, and keying on `DayDef.index` rather than on the array position is the whole point of the check above—so the honours arrived in schedule order and froze that way, leaving every consumer to show the Days out of chronological sequence or to re-sort defensively against a contract that already promised the order. So `draftEventArchive` sorts its carried honours by `dayIndex` before the record is built: sorted rather than refused, because there is nothing wrong with the schedule, and sorted on the DEDUPED list so the console's preview and the frozen record come out of one expression. The scheduleless path was never affected—`perDayHonors` sorts its derived list already—so this makes the two paths agree rather than imposing something new on one of them. `writableArchiveRecord` asserts the order too (`ascendingHonorDays`), strictly ascending so the same clause re-states the one-honour-per-Day contract; `firestore.rules` cannot ask it at all, because rules have no iteration and no way to relate one list element to the next, which is exactly why the pre-quiesce check has to.

**And so is the LIVE display the record promises to preserve** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162), round 9). Sorting only in the builder ordered the record and left the display it copies unordered, which is the same split from the other side: `pinnedOrDerivedDailyHonors` handed back the schedule's own order, and the podium (`buildPodium`) and the Feed's honours line render that list straight through, so on a `[{index: 4}, {index: 1}]` schedule they showed D5 ahead of D2 against a record that says `[1, 4]`. So the SHARED SELECTION is where the order comes from now—ascending by `dayIndex`, on the array its own `flatMap` minted, stable so a repeated index still yields the entries in the order the builder's first-entry-wins dedupe reads them. `Podium.dailyHonors` declares itself sorted by Day index exactly as `EventArchive.dailyHonors` does, so this is the contract both of them already stated, answered once instead of re-asserted defensively by each consumer. The Leaderboard's own strip is the single surface this does not reach, because it does not render the selection: it renders a chip for every Day the SCHEDULE names, winnerless Days included, and reads each holder out of the selection by index—so it orders its chips by `DayDef.index`, on a copy of `EventDoc.days`, to the same answer. `draftEventArchive`'s own sort is KEPT rather than removed. It is now a no-op on every list the selector can produce, but the two are not the same guarantee: `writableArchiveRecord` only ASSERTS the order, and that assertion fires as `record-unwritable`—a refusal that reopens play with nothing frozen—so a regression in the selector's order would take the archive down rather than merely render out of sequence, and the builder is called from surfaces that never went through the freeze's gate.

**And "a Day index" means one the contract actually has—`0 … MAX_DAYS - 1`** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162), round 7). The predicate asked `Number.isInteger`, and `-1`, `10` and `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER + 2` all pass it while naming no Day: `DayDef.index` is declared `0..9` (`src/domainTypes.d.ts`) and `MAX_DAYS` in `src/data/eventLimits.ts` says why that is a RULES fact rather than a preference—`daysThemeLockOk` unrolls the schedule lock over exactly ten array positions, so an eleventh Day sits outside the lock the moment it is written. These are WORSE than the unreadable index above rather than milder, because they are perfectly addressable: `days/-1/meta/-1` is a real path, the honour pin fetch succeeds, and the freeze could permanently carry an `ArchivedDayHonor` chipped `D0` or `D11` inside a `dailyHonors` list `firestore.rules` cannot walk to refuse. The same integer arrives from the other side too, off a `dayStats` KEY: that map is Player-written under [ADR 0001](../docs/adr/0001-honor-system-trust-model.md) with a rules arm that validates nothing inside it, so a row can name Day 4000 and an Event with no schedule at all renders the derived honours straight through. So ONE predicate states the range—`supportedDayIndex`, beside the `MAX_DAYS` it is derived from—and four callers ask it: `usableDayIndexes` (and through it `archiveEvent`'s `schedule-unusable`), the console's honour fan (`useDayMetasStatus`, which therefore subscribes to no such path either), `pinnedOrDerivedDailyHonors`' derived selection, and `draftEventArchive`'s carried-honour filter, which is where a PINNED honour on such a Day is discarded for every caller that reaches the builder without the freeze's gate. A unique GAP is still not an out-of-range index: a one-Day Event at index 4 is read, previewed and frozen exactly as before. The console's own copy names the defect rather than calling the day unreadable, because an Admin sent looking for a broken entry would find one that looks perfectly fine.

**And the CONSOLE has to address the same Days the freeze does** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). The freeze reads `days/{d.index}/meta/{d.index}` for every `DayDef.index` in the stored schedule; the console's own honour fan (`useDayMetasStatus`) took the schedule's LENGTH and subscribed to `days/0 … days/n-1`. Those are the same set of documents only while the schedule is contiguous from zero—a property `dayCompletenessIssues` enforces on a DRAFT in the setup wizard (`days[position].index === position`, so consumers that read `days[i]` by position deal the right Day) and which nothing revalidates on a STORED Event: `EventDoc.days` is admin-written with no per-entry rules validation, legacy and seeded Events never went through the wizard, and every day-scoped PATH in the estate keys on `DayDef.index` rather than on array position ([#447](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/447)'s Phase 4b precedent, which `useMyDayBoards` already follows). On a one-Day schedule at `index: 4` the console therefore confirmed `days/0/meta/0`—a document that is not there, which the server answers as an ordinary "no pin here"—so every gate passed, the preview fell back to the roster-DERIVED honour or to none, the Admin armed and archived, and `archiveEvent` then read `days/4/meta/4`, found the real pin, and froze a DIFFERENT honour from the one on the screen that was approved. Irreversibly. The hook now takes the schedule's actual indexes and its `loaded`, `serverLoaded`, `serverConfirmed` and `failed` all key on those, and the Leaderboard's strip and Board's own honours read pass them too. A non-contiguous schedule is NOT rejected—no spec forbids one on a stored Event, and refusing would strand an Event over a shape the freeze itself handles—but an index that is not an integer is not ADDRESSED either (there is no document at `days/undefined`), and a repeated one is collapsed so the completion tests, which count distinct Days, can still complete.

**But NORMALISING is not the same as ACCEPTING** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). Both shapes the fan normalises away are ones the freeze REFUSES as `schedule-unusable`, and collapsing them silently is what let the console arm over a schedule the flip was going to turn down: the fan completed, every latch went true, the Admin closed play, the flip refused, and the handler reopened it—a round trip through a shut Event for a defect that was on screen the whole time. The normalisation stays, because the fan still has to address something and a list it cannot complete would hang every gate on this hook; what changes is that the hook REPORTS it had to, as `scheduleUnusable`, asked through `archiveEvent`'s own `usableDayIndexes` rather than restated. The Archive control is a fourth precondition shut on it—on both surfaces that reach the flip—with its own sentence naming the same repair the post-flip refusal names, stated ahead of the loading line because nothing here is still arriving.

**What is deliberately NOT swallowed is the WRITE.** A failed commit is a failed archive, and reporting "nothing was frozen" about one would be a claim the caller cannot make. That is why the transaction's own Event re-read is handled differently from the four above: `runTransaction` rejects for both reasons and the two are indistinguishable from outside, so the read is flagged and RE-THROWN rather than caught—the SDK still gets to retry a transient one, exactly as it did before—and the classification happens outside the transaction, where a rejection is known to have written nothing. A rejection with no read flagged against it is re-thrown untouched.

### The inputs are validated BEFORE the Event is shut

`players/{uid}` is self-written under the honour system and its rules arm validates NOTHING—not `uid`, not `displayName`, not either count. So a Player can delete a field from their own row, or store a megabyte-long name, and a copy taken naively would either refuse to serialize (Firestore rejects `undefined`) or push the Event document past its 1 MiB limit. Either way EVERY archive attempt would shut the Event and then fail, which is the worst outcome the protocol can produce: a live Event left unplayable with nothing to show for it. That is why the checks live beside the builder in `draftEventArchive`, and why the Admin console reads them before it takes the quiesce.

- **A row with no usable `uid` is SKIPPED and counted.** It cannot be ban-filtered, matched against the headline honour or rendered, so there is nothing to default it to; the confirm row states the count so an Admin is told what the record will not contain rather than discovering a short roster afterwards. Dropped BEFORE any selection, so an unidentifiable row can never take an honour either.
- **And the `uid` it is asked about is the DOCUMENT ID, never the stored field** ([#1151](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1151), Codex P1, PR #1162). The rules arm binds the PATH — `isOwner(uid)` — and validates nothing inside the document, so `players/{uid}.uid` is an unbounded Player-written string that happens to share a name with the row's real identity. A record that copied it froze a 300 KB field into a 256 KiB ceiling and refused `too-large` on EVERY attempt, permanently, on an Event the closing write has already shut — and the same field is what `isBanned` and every honour match read, so a stored uid naming somebody else evaded a ban and matched another Player's honour besides. `playerConverter` pins `PlayerDoc.uid` to `snap.id` for every reader, which is what `itemConverter`, `proofConverter`, `claimConverter` and `momentConverter` already do for their own ids; the console's preview and the freeze's server re-read both go through it, so the two cannot disagree. `MAX_ARCHIVED_UID` (128, the longest uid Firebase Auth mints) is the backstop for the ids no rules arm ever saw — an Admin-SDK repair, a seed script, a console hand-edit — where a path segment may run to Firestore's own 1500-byte limit; an id past it is SKIPPED and counted exactly as a missing one is, because it is the same field failing the same question, and because a Player row's id cannot be renamed, only deleted, so refusing would strand the Event on a row the record can simply leave out. The headline pair needs no separate bound, because it is selected from these same rows. A Day honour's PINNED holder is the one uid that is not, and it gets its own clause below.
- **And a PINNED daily honour's holder is asked the same question, with the pin DISCARDED when it cannot answer** ([#1151](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1151), Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). This spec used to claim a pin's holder was `request.auth.uid` "by that arm's own clause", and that is only true of the arm's PLAYER branch. The Day-meta create is `(firstBingo.uid == request.auth.uid || isAdmin(eventId))`, so on the ADMIN branch `uid` is not type-checked at all—`displayName` and `at` are, and it is not—while the Admin SDK beside it is constrained by no arm whatsoever. A pin can therefore arrive carrying a `uid` that is not a string, which is a defect the SIZE refusal cannot catch (it is a shape, not a length) and which `completeArchiveRecord` cannot catch either, because Rules cannot look inside a list: the flip SUCCEEDED and froze an `ArchivedDayHonor` violating its own declared shape, permanently, on every archived surface that renders one. So the builder asks `usableUid` of a pinned holder exactly as it asks it of a roster row's document id, and an honour that fails is discarded and counted in the draft beside the skipped rows, where the confirm row states it. **The Day is then recorded as having NO honour—the pin is hidden, never reassigned.** That is the BAN rule rather than the missing-pin rule, deliberately: derivation from the roster is what an UNPINNED Day gets, and this Day is pinned. The pin is write-once and its existence says an honour was claimed; the only thing wrong with it is that the record cannot express WHO holds it, so handing that Day to the roster's runner-up would name somebody the pin does not—the one adjudication this module must never make ([ADR 0001](../docs/adr/0001-honor-system-trust-model.md)), and exactly the clause `pinnedOrDerivedDailyHonors` already applies to a pin whose holder is banned. The honour's other copied scalars need no discard, because they are already coerced on the way out precisely as a standings row's are: `displayName` through the trimmed, defaulted, `MAX_ARCHIVED_DISPLAY_NAME`-bounded stand-in, and `firstBingoAt` through the non-finite-reads-`0`, finite-is-clamped coercion every number in the record carries. `uid` is the only one that cannot be coerced, because there is nothing to default an identity to.
- **Every other malformation is COERCED to a safe default:** a missing name reads `'Anonymous'` (the estate's existing stand-in), a missing or non-finite count reads `0`, a bad instant reads `null`, and every name is bounded at `MAX_ARCHIVED_DISPLAY_NAME` (100—the cap `firestore.rules` already enforces on every other Player-authored display name, where the profile editor's own limit is 40). None of that adjudicates a stat: it decides nothing about who won, it only makes the row expressible in the record's declared shape.
- **Per-Day buckets are made READABLE before any selector reads one.** `dayStats` is a Player-written map that can hold a `null`, a string, or a bucket missing every field, and the honour selectors dereference the bucket directly—so one such row THREW out of the builder. Because the console builds the draft during RENDER, that exception took Game settings and its Reopen play control down with it, on an Event that may already be shut. A `dayStats` that is not a non-null, non-array object at all—`null`, a string, an array, never a map—reads as absent, where the row used to pass through with that value untouched. A bucket that is not a non-null, non-array object is dropped (plainness is not asked—a `Date` or a `Timestamp` written where a bucket belongs is kept, with every field unreadable), and so is one under a key that is not the canonical spelling of an integer (`canonicalDayStatsKey`, `src/data/eventLimits.ts`); every field inside a bucket that survives gets the same coercion the rows get; a map in which nothing had to be dropped or coerced passes through by identity, a field beyond the three the rankers read included; and a map left with nothing reads as absent, so the row ranks by its roots as a legacy row does. That is the Functions read boundary's own entry rule, stated on both sides and pinned together by `tests/functions/finale-parity.test.ts` ([#1168](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1168)): the key used to be preserved verbatim here and canonicalised through `Number(key)` there, and an array bucket kept here and dropped there, so a row could carry a Day on one side that the other never saw.
- **And the ROOT `firstBingoAt` is normalised in the same pass** ([#1142](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1142) item 9). Sanitising only the buckets left the one path that does not read them exposed: `effectiveCruiseFirstBingoAt` falls back to the root stamp for a row with no `dayStats` at all, and the headline selection orders candidates with `<`. Every comparison against `NaN` is false, so a `NaN` row encountered FIRST was kept as the best and never displaced by a real one—and the record then named that Player in the hall of fame's headline at `at: 0`, permanently, over a Player who genuinely bingoed first. A root stamp that cannot be read now simply yields no eligible instant, exactly as a row that never bingoed does.
- **And so are the ROOT COUNTS, before anything is sorted** ([#1151](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1151), Codex P1, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). The stamp was normalised because the headline SELECTION reads it; `bingoCount` and `squaresMarked` are what the ORDER reads, and the order is the other thing this builder decides. `comparePlayers` SUBTRACTS them, so a missing or `NaN` count makes every comparison against that row `NaN`—and a `NaN` comparator result leaves `Array.prototype.sort` free to keep the malformed row exactly where the caller's roster put it, which can be ahead of a legitimate champion. `Infinity` is worse than free: it compares as the largest count there is and takes rank 1 outright. Either way `toStandingRow` then serialised the same row at `0`, so the frozen record ranked a Player first and printed no bingos beside them, permanently, on the one write that can never be amended. Normalising in the readable pass rather than at serialisation is what makes the ORDER and the ROW read the same numbers: `Rankable`'s three fields, coerced once, before `sortPlayers` sees any of them. Nothing beyond them—`blackout` and `displayName` are coerced where they are serialised, because no selector or comparator reads them—and no count is recomputed: whatever the Player's own row said is still what the record says (ADR 0001).
- **And every number the record carries is CLAMPED to the range `firestore.rules` accepts** ([#1151](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1151), Codex P1, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). The coercions above kept any FINITE value, while the flip arm's `finiteArchiveNumber` accepts only the ones inside ±4,102,444,800,000—the magnitude the estate already uses as its stand-in for an `isFinite()` Rules does not have. So a Player self-writing `bingoCount: 5e12` on their own unvalidated row, and holding the headline honour, produced a record the RULES refused: that refusal lands on the flip, which runs after the closing write has already shut the Event, and a rejected write rejects rather than returning—so it goes past `archiveEvent`'s typed refusals and past the console's automatic reopen alike, leaving a live Event closed with nothing frozen and every retry failing identically until an admin found and repaired, banned or deleted that one row. `MAX_ARCHIVE_NUMBER` is the writer's half of one shared representable-number contract, applied in the same readable pass to the root and per-Day counts, every instant, the headline `at`, and the resolved `freezeAt`—which is the one number no Player has to touch, because it resolves from the Admin-SDK-written `frozenAt` that no rules arm constrains. Clamping rather than refusing, for the reason every coercion here clamps: no real count or instant is within nine orders of magnitude of the bound, so it decides nothing about who won (ADR 0001) and only keeps the row writable. `rank` is deliberately outside it—the one number the builder COMPUTES rather than copies, a positive integer by construction, which is exactly what the rules hold it to. **And the LIVE ranking applies the same bound** (Codex P2, PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165)): while it did not, two rows above the bound could order one way on the last live Leaderboard and another way in the frozen record, so the record stopped copying what Players last saw. `clampArchiveNumber` and `MAX_ARCHIVE_NUMBER` therefore live in `src/data/eventLimits.ts`—which imports nothing, so the pure `src/game/logic.ts` and this data module can share them without the cycle a `game`→`eventArchive` import would create—and `src/data/eventArchive.ts` re-exports `MAX_ARCHIVE_NUMBER` so the rules suite still imports the writer's own constant from the writer's own module. **And so does the SCHEDULER's podium** (Codex P2, same PR, round 6): the finale beats rank their own server-side roster through the decoupled `functions/src/finaleContent.ts` mirror, which asked only whether a value was finite—so the podium Moment posted at the freeze, written once and never amended, could carry a champion and a First to BINGO this record then contradicts. `readFinaleRoster` (`functions/src/unlockDay.ts`) now maps every row through `withReadableFinaleRanking`, the mirror of `withReadableDayStats`, with the bound constants, the clamp and the normaliser's output pinned against the client's by `tests/functions/finale-parity.test.ts` and the seam pinned by `tests/functions/d15-scheduler-unlock.test.ts`. The podium, the live board and the archive rank by one normalisation; see [`d15-finale`](d15-finale.md) § Contract.
- **And the finished record is asked the BOUNDARY's own question before the quiesce** (same finding). `writableArchiveRecord` mirrors `completeArchiveRecord` and its two helpers clause for clause, so a record the rules would refuse is refused here—as `record-unwritable`, beside `too-large`, on the near side of the close—rather than arriving as a rejected write on an Event already shut. It is a BACKSTOP rather than an expected answer: the clamps above are what make it unreachable for any value a Player can write, and it exists so a cause nobody anticipated is caught on the same side of the quiesce as the ones that were. It mirrors the boundary clause for clause with ONE deliberate exception, `dailyHonors`' entries ([#1151](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1151), Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)): Rules cannot iterate a list, so the boundary looks inside neither list—but a pinned honour's `uid` is the one value in the record that no arm ever type-checked, so a record the rules would happily take can still carry an `ArchivedDayHonor` that violates its own declared shape. Being stricter there refuses no record the writer can legitimately produce, because the discard above is what makes every clause it asserts true by construction; it exists so a later regression in the discard is refused on the near side of the quiesce instead of frozen. `standings`' rows stay unchecked for the original reason unchanged: every one is built from a row `usableUid` has already accepted, so no unvalidated value is left in them to ask about.
- **The record is measured TWICE.** Against `MAX_ARCHIVE_BYTES` (256 KiB, a quarter of the document's budget) for its own share, and against `MAX_ARCHIVED_EVENT_BYTES` (900 KiB) for the PROJECTED document—the stored fields the update retains plus the ones it writes. The second is the one the write actually meets, because the record never lands on an empty document: an Event already carrying large `days` / `bannedUids` / `mostLovedPhoto` fields could pass the first and overflow on the second, inside the transaction, with gameplay already shut (Codex P2, PR #1139). Either is reported as a REFUSAL rather than a throw, so the caller can decline before taking the quiesce.

  **And the refusal names the levers that actually move the size, and WHICH ceiling it met** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). The copy used to blame "a Player row carrying far more text than a name" and recommend banning that Player, and neither half survives what the builder copies. `toStandingRow` takes six SELECTED fields—no `dayStats`, no photo, no arbitrary Player text—with every name clipped at `MAX_ARCHIVED_DISPLAY_NAME` and every `uid` pinned to a document id `usableUid` has already bounded, so unrelated text on a Player document cannot contribute a byte, and a full-sized record is tens of kilobytes. The recommended remedy was worse than useless: banning drops one bounded row and ADDS a uid to `bannedUids`, which is stored on the very Event the record has to fit beside, so it can make the projected document larger. What is actually unbounded in the sum is the Event's own retained data—`days` with each Day's frozen Prompt list, `bannedUids`, `mostLovedPhoto`—and that is what the copy now names. **The one exception it used to carry is closed** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162), round 7): the honours count on an Event with NO schedule really was unbounded, because the derived fallback yielded one honour per Day index any Player's `dayStats` mentioned and that map is Player-written with no rules validation—so one row filled to Firestore's own document limit pushed the record past its OWN share, and banning that Player was the remedy the copy denied. The supported-range filter above removes it: an honour is derived only for a Day the contract has, so a Player contributes at most one bounded standings row, the bounded headline pair and at most `MAX_DAYS` bounded honours. It is MEASURED rather than argued—the largest record `draftEventArchive` can build (200 rows at the uid and name bounds, every honour, a clipped Event name, and a hostile out-of-range Day per row) is ~74 KiB against the 256 KiB share, and the finding's own fixture is ~4 KiB—so "banning does not help" is now true without qualification and the record's own ceiling is unreachable with the bounds as shipped. `tooLargeCeiling` keeps both sentences anyway, because `MAX_ARCHIVED_STANDING_ROWS`, `MAX_ARCHIVED_UID` and `MAX_ARCHIVE_BYTES` are independent constants and collapsing them would hand an Admin the Event-data advice on the day one of them moves; the size test is the tripwire that fails first. The console appends WHICH of the two ceilings the draft met, because they send an Admin after different things and only the pre-quiesce check can tell them apart—the flip's own `too-large` is decided server-side over a roster the console never saw, so the shared sentence stays cause-neutral and the ceiling is stated only where the draft is in hand. The closing-state suffix drops its ban instruction with the rest and says what is true instead: play is already closed and nothing has been frozen.

The same `too-large` check runs again inside the transaction, on the record actually built from the server re-read, because that is a different roster from the one the console previewed and can be a different size.

**The builder is PURE.** No Firestore, no clock, no globals—`archivedAt` is supplied—so the console's preview and the writer's commit are the same function over the same inputs and cannot drift, and the whole of § "The frozen record" is unit-testable without an emulator or a mounted component (the `src/data/finale.ts` precedent).

### The pending-claim drain gate

Resolving a Claim writes the claimant's Board and their Player row, and the freeze denies both. A Claim still pending at the moment of the flip is therefore pending FOREVER, behind a Confirm/Reject pair in the Review queue that can now only fail—and the console cannot even reopen a state the flip has made permanent. So the archive refuses to proceed over one.

`claimsAwaitingAdmin` (`src/data/moderation.ts`) is the ONE predicate, applied at both ends of the same gate: the console applies it to the claim queue `Admin.tsx` is already subscribed to, so the control is disabled before the first tap and the reason names the Review queue; and `archiveEvent` applies it to a SERVER read taken AFTER the closing write, because a subscription reports only what has already been DELIVERED and a Claim committing between the last render and the quiesce would sail straight through a check made in the console alone. One predicate, so the two halves can never disagree about what counts. The server take reads a NORMALIZED Claim Mode (`migrateClaimMode`), because the console gates on the CONVERTED document while the freeze reads the stored one, and an Event written before the `verified` → `admin_confirmed` rename would otherwise have the console refusing to arm while the writer passed vacuously (Codex P2, PR #1139).

Scoped to `claimsQueueOpen` deliberately: outside `admin_confirmed` mode nothing creates a Claim and the Review queue offers no Confirm/Reject at all (#269's mode gate), so a stale pending Claim there has no drain path and blocking on it would be a dead end rather than a gate.

**And once play is closed, nothing may put a Claim BACK into the queue** ([#1151](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1151), Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). The server re-read closes the window between the console's last render and the quiesce; it cannot close the one after itself. The flip's transaction reads ONLY the Event document, so an admin client moving a terminal Claim back to `pending` between the drain read and the commit neither conflicts with that transaction nor retries it—and the archive becomes permanent over exactly the state the gate exists to refuse, with a Confirm/Reject pair that can now only fail. Rules cannot query a collection, so "the queue is empty" can never be a condition of the flip; what CAN be held at the boundary is the TRANSITION that creates the state, and holding it is enough, because a Claim that was terminal when the drain read saw it then cannot become pending afterwards. The Claims arm therefore denies any update whose RESULTING status is `pending` while the Event the request LEAVES BEHIND is closed to play, unless the stored status was already `pending`. Scoped that way for the drain's own sake: a pending Claim must still be confirmable and rejectable from the closing state, or the gate would bar the very remedy it points the Admin at, and restating a stored `pending` is not a transition into it. A CREATE of a pending Claim while closed needs no clause here—it is already denied by that arm's `eventOpenForPlay`, which is the gameplay denial every Claim create rides. The clause reuses the Event document `isAdmin` has already read rather than calling `eventClosedToPlay`, whose `exists()` guard would touch the same path again.

**And the Event it asks about is the one the request LEAVES BEHIND, not the one it found** (CodeRabbit, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). `get()` reads the PRE-write document, and rules decide every document in a batch independently—so one atomic admin batch that set `archiving: true` and moved a terminal Claim to `pending` was judged against an Event that was still open. Both writes committed, and what they committed together is exactly the state the clause exists to refuse: an Event closed to play with a Claim back in a queue whose Confirm/Reject pair can now only fail, which the flip is then free to make permanent. `getAfter` closes it by asking the RESULTING Event, which no single write and no batch can slip past—the same projection `reshuffleCounterPaired` already uses to require a paired counter write. It answers the opposite direction honestly too: a batch that REOPENS play and requeues in one write is allowed, because the Event it leaves behind is open and an open Event's queue may legitimately refill; nothing downstream leans on that being barred, since a reopen breaks the quiesce and any flip still bound to it is refused by the flip arm's own stored-`archiving` and generation checks. The projection REPLACES the pre-write read rather than joining it, so no `exists()` is spent and a batch that deletes the Event denies as an evaluation error; it stays confined to the requeue branch, so an ordinary resolution and an unchanged restatement still short-circuit before the Event is consulted at all; and it names the same document `isAdmin` has already read, so the arm's document-access boundary is unchanged, measured against the emulator on the branch that actually reaches the read (a terminal Claim moved to `pending` on a LIVE Event): 20 distinct Events pass in one batch and 21 deny, with the pre-write read and with the projection alike.

**Refused, never fixed.** Resolving a Claim from inside the freeze would be exactly the gameplay write the quiesce just denied. When the server re-read refuses, the console reopens play—it shut the Event, so it puts it back—and only when it CREATED that quiesce and still holds the generation in force (`BeginArchiveOutcome.created` plus `expectedToken`; [#1142](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1142) item 6). A quiesce it merely JOINED belongs to whoever opened it and is left exactly as found, with the Admin told that play is still shut and why.

**And the same gate carries the pre-finale check** ([#1151](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1151), routed from [#1150](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1150)'s review). The quiesce only DELAYS the finale beats—the freeze stamp, the podium Moment and the Most-Loved award are withheld while play is shut and land at the scheduled cutoff once it reopens—but `status: 'archived'` is irreversible, so an Event flipped BEFORE its Standings Freeze never receives them at all and the record it froze is one the podium never got to settle. Unlike the drain gate this is a WARNING rather than a bar, because an Admin may legitimately end an Event that will never reach its finale: `finaleHasRun` is satisfied by a stamped `finaleCompletedAt` or by an Event with no resolved Standings Freeze at all (the pre-ADR-0011 "legacy Events never freeze" shape, which gating on would strand forever), and anything else requires an explicit acknowledgement. It is offered on BOTH surfaces that reach the flip—the confirm row and the closing state's **Freeze the record** (Codex P2, PR #1162)—because `ready` gates each of them and an Admin who has already used **Close play** cannot get back to the other one: rendering it in the confirm row alone left the closing button disabled forever, with reopening gameplay on a deliberately shut Event as the only way to tick a box. `archiveEvent` refuses with `finale-pending` without it, so a direct caller—and a console whose read predates a finale that then failed to run—meets the same bar.

**And the evidence is `finaleCompletedAt`, not `frozenAt`** (Codex P1, PR #1162). The freeze stamp records ONE beat: `runFinaleBeats` writes it inside the freeze transaction and posts the podium Moment afterwards, as a separate best-effort write under its own try/catch whose retry guard is deliberately decoupled from the freeze (`finaleActions.postPodium`, Codex #228). So an Event carries a stamp and no podium for as long as that beat keeps failing—and reading the stamp answered "the finale has run" for the whole of that window. An archive taken there closed the Event over a podium that, because a closed Event's finale is never retried (§ "The server-side half"), would then never arrive: the beat is lost for good, on the one record that can never be amended. `finaleCompletedAt` is the COMPOSITE marker `runFinaleBeats` stamps last, only once it can observe every required beat, so a stamp without it keeps the acknowledgement on screen. The Most-Loved award is deliberately not a third condition: it has no retry path of its own—`freezeStandingsAndPersistMostLovedAward` writes it in the SAME transaction as `frozenAt`, and the compat branch beside it only runs when an award is already persisted—so requiring it would hold the marker back for an Event whose award came out empty rather than for anything still owed. The marker is also the only one of the two no client can write: `firestore.rules` refuses any change to it on the general Event arm and no dedicated arm names it, where `frozenAt` beside it stays admin-writable, so the old evidence could always have been satisfied by hand. An Event whose finale ran before this shipped carries no marker and acquires one on the next quarter-hourly sweep, so the acknowledgement is asked for at most once.

### The surfaces

- `src/components/admin/ArchiveEvent.tsx`—the end-of-Event control, rendered last inside **Game settings** (`specs/admin-console-ia.md` § "Game settings"), because it is the one control there that ends the Event rather than tuning it. THREE actions, and only two of them are reversible: **Close play** takes the quiesce and stops there, **Reopen play** lifts it, unconditionally, because it is an Admin acting on the Event standing in front of them, and **Archive** runs both writes in order behind a second, explicit confirmation. The archived state is RENDERED with the record it froze—the flip can also reach the document without this surface (an Admin-SDK edit), and an archived Event whose console said nothing would be the same silence this control exists to break. The action's status message is a sentence about the state the action left the Event in, so it is scoped to that state: it stays through the transition this Admin caused and clears the moment another Admin moves the Event somewhere the sentence no longer describes (Codex P2, PR #1157). The record is made against the state observed when the action RESOLVES, not the render the click happened in, so a change that lands during the awaited write is not missed (Codex P2, PR #1157 round 7). A phase that moved at all while the action was in flight disqualifies the starting phase as evidence, so a completed round trip back to it shows nothing rather than a stale sentence (Phase 4b P2, PR #1157 run 3). Both of those facts are held PER ACTION rather than shared (#1152, from Codex round 10 and the post-4b barrier round on PR #1157), because the controls are swapped by the LISTENER and not by the action: while a Close is pending the closing snapshot puts **Reopen play** on screen, and an Admin who presses it starts a second action over the first. A sequence number captured at click time is what settles that—an outcome from anything but the newest invocation describes an intent the Admin has already replaced, so it is discarded outright rather than reconciled against a phase, and the reopen a refusal declines is dropped on the same terms. **That check gates the automatic reopen ITSELF, not only what is reported about it** (Codex P2, PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165)). The reopen is a WRITE, and it was issued unconditionally while the liveness check guarded only the result state around it: the closing snapshot an open-phase **Archive** produces puts **Freeze the record** on screen, so an Admin can start a newer freeze against the same generation while the first action is still reading the snapshot—and a late `config-changed` from that first action then cleared exactly the quiesce the newer freeze was committing against, failing it with `not-closing` and overriding the intent the Admin most recently expressed. Neither the generation binding nor the `created` guard can see this case: the token still matches, and this handler really did create it. So a superseded invocation performs no cleanup at all—its refusal is dropped exactly as its outcome is. **And every lifecycle control is SHUT for as long as that cleanup is in flight** (Codex P2, PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165) round 5). The liveness check closes the interval BEFORE the reopen is issued; it cannot touch the one after. It passes, `abandonArchive` goes out, and the closing surface this action's own quiesce put on screen is still offering **Freeze the record**—so a newer freeze can be started against the generation this handler is a round trip away from lifting, and a sequence number cannot recall a write already issued: if the reopen commits first it clears that generation and the newer flip comes back `not-closing`. An issued write cannot be recalled, so the surface stops OFFERING the race instead. **Close play**, **Reopen play**, **Freeze the record** and the armed **Archive now**—all four are reachable inside that window, because another Admin lifting the quiesce swaps the open surface back in with the confirm row still armed—are disabled for the length of the cleanup, with the reason stated beside them, and the flag is cleared in a `finally` rather than behind the liveness check so a REJECTED reopen gives them back too. Disabling rather than making a newer action AWAIT the pending cleanup is what keeps the reports truthful: a newer action bumps the sequence number the instant it is pressed, which discards the refused action's own message, so the Admin would be left with a reopened Event and no explanation of why nothing was frozen—and the newer outcome would then be judged against a phase the deliberate wait itself moved. Disabled, the refusal lands as it stands and the next click starts from a settled Event with its own honest starting phase. The "it moved" half is a monotonic count of observed phase changes, snapshotted at click time, rather than a boolean two overlapping actions clear for each other: the flag belonged to whichever action wrote it last and was cleared by whichever finished first, so the LATEST action's own round trip went unrecorded. Both counters live in refs written TOGETHER, so they cannot disagree about one commit, and they are written in the COMMIT PHASE rather than during render (CodeRabbit, PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165)). React may discard render work—an interrupted concurrent pass is the reachable case—and a ref written during one keeps a conclusion that never committed: a discarded pass for a phase the Event never reached leaves the observed phase naming it and counts a move that never happened, so a pending action fails BOTH tests above and loses a status that was true. It is the safe direction—a message suppressed rather than a stale one shown—but it is a message lost for no reason, and it is the same rule the [#452](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/452) finding put the routing latch in state for. A LAYOUT effect rather than a passive one, because the refs have to be current the instant a commit lands: `report` runs after an awaited write and the sequence number is snapshotted at click time, and a passive effect is a separate task either could beat. A layout effect runs synchronously inside the commit, so a committed phase is never one behind and an abandoned pass leaves both refs untouched. Everything the invocation does to the surface is held to the same liveness check the outcome is: the automatic reopen (above), and DISARMING the confirmation panel, which cleared unconditionally and closed a row a later action had left on screen. **And the state a refusal describes is where the Event ENDS UP, not where the flip left it** (Codex P2, PR #1162): the flip's four refusals each leave a closing Event, but an open-phase **Archive** that CREATED the quiesce reopens play for every one of them—so the Event lands back open, and a message classified as describing a closing Event was discarded twice over, by the reopen this handler itself performed and by the moved-phase rule that reopen tripped. The Admin was left looking at reopened controls with no explanation of why nothing was frozen. A refusal followed by a SUCCESSFUL automatic reopen is therefore recorded against the OPEN phase; `closing` is retained only where no reopen was attempted or none succeeded—a quiesce merely JOINED, one another archive has taken over, and the closing surface's own **Freeze the record**, which never reopens. The staleness rule still applies from there: another Admin archiving afterwards clears it. **The four read refusals join that set on every one of those counts** (CodeRabbit Major, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)): the automatic reopen is stated once as `REOPEN_AFTER` rather than as a condition restated per outcome, each `read-failed:<stage>` maps to a closing Event exactly as the other four do, and each carries one sentence naming the read in the Admin's own vocabulary—**the Event**, **the Review queue**, **the final standings**, **the daily honours**—because the remedy is identical and only the subject of the sentence carries information.
- **The Archive action ships WITH the gate that refuses to open it** (Phase 4b P1, PR #1157). It was deliberately withheld from #1149 and returns here, beside the two things that make the one-way door safe to press: the pending-claim DRAIN GATE and the durable record. The preconditions are read BEFORE the first write, because the first write is what shuts the Event:

    1. **Every preview input CURRENTLY server-committed, the Event document included.** Until the server has spoken an empty roster and an unpinned Day are indistinguishable from a roster the [ADR 0006](../docs/adr/0006-offline-resilience.md) persistent cache has not filled in yet—so the confirm row would understate what is about to be frozen. The Event document is the third input, and it is gated on the same evidence rather than on its presence (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)): a non-null `event` is what the persistent cache delivers, not what the server said, and the roster and Day-meta latches resolve INDEPENDENTLY of it—so both could hold while the Event was still the cached copy, and the schedule, name and ban list the preview is built from would be whatever that cache happened to carry. The console therefore threads `useEventDoc`'s own fully-server-committed test down to the control (`hasServerData && !fromCache && !hasPendingWrites`, the same three flags `src/App.tsx` requires before it moves a Player off their Card, and for the same reasons: the latch is for life, and an Admin's own optimistic `archiving: true` is emitted server-backed but undecided). It gates both surfaces that reach the flip, because an Admin who has already used **Close play** cannot get back to the confirm row. `dayCount` being derived from the Event is a second reason rather than the only one: while the document is absent, the Day fan is vacuously satisfied.

        **And all three are held to the SAME three flags, per render** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). `useLeaderboard`'s `hasServerData` and `useDayMetasStatus`' `serverLoaded` are LATCHES on "the server has spoken", which is a different claim from "this is what the server says", and they never clear. So a console that confirmed every input and then went offline kept both true while the persistent cache re-served the roster and every honour, and Archive armed over precisely the cached preview the latches exist to refuse; a PENDING local write is the same hole from the other side, emitted server-backed but undecided and rolled back if it is refused. The roster is therefore gated on `hasServerData && !fromCache && !hasPendingWrites`, and the Day fan on a new `serverConfirmed` that asks the same of every Day's LATEST snapshot rather than latching the first server answer—the test `eventConfirmed` already carried for the Event document, now applied to all three. Each can fall false again, which is the point: the confirmation has to describe the preview on screen at the tap, not a moment that has passed. The latches stay on the hooks for the surfaces that legitimately ask the "seen once" question—`Board`'s ceremonial First-to-BINGO edge and `ConfirmWinMoments` read the roster's, and neither changes, because a claim about a moment that has already happened is not a record being frozen.

        The RECORD does not depend on these subscriptions at all (`archiveEvent` re-reads its inputs after the quiesce), but a console that has not loaded is still not a console to end an Event from, and the record the freeze takes is the one the Admin was shown.

        **A FAILED honour subscription confirms nothing, and says so** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). `useDayMetasStatus` fans one listener per Day, and an `onSnapshot` error is TERMINAL for the listener it kills. Such a Day used to satisfy `serverLoaded` as well as `loaded`, which made the strict latch a lie in exactly the case it exists for: the listener died before any server snapshot, so that Day's honour was never confirmed, and yet the archive gate read it as confirmed and armed. The preview beside it had fallen back to the roster-DERIVED honour, or to none, while `archiveEvent`'s own `getDocFromServer` re-read can recover the PINNED holder and freeze THEM—a different record from the one the Admin approved, on the one write that can never be amended. So an errored Day resolves `loaded` (the live honours strip must not hang on a dead listener, and it renders the same derived fallback it always did) and stays out of `serverLoaded`, and the hook reports it on a third flag, `failed`. The console needs that third flag rather than the absence of the second: a latch that can never complete would otherwise sit behind a "loading" message that never resolves, which is the deadlock the old behaviour avoided by the wrong means. The blocked reason therefore states what actually happened—**the daily honours could not be read from the server, so the archive cannot tell a Day that had no First to BINGO from one whose honour never arrived**—with the remedy (reload the console) that a terminal listener error actually needs, and it is stated ahead of the loading sentence on both surfaces.

        **But only while the confirmation is actually MISSING** (CodeRabbit, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). `failed` is not the complement of either confirmation, and the error callback removes nothing: a Day the server answered before its listener died stays latched and stays CURRENT, so `failed` can be true beside a fully confirmed fan. Read on its own it therefore printed that terminal "reload the console and try again" sentence beside an ENABLED Archive control, sending the Admin after a problem that was not standing in their way. The blocked reason is a statement about why the door is SHUT, so it is `failed && !serverConfirmed`—which is also what keeps it agreeing with the gate beside it, since the gate reads the confirmation alone.
    2. **The claim queue drained**, from the queue `Admin.tsx` already subscribes to rather than a second listener (§ "The pending-claim drain gate").
    3. **The record fits the projected DOCUMENT** (§ "The inputs are validated BEFORE the Event is shut"), stated with the levers that actually move the size, which of the two ceilings it met, and a remedy an Admin can actually take.
    4. **The finale has run, or the Admin acknowledges that it will not.** A checkbox on the confirm row rather than a bar, and `beforeFinale` carries the answer to the writer.

  TWO TAPS, never one, because the write is permanent behind write-once rules and there is no second attempt to correct it: the control arms a confirm row first (the `AdultContentConfirm` posture, #610, inline rather than modal because there is nothing to explain the row cannot say itself), which states the roster size, the honour count and any unreadable rows, and the second tap runs both writes. The preconditions are re-checked at that second tap, not only the first: a Claim can arrive, or a subscription re-key, while the row is armed. Every refusal reports through the same status line the two reversible actions use, and the `AsyncButton` failure pill covers the rejection case.
- `src/game/logic.ts` and `src/hooks/useData.ts`—**a roster made readable before it is ranked** (#1145, #1142 item 10). `players/{uid}` validates none of its fields, and `comparePlayers` SUBTRACTS two of them, so a Player leaving a `bingoCount` such as `{ toString: null }` threw a TypeError out of `sortPlayers`. That is not a bad row rendering badly: `sortPlayers` is what `useLeaderboard` returns and what `draftEventArchive` ranks with, so one row took down every roster consumer—**Game settings** among them, and with it the **Reopen play** control that is the one way back from a closing Event.

  ONE guard, at each ranking ENTRY POINT rather than inside the comparator (#1151, Codex P1 on PR #1162)—and ONE FUNCTION at both of them. `useLeaderboard` maps each row through `withReadableDayStats` on its way into `sortPlayers`, exactly as the archive builder does on the roster it re-reads, and that function calls `withReadableRanking` for the three root fields and normalises the per-Day buckets beside them (#1152, Codex P2 on PR #1165 round 4—the bullet on `src/hooks/useData.ts` below has why the live path could not stop at the root). Normalising before the sort is what makes the ORDER and the row that is printed or frozen read the same numbers off the same row—a coercion inside `comparePlayers` as well would let those two disagree again, and would break the comparator's byte-identity with its `functions/src/finaleContent.ts` mirror. It is also the only guard that helps the RENDER: a sort that no longer crashes still hands the Leaderboard a row it prints straight into the DOM, where React throws on an object child. A well-formed row comes back by IDENTITY, so a healthy roster pays one array and nothing else.

  The coercions are the ones every surface already agrees on—an unreadable count reads `0`, an unreadable instant reads `null`—and they decide nothing about who won (ADR 0001). The RECORD is unchanged either way: `toStandingRow` applies the identical coercion to each row it copies, so the archive is byte-for-byte what it was. The two normalisers stay separate rather than shared because a pure game module cannot import the data layer, and because `withReadableDayStats` has a second job the live path does not need—making a row's per-Day BUCKETS readable to the honour selectors—on a roster that never passes through this hook.

- `src/components/ArchivedLeaderboard.tsx`—the read-only archive surface (#1152): the frozen standings, the hall of fame (Event-wide First to BINGO plus the daily honours strip), and the Share Card. It subscribes to NOTHING. There is no `useLeaderboard`, no `sortPlayers`, no `eventFirstBingoWinner` and no day-meta read in it; every number, name and honour was decided once, at the archive—down to the LABEL on each honour chip, which is why `days` never enters this component's Event prop. That is what makes "opened later, the standings persist unchanged" true rather than merely likely. A record whose `dayLabel` is absent falls back to the honour's own ordinal rather than reaching into the live schedule, because `eventConverter` validates no field of a stored archive and a hand-written record must stay frozen-safe. **And the empty state is chosen by `playerCount`, not by what is visible** (Codex P2, PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165) round 4). The visible rows are the stored ones minus whoever is banned right now, so moderation alone can empty them—a one-Player Event whose Player is banned after the freeze, or a truncated record whose whole retained prefix is hidden. Saying nobody was ever on the board there is the record contradicting itself: the frozen count is positive and the footnote below prints it, and a ban decides who is SHOWN rather than re-ruling who took part. A positive count with no visible rows therefore states that count and says moderation hides every row THIS RECORD CARRIES—the retained prefix on a truncated archive, not the whole roster it counts—while a genuinely zero count keeps the "no players were on the board" sentence. Moderation is the only thing that can produce the first case, because `standings` and `playerCount` are one contract at the rules boundary (`standingsSizeMatches`): a record carrying a positive count and no rows cannot be written.

  **Moderation is the one live input, and it is not a re-derivation.** The surface takes the CURRENT `bannedUids` off the Event document its parent already subscribes to, and hides a banned Player's stored row, their daily honour chip, and their appearance on the Share Card—vacating the headline First to BINGO to "No one got there." rather than naming the next-earliest Player. The repository's ban contract is that every public Leaderboard view hides a banned Player (`specs/w2-ban-console.md` § Leaderboard), the freeze deliberately leaves `bannedUids` editable so a permanent record still has a takedown path, and a record whose only Leaderboard view could not be moderated would be the [#808](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/808) support incident in a new place. Nothing is promoted into the vacancy and nothing is recalculated: the stored `standings`, `playerCount`, every remaining row's numbers and the honours all keep the values the archive stamped, and an unban restores the hidden row exactly as it was. The displayed rank ordinals close the gap over the visible rows, which is what the LIVE Leaderboard already does with a banned row—leaving a hole at #1 would advertise that a row was removed, which is the opposite of what hiding is for. The truncation footnote keys on the STORED pair, so moderating one Player never makes an un-truncated archive claim it was cut short.

  **The footnote reports the STORED prefix, and says what moderation is doing to it** (Codex P3 + P2, PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165) round 7). "The top N of M" is a claim about the frozen RANK CUTOFF, so N is `archive.standings.length`, which a ban never moves—reading it off the visible rows made a 200-of-300 record with rank 1 hidden say "the top 199 of 300" while the displayed set still included the originally ranked #200, which is not the top 199 of anything. The rows moderation removed are counted separately beside it ("…, N hidden by moderation") rather than silently deducted, and a truncated record with nothing hidden keeps the plain sentence. The permanence claim beside it is scoped the same way: what is frozen is the stored RECORD, and what moderation can still change is which of it is shown—an unconditional "nothing here changes again" is contradicted by this very page, which narrows the standings, the honours, the headline and the Share Card from the live `bannedUids` on every snapshot.

  **The Event's own name comes out of the record too.** The card's title and context line read `EventArchive.eventName`, not the live `EventDoc.name`—the freeze leaves that field editable, so a card rebuilt from it drifted the moment an Admin renamed the Event. The app's own name stays the fallback for a record that carries none, which is a hand-written one; the fallback is frozen-safe rather than reaching back into the live document.

  **The Share Card's pinned eleventh row falls back to `firstBingoRow`.** It prints the ten visible rows plus the headline holder when they rank outside them, and it searched `archive.standings` for that row—which on a truncated record does not contain the holder at all, so the row this card names in its own headline silently disappeared. The stored row is used instead, at the rank it held in the complete standings; a ban still vacates the headline first, so a hidden Player is never printed from it.

  **The card is pre-rendered on mount, and the tap never waits on it**—the `FarewellPodium` treatment (#712 round 3), which the archive earns more plainly than anything else in the app because its payload is IMMUTABLE at the rules boundary: pre-rendering cannot bake in something a later snapshot would change, and unlike the live Leaderboard (which re-renders on every roster snapshot and refused mount-eager rasterization for exactly that reason) there is no per-snapshot churn to pay for. `navigator.share` needs transient user activation, and so does `clipboard.writeText` on Safari and Firefox, so a tap that AWAITS a rasterization can outlive the activation and skip every share leg on precisely the slow phone that needed it (Codex P2, PR #1139). The handler therefore takes the warmed render only if it has ALREADY settled and calls `shareCardBlob` in the same turn as the gesture; an unsettled render costs the image, never the share. Hover, focus and pointer-down warm it too, covering the one input that can invalidate the card—a ban or an unban. The archived view stays shareable through the existing on-device Share Cards (#36, [ADR 0005](../docs/adr/0005-client-side-share-images.md))—a frozen leaderboard is the most shareable thing an Event ever produces—and there is still no crawler-facing page.

- `src/components/Leaderboard.tsx`—splits into a routing component that owns only `useEventDoc`, and a `LiveLeaderboard` child that owns the live view's three subscriptions: the whole `players` roster (`useLeaderboard`), every Day's meta document (`useDayMetasStatus`) and up to 60 live Proofs (`useProofKindsByUid`). An archived Event renders `ArchivedLeaderboard` from the routing component and NEVER MOUNTS the child, so none of those listeners open. The split is what makes "it subscribes to NOTHING" true: a branch taken after the hooks had already run would render the frozen page with a live listener fan open behind it. It is also the only way to do this without breaking the [#280](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/280) hook-order rule—conditioning hooks in place is illegal in React, whereas returning a different component unmounts the live one and its listeners with it, and each component's own hook sequence stays fixed. The presentational filters stay with the live child, because a frozen record has one shape.

  **And the live branch WAITS FOR THE SERVER** (Codex P2, PR #1139). The split decides nothing on a cold visit: `useEventDoc` starts at `data: null`, and the ADR 0006 persistent cache can then replay the Event as it stood when the tab last saw it—`active`, because it was. Falling through to the live child on either mounts the whole fan on an archived Event and tears it down a snapshot later, so "it subscribes to NOTHING" was false for exactly as long as the roster, every Day's meta and 60 Proofs took to answer. An UNRESOLVED status therefore renders the live view's own loading state, under the same label, so there is no visible seam between this wait and the roster's.

  `useDocSub` gains `serverResolved` beside `hasServerData` for it, the same pair `useDayMetasStatus` already draws between `loaded` and `serverLoaded`: `hasServerData` is "the server has spoken", `serverResolved` is that OR the subscription ERRORED, which is terminal for an `onSnapshot` listener and can therefore never become a server answer. Three things end the wait short of a server snapshot, because a spinner nobody can get past is worse than the listeners it avoids: an errored subscription, a client the browser reports as OFFLINE (since no server answer can arrive for it and this app is offline-durable by design), and a cache-served snapshot the server has not contradicted inside a bounded wait. `useOnline`'s `false` is the trustworthy half of that hook, and it is read here to stop waiting, never to authorize anything. **But offline stops the wait at the CACHE RESULT, not before it** (Codex P2, PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165)): `useOnline` already reports false on the first render of an offline cold mount, while the Event subscription is still at `data: null` with its cache read in flight, so `!online` alone settled the status over no Event at all and mounted the live child—opening the whole fan on a cold offline visit to an ARCHIVED Event and tearing it down a snapshot later, which is the very defect the server wait closes, reached through the escape hatch instead. The offline arm therefore also requires `useDocSub`'s `loading` to have cleared, which is exactly "this subscription has produced its cache result, or failed"; until then the routing half renders the loading state and opens nothing. **And `navigator.onLine === false` is not the only non-error escape** (Codex P1, PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165)). Behind a captive or partially routed portal—a ship's Wi-Fi, which is the operating context this whole app is built for—Firestore cannot reach the server while the browser goes on reporting ONLINE: `onSnapshot` serves the cached Event with `fromCache: true` and RETRIES indefinitely rather than invoking the terminal error callback that resolves `serverResolved`, so neither escape above can fire and a cached ACTIVE Event's Leaderboard held its spinner indefinitely. The subscription exposes no reachability signal to read instead, so the third escape is a BOUNDED WAIT: once the subscription has answered from anywhere—`loading` cleared, the same half the offline arm reads—the gate gives a server-committed snapshot `CACHED_EVENT_SETTLE_MS` to arrive, and then settles on the cached Event exactly as the offline arm does one render earlier. A cached COMMITTED archive routes to `ArchivedLeaderboard` and opens nothing; a cached active Event falls through to `LiveLeaderboard`. The constant is four seconds, stated once in `src/components/Leaderboard.tsx` and exported so the tests drive the real number rather than a copy of it: it bounds how long a Player stares at a spinner before being shown the record their own device already has, and is not an estimate of a round trip. A server snapshot inside the window still WINS—it resolves the status outright and the timer is cancelled with it—so the answering case is untouched and the wait is what ends a portal's silence. The latch is monotone, so a reconnect cannot bounce an already-rendered live view back through the spinner—which would unmount the child, drop its listeners and reset the Player's filter. It is held in STATE, adjusted during render the way `Board`'s dangling-sheet close already is, rather than in a ref: React discards a state update from an abandoned render, while a ref written during one would keep a conclusion that never committed (the [#452](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/452) finding).

  **The ARCHIVED branch needs no such wait, and one guard instead.** A cached `archived` record can never be contradicted—the flip is write-once at the rules boundary, so an archived Event can never be un-archived—which is what keeps the archive readable offline; requiring `fromCache` to be false there would break exactly the read the gate exists to protect. What it does require is `hasPendingWrites` to be false, the Card redirect's own rule (Codex P2, PR #1157 round 9) applied to the surface that redirect points AT: an Admin's flip is emitted optimistically before the rules decide it, and a refusal rolls it back to open, so a record the server may never accept would otherwise tear down the live listeners and print itself as final. **That guard is about the TRANSITION, not about the state** (Codex P2, PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165)). `hasPendingWrites` is a flag on the WHOLE Event snapshot rather than on the field that moved, so an Admin's ban or unban after the freeze raises it over an archive the server settled long ago—and reading that as an unconfirmed archive dropped the page back to `LiveLeaderboard`, reopening every gameplay listener the archived surface exists not to open and printing re-derived live standings over the frozen record, for as long as the moderation write stayed in flight (offline, until the client reconnects). So the confirmed archive is LATCHED: the first snapshot that is `archived`, server-backed and free of local writes (`!fromCache && !hasPendingWrites`) is the flip COMMITTING, and the routing half stops asking after it. Monotone like the status latch beside it, safe for the reason this paragraph opens with—nothing can un-archive the Event underneath the latch—and every snapshot before that one still meets the pending-write guard in full. **And the latch is PERSISTED per archive generation, so it survives a remount** (Codex P2, PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165)). An in-session latch covers only moderation that begins after this mount has already observed a clean server snapshot; a tab reloaded—or the Leaderboard revisited—while an offline ban or unban is still queued starts from nothing, sees a cached `archived` snapshot carrying `hasPendingWrites: true`, and mounted `LiveLeaderboard` with all three gameplay listeners over an archive committed long before that write existed. `EventDoc.archivedUnder` is the generation the flip was bound to at the rules boundary, so it is the one value that names WHICH archive was confirmed: a server-committed archived snapshot records `{eventId, archivedUnder}` in `localStorage` (`gcb.archive.<eventId>.confirmedUnder`, small, keyed, try/catch on every access, read once at mount), and a later cached snapshot naming that same generation is treated as confirmed however many unrelated writes are in flight. An OPTIMISTIC flip carries a generation nothing has ever confirmed, so it is still declined—which is exactly the distinction a bare "this Event is archived" flag could not carry, and why the record is per generation rather than per Event. **And that record is written by the SHARED Event subscription, not by the Leaderboard** (Codex P2, PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165)). Persisting it from the Leaderboard's own effect left it unwritten in exactly the case it exists for: only a mounted Leaderboard ever recorded a commit, and the visit that NEEDS the record is the one where some other route saw it. An admin who receives the committed archive on the console, queues an offline ban there, and only then opens the standings hands that mount a first snapshot which is `archived` with `hasPendingWrites: true` and nothing persisted behind it—both latches false, and `LiveLeaderboard` mounted after the settle escape with every gameplay listener open until the moderation write synced. The observation is a fact about the DEVICE rather than about a component, so `useEventDoc` records it from the `onSnapshot` callback every route already holds, and the Leaderboard only reads it back. From the callback rather than from a render or an effect, so the record does not depend on the observing surface re-rendering, staying mounted, or ever reading the value itself; the write is idempotent (the same generation rewritten is the same string) and total. The conditions are the ones the in-session latch turns on plus the record itself: `status: 'archived'`, the `archive` present, `!fromCache && !hasPendingWrites`, and a numeric `archivedUnder`—the `archive` because the routing gate this record serves requires it too, so vouching for a hand-edited document without one would confirm a page nobody can reach. The store is trusted for ROUTING alone: no number, name or honour is read from it, and the record the page prints still comes off the Event document. An Event marked archived with NO record at all is not a state this app produces—`archiveEvent` writes status, stamp and record in one update—so the live view is left as the fallback for a hand-edited document. It is still read-only in the only place that counts: the rules deny its gameplay writes on the `status` field alone.

- `src/data/archiveConfirmation.ts`—the device-local record that a SERVER-COMMITTED archive was observed for an Event, and the single owner of its `localStorage` key (Codex P2, PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165)). `useEventDoc` calls `recordArchiveConfirmation` from the shared subscription's own `onSnapshot` callback, on every route; `Leaderboard` calls `confirmedArchiveGeneration` and nothing else. A module both sides import rather than a helper in either of them, because the writer and the reader are now different files and a key spelled twice is a confirmation that silently never matches. Deliberately not `src/data/eventArchive.ts`, whose contract is a Firestore-free, React-free PURE half—this one is a side effect on browser storage—but it reuses that module's `isEventArchived` rather than restating `status === 'archived'`.
- `src/hooks/useData.ts`—`useLeaderboard` normalises its roster through `withReadableDayStats`, the builder's own function, rather than through the root-only `withReadableRanking`: the First-to-BINGO pin ranks by a row's per-Day buckets whenever it has any, so a second entry point that saw only the root let the live pin and the frozen one disagree. `useDocSub` takes an optional per-snapshot observer, and `useEventDoc` is the one caller that passes one. It runs inside the snapshot callback, so what a route observed does not depend on that route re-rendering or staying mounted, and it must be a module-scope constant because the subscription effect is keyed on the subscription key alone. **And the Event id is HANDED to it, not read by it**: `useDocSub` captures `EVENT_ID` where the listener is OPENED and passes it to every observation. `EVENT_ID` is a live ESM binding, so an observer reading it in the snapshot callback read whatever it said when the snapshot LANDED — which, in the window between the active Event moving and this effect's cleanup retiring the listener, is already the NEW Event. Event A's archive generation was then persisted under Event B's key, which `specs/event-scoped-client-state.md` forbids outright, and a matching pending generation in B could be read back as previously server-confirmed. The pairing that keeps the capture honest is the subscription key: every Event-scoped key is built from `EVENT_ID` in the same render and the effect re-runs on every key change, so the captured id and the key always name the same Event.
- `src/index.css`—the `.lb-archived-banner` block. Quiet on purpose: an archive is a keepsake, not an error state, so it borrows the panel chrome the honours strip already uses.
- `src/App.tsx`—the Card tab of a closed Event routes to the standings rather than mounting `Board`. Decided in the route table rather than inside `Board`, because a branch taken after the hooks have run still mounts the component and opens its listeners, and `Board`'s mount is what deals a Day Card. It does NOT wait for the server: `useEventDoc` starts at `null`, which reads as OPEN, so a cold visit renders the Board exactly as it does today and re-routes when the snapshot lands. Making the Card tab of every LIVE Event wait on a round trip would be a far worse trade than one late redirect. The redirect waits for a SERVER-BACKED snapshot (`hasServerData`, the latch `useDocSub` sets once the server has answered, AND the current snapshot's own `fromCache` being false, because the latch is for life and a device that once saw the quiesce can navigate back on a cached closed value while offline, AND `hasPendingWrites` being false, because an Admin's own optimistic `archiving: true` is emitted server-backed but undecided and a refusal rolls it back to open; Codex P2, PR #1157 rounds 8 and 9): a cached `archiving: true` from before another Admin reopened play must not move a Player off their Card with a replace navigation the later open snapshot cannot undo, the same rule the join and the profile mirror follow (Codex P2, PR #1157 round 6).
- `src/data/api.ts`—`joinAndDeal` declines the whole join on a closed Event. The daily branch merges identity into `players/{uid}` on EVERY visit, returning Players included, and `eventOpenForPlay` denies that write. Attempting it anyway is not a harmless failure: `runDeal` classifies a permission-denied as PERMANENT, so `App` replaces the Card tab with the full-screen `DealError`, and its Retry repeats exactly the same forbidden write. `false` is the same "no new Board was dealt" the function already returns for a returning Player, so no `join_event` is recorded and no error is raised. Only a SERVER-BACKED snapshot declines, exactly as the profile mirror decides it (Codex P2, PR #1157): `getDoc` can still answer from the persistent cache during a transient outage while the browser reports online, and a cached `archiving: true` can describe a quiesce another Admin has since lifted. Skipping on that is the worst outcome available here, because the decline is reported as success—`runDeal` records no retryable error, never reruns, and a FIRST-TIME visitor sits without a Player row or a Board until the next connectivity transition or reload. So a cached closed state attempts the join and lets the rules answer.
- `src/data/profile.ts`—`updateExistingPlayer` skips the Event-local mirror on a closed Event. The global `users/{uid}` write has already committed by the time it runs and the two are not one transaction (they cannot be—different roots), so a mirror denial must never surface as "the save failed". Two guards, because the read cannot be atomic with the write: the status read skips the write we know is denied, and a `permission-denied` catch covers the Event that closes between the two. An UNREADABLE Event reads as open and the write is attempted, exactly as it was before the read existed—the status decides whether to skip a write, never whether the save may proceed. Only a SERVER-BACKED snapshot skips: offline, `getDoc` can answer from the persistent cache with a quiesce another Admin has since lifted, and skipping on it would drop the mirror for good, so a cached closed state attempts the write and leaves the freeze, if it still holds, to the `permission-denied` handler (Codex P2, PR #1157); a snapshot carrying a PENDING local close is treated the same way, because a refused close rolls back to open after the decision (Phase 4b P2, PR #1157 run 3).
- `src/data/proofs.ts`—`deleteProof` reads the Event INSIDE its transaction, skips the gameplay cleanup on an ARCHIVED one, REFUSES a Proof that still backs a marked cell while the Event is merely CLOSING, COMMITS before it revokes the media, and writes the media's pending-revocation TOMBSTONE in that same commit—naming the object the Proof document it read INSIDE the transaction stores, never the caller's `storagePath` argument; `attachProof` deletes the object it just uploaded when its own transaction is refused. See § "Moderation is not a gameplay write" below.
- `functions/src/proofStorageDeletes.ts`—`revokeProofMedia`, the whole decision the tombstone sweeper takes, behind injected seams: confine the row's object path to its own Event and Proof, read the row STANDING at that path and require it to be the same revocation the delivery was created for (`isSameRevocation`, over the structural `sameFirestoreValue` so an unchanged malformed row still matches itself and can be retired), CLAIM the row for exclusive processing with a server-only sweep lease (`hasActiveSweepLease`, `SWEEP_LEASE_TTL_MS`), delete the object bound to a generation ALWAYS, and retire the row ONLY once the object is provably gone and only while this delivery's own lease still stands—retirement being the sweeper's alone. `revokeDeletedProofMedia` (`functions/src/index.ts`) is the trigger that carries it, on the tombstone's CREATE, `retry: true`, under the Admin SDK identity. See § "Moderation is not a gameplay write" below.

### The enforcement—`firestore.rules` and `storage.rules`

`eventOpenForPlay(eventId)` is the single predicate, and it is ANDed into every gameplay write arm: Boards, Player rows, the write-once per-Day honour, reshuffle spends, Prompt creates and reports, Proof creates, reports and owner-deletes, Claims, Tally markers (create, update and delete), Doubts (raise, satisfy, withdraw), Hearts (create and delete), Moments, and retraction tombstones. It is the negation of `eventClosedToPlay(eventId)`, which reads the Event document ONCE and closes on either half of the freeze—`status == 'archived'` or `archiving == true`.

`storage.rules` mirrors it on proof-media upload AND on the OWNER's media delete, because a Proof document and its media must refuse together or the archive still grows blobs—the #806 lesson, restated by `specs/path-addressing-and-root.md` § D8 as "Storage uploads under that Event's prefix alike". An unconditional owner-delete arm was the other half of that same hole (Phase 4b P1, PR #1157): `firestore.rules` refuses the owner's Proof DOCUMENT delete on a closed Event, so a direct `deleteObject` stripped the media out from under a document that survives, leaving a Feed entry whose image can never load on the one Event where nothing can be re-posted. **That binding has since been replaced by a stronger one** (Codex round 4 P2, PR #1163): the owner may delete a proof object only when its Proof document is already GONE, in every Event state, and the freeze clause on their arm is retired because it can no longer be reached. The reason is the delete-and-recreate window § "The enforcement" describes below—immutability refuses an overwrite and welcomes a RECREATE, so an owner who can delete a live Proof's object can empty the path and refill it inside the generation-capture window. **And the condition then had to move from the owner's BRANCH to the whole ARM** (Codex round 5 P2, PR #1163), because the owner and admin rosters OVERLAP: an organiser who plays their own Event is the media's owner and an `admins` entry at once, so an admin-owner still passed the admin branch while their Proof document stood and the upload arm still welcomed their recreate of the emptied path as its owner—the same window, reached through the other branch. Generation capture has to be serialised against every identity that can empty and refill the path, so `proofDocMissing` now gates every client delete. Nothing the app does needs the arm that went, on either path: `deleteProof`—which is the owner's delete AND the Admin takedown, one function for both—commits the document's removal BEFORE it revokes the object, and `attachProof`'s rollback deletes an object no document was ever written for, so every client delete is an orphan delete. The ADMIN takedown is therefore untouched in practice and in every state, which is the property that matters (#808): it reaches this arm as an orphan delete like any other.

**A PROOF OBJECT IS IMMUTABLE, which is the third thing the upload arm now says** (Codex round 3 P2). The arm allowed an overwrite in place, and `deleteProof` reads the object's Storage `generation` before its transaction opens: an overwrite landing between that read and the commit binds the media-revocation tombstone to the PREVIOUS blob although the Proof pointed at the replacement when it was deleted, so a failed inline delete leaves the sweeper taking `412` and retiring the row as a post-delete replacement—with the deleted Proof's own media still reachable. Immutability closes that at the source rather than teaching the sweeper to tell an overwrite that PRECEDES its row from one that FOLLOWS it: with no overwrite possible, a `412` can only mean the name was re-occupied after a DELETE, which retirement is already correct for. **`resource == null` alone was not enough, because it welcomes a RECREATE** (Codex round 4 P2, PR #1163). An owner able to delete a live Proof's object could take it down and immediately upload a replacement to the now-empty path, reaching the same desynchronised tombstone by the other route: the row describes the OLD generation, the path holds the new blob, the inline delete fails, the sweeper takes `412` and retires the row as a post-delete replacement. The half that closes it lives on the DELETE arm rather than here—no client may remove media while its Proof document stands, an admin-owner included—so inside the window there is nothing to recreate. Asking the UPLOAD arm instead, whether a `proofStorageDeletes` row stands for the id, is not available: that is a third `firestore.exists` and `eventClosedToPlay` already spends the whole two-access budget (measured for round 4—and the Storage EMULATOR enforces no cap at all, admitting an arm that makes eighteen distinct accesses, so the measurement had to be reasoned rather than asserted). The app never wanted the overwrite—`attachProof` mints a fresh Firestore auto-id per capture, a refused transaction DELETES its object, and the Player's retry re-uploads under a NEW id—and `src/data/storage.ts` has asserted the invariant since #363, which is why proof media is served `immutable` while `avatars/{uid}.jpg`, overwritten in place on a profile edit, deliberately is not. That avatar arm is separate and keeps its full `write`.

The predicate is `resource == null`, NOT an `allow create` arm without `update`. The two are supposed to be equivalent—`create` is documented as the new-object method and `update` as the existing-object one—and they are not: the Storage emulator evaluates a second upload to an occupied path under `create` and admits it, so an arm that merely dropped `update` would deny nothing and no test could catch it. Asking the object's state directly is both correct and provable, and it holds whichever method a platform decides a wholesale replacement is; the method list stays `create, update` so neither can slip past. `resource` is the EXISTING object's metadata, so this reads the bucket rather than Firestore and the two-access budget below is untouched. It is also not a claim that nothing under the prefix is ever rewritten: the Vision handler's thumbnail (`functions/src/index.ts`) re-saves `{proofId}_thumb.jpg` on the Admin SDK, which bypasses `storage.rules` entirely. What the arm governs is the browser.

Both Storage arms bind the document read to a helper ARGUMENT rather than re-reading it, because Storage Rules allow at most two Firestore accesses per evaluation and make no promise of deduplicating a repeated `firestore.get()` on one path. On the delete arm that is what makes the clause possible at all: one `get()` answers the admin check (`proofMediaDeleteOk`, which takes the document's data as an argument), where a helper fetching its own copy beside the `exists()` the arm now always spends would have read three times and failed every legitimate delete closed. That helper answered the admin check and the freeze check together until the owner's arm left it (Codex round 4 P2, PR #1163), and it now answers only "is this caller an Admin of this Event"—kept, rather than dropped along with the freeze clause, because a signed-in STRANGER must still be refused an orphan that is not theirs. The threading is unchanged, and it is still what #806 has to reclaim. `admins` is read through `get('admins', [])` so an Event document missing the field denies cleanly instead of erroring the evaluation.

`events/{eventId}/proofStorageDeletes/{proofId}` is the one collection [#1153](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1153) ADDS to the rules file, and it is not a freeze arm at all—it is what makes the reordered takedown durable (§ "Moderation is not a gameplay write"). Its CREATE predicate is the Proof delete arm's own, restated verbatim—admin, or the media's owner on an open Event, the admin branch ungated by the freeze—plus the exact three-key shape, plus the object path pinned by string EQUALITY to `proofs/{eventId}/{uid}/{proofId}.{ext}`, plus a binding to the live Proof document (it must exist, name the same owner, and STORE the same object path), plus `existsAfter` requiring that the SAME request removes that Proof. Read, update and DELETE are all denied outright: create is the only client write this collection has, and retiring a discharged revocation is the sweeper's alone (§ "Moderation is not a gameplay write"). The `hasOnly` shape is also what keeps the SWEEP LEASE out of client hands—`leaseId` and `leaseAt` are the only fields any server writes to this collection, so a create carrying either is refused here and the standing row cannot acquire one afterwards, both because update is denied.

**The `existsAfter` clause is the owner-squat fix** ([#1147](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1147)). Binding the row to a LIVE Proof stops one Player minting a row at ANOTHER's Proof id, but not an owner minting one at their OWN and leaving the Proof standing: `deleteProof` writes with `set`, `set` against a standing row is an UPDATE, update is denied, and a denial inside that transaction fails the whole takedown—so one Player could make every media Proof in the Event permanently un-deletable, and the sweeper would meanwhile revoke media a surviving Feed entry still points at. Rules cannot see the other WRITES in a request, but they can see the projected STATE of a named document, and that is exactly the accompanying delete this needs to require. Admitting the row only as part of the request that removes its Proof closes both holes at once and makes a standing row mean one thing: a Proof that is already gone. `deleteProof` never writes a tombstone for a Proof it did not read, so a `set` here is always a create and never meets the denied update arm. The alternative #1147 offered—admitting an idempotent same-target update for the admin—unblocks the takedown but leaves the sweeper free to strip live media, so it was not taken.

**And EVERY client delete reaches only media no Proof DOCUMENT points at** (Codex P1, PR #1157 for the carve-out; Codex round 4 P2, PR #1163 for making it the owner's only branch; Codex round 5 P2 for making it the whole arm's condition). The harm the delete arm exists to stop is a Feed entry whose image can never load, and that needs an entry to be the harm at all: media no Proof document points at is a blob nobody can reach through any surface, so it may be cleared in every state, closed Events included—and media that IS pointed at is nobody's to remove, in any state, the owner's, the Admin's and the admin-owner's alike. The arm asks that question FIRST—`firestore.exists()` on `events/{eventId}/proofs/{proofId}`, where the Proof id is the object's basename because `uploadProofMedia` writes `proofs/{eventId}/{uid}/{proofId}.{ext}` and `firestore.rules` pins `storagePath` to exactly that shape—and short-circuits, so a live Proof's object is refused for ONE access whoever asked, and the OWNER's orphan delete is allowed on that same single access because `isOwner` reads only the token and the path. Only a NON-OWNER clearing an orphan—an Admin taking a reported photo down, or a stranger being refused one—spends both, which is the cap rather than past it. That carve-out is not a loosening for its own sake: it is what makes both client paths recoverable across the quiesce, because an `attachProof` upload whose transaction the freeze then denies, and a `deleteProof` whose commit landed before its Storage delete could, each leave exactly such a blob. The tombstone sweeper needs none of it—`revokeDeletedProofMedia` runs on the Admin SDK, which bypasses `storage.rules` entirely—so the carve-out is there for the two CLIENT paths and nothing else. The Proof id is the file name with only its FINAL extension stripped, so a dotted id such as `p.q` (media `p.q.jpg`) is looked up whole; a split on the first dot looked up `proofs/p` and released media a surviving document still pointed at (Phase 4b P1, PR #1157 run 2).

The `exists()` that used to guard the Event `get()` is spent on the Proof instead, so a MISSING Event document denies by erroring that `get()`—now on the NON-OWNER orphan branch, which is the only branch that still reaches it. Media whose Proof document lives under an Event document that does not is refused one step earlier still, by the Proof check itself. Denying is the conservative direction either way; absence-means-open is preserved where it actually matters, because a legacy or orphaned path has no Proof document and its OWNER is answered by the carve-out before the Event is ever read.

The ADMIN takedown is deliberately untouched and stays open in every state, live, closing and archived: a permanent record that locks out its own Admin is [#808](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/808)'s support incident in a new place.

`eventQuiescing(eventId)` is the ONE predicate that tells the two halves apart—shut but not yet frozen—and it guards exactly two arms: the admin deletes on `players/{uid}` and on the per-Day honour. Those are the two documents the frozen record IS built from, and the freeze's transaction reads only the Event document, so a delete landing between the record's server re-read and the commit would neither conflict with it nor retry it—the write-once record would simply keep a row moderation had already removed. They hold still for the length of the quiesce and REOPEN once the freeze lands, and that asymmetry is the point: a permanent record needs a takedown path, and a record whose Players could never be erased is [#808](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/808)'s support incident in a new place.

**And the Event document itself cannot be deleted while closed to play** (Phase 4b P1, PR #1139). Deleting a Firestore document leaves its subcollections in place, and a MISSING Event document reads as open—the default that keeps every Event written before the freeze existed playable. Left on its bare `isAdmin` gate, the delete arm was therefore the one admin write that undid the freeze: an admin deleting an archived Event reopened every gameplay write under the surviving Player rows, Boards and Proofs in a single operation. `firestore.rules` denies the delete on both halves, reading `status` and `archiving` from the stored document rather than through `eventClosedToPlay` (which would spend a `get()` on the document the arm already holds); the remedy while closing is the console's own **Reopen play**.

Rendering is not enforcement, and this spec refuses to pretend otherwise: hiding the controls client-side would leave a direct Firestore write succeeding, which is exactly the client-only convention D8 rejects. Every rules case in the test map below is a direct SDK write, not a UI path.

The Event document's own writes split across TWO admin-gated arms—which is where "admin-only toggle" comes from, inherited rather than invented, since no Player may write that document at all. The split is legible and mechanical at once: `allow` statements short-circuit, so the flip is decided by a small dedicated arm and never pays for the schedule and freeze-boundary validation the general arm performs. That arm already sits one clause from Firestore's 1000-expression cap (#850/#934), and folding the archive checks into it exceeded the cap on the very write they exist to validate.

**The archive-flip arm** accepts exactly one shape of write:

1. It may only be taken from the QUIESCED state—a stored document carrying `archiving: true` and not yet archived—so no writer, client or direct SDK, can skip the window in which gameplay was already shut. And only from the SAME quiesce the caller took: the stored document must carry a USABLE generation—a positive integer—and the write must name it in the flip-only `archivedUnder` (`boundToStoredQuiesce`), which is one of the keys clause 5's `hasOnly` guard admits alongside the three transition fields.
2. It must carry a numeric, bounded `archivedAt`, which makes "no reader ever sees an archived Event with no stamp" an enforced invariant rather than a property of one client's writer.
3. It must carry the COMPLETE record (`completeArchiveRecord(request.resource.data.archive, request.resource.data.archivedAt)`). Type-checking `archive` only when present accepted `{status: 'archived', archivedAt}` on its own, and that state is both irreversible and useless: the freeze denies every gameplay write while the archived surfaces fall back to the LIVE view the record exists to replace (Codex P2, PR #1139). The helper types every top-level key of `EventArchive`—`eventName`, `freezeAt` and the First-BINGO pair as type-or-null, because `null` is a legitimate stored value for each—validates that pair TOGETHER through `firstBingoPairComplete` (both `null`, or two maps naming the SAME holder; typing them one at a time accepted a map beside a `null` and two maps naming different Players), and requires the record's own `archivedAt` to EQUAL the document's, which both type-checks it and stops the two ever putting different answers on screen. An ABSENT key needs no clause of its own: reading a missing key errors the expression, which denies. Rules cannot iterate a list, so the standings ROWS stay unchecked—the same stated residual `bannedUids` carries—but their COUNT is bound to `playerCount` through `standingsSizeMatches`, because a list's size is one expression rather than a walk (§ "The frozen record"). `dailyHonors` is bounded by the same reasoning at `MAX_DAYS` (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162), round 9): it had no length clause at all, so a direct admin flip could freeze thousands of scalar or duplicate entries into a field whose contract is at most one honour per Day on an Event that supports at most ten Days—a record no builder can produce, permanent because the flip locks `archive`, and unrepairable because no client can amend it. `archive.dailyHonors.size() <= 10`, with 10 restated from `MAX_DAYS` in `src/data/eventLimits.ts` for the same shared-contract reason the 200-row bound and `finiteArchiveNumber`'s magnitude are restated. `writableArchiveRecord` mirrors it EXPLICITLY rather than leaving it to follow from `ascendingHonorDays`: that clause proves the indexes strictly increase and `writableDayHonor` asks only `Number.isInteger` of each, so `[100, 200, 300, …]` is a strictly ascending list of well-formed entries of any length, and at most ten does not follow.

    **And each half of the pair is validated WHOLE, not merely matched** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). Matching uids prove the two halves agree about WHO; they say nothing about whether either half can be rendered—so two uid-only maps passed, and a direct admin write could flip an Event whose `firstBingo` carried no `displayName` and no `at` and whose `firstBingoRow` carried none of the standings fields and no `rank`. The flip is irreversible, so the archived Leaderboard and Share Card that the record exists to feed would read a hole out of a record the rules had already locked. `firstBingoHonorComplete` and `firstBingoRowComplete` therefore type every scalar `ArchivedFirstBingo` and `ArchivedFirstBingoRow` declare, exactly as `src/data/eventArchive.ts` writes them: `displayName` a string (`archiveName`), `at`, `bingoCount`, `squaresMarked` and `rank` numbers, `blackout` a bool, and `firstBingoAt` type-or-null, because `archiveInstant` yields `number | null` for a holder with no readable instant. Type checks only, and no character bound on the two strings, matching the rest of the helper: the record's own ceiling is BYTES, measured twice before the quiesce and again inside the transaction, and a second differently-counted ceiling here could deny—after the Event was already shut—a name the writer's own UTF-16 clamp had passed. They are separate helpers on the same arm for the reason `completeArchiveRecord` is one: the general Event arm sits at Firestore's 1000-expression cap, `allow` statements short-circuit, and the full-sized record case proves the flip still evaluates well inside it.

    **But `is number` was never enough for a NUMBER** (CodeRabbit, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). It admits `NaN` and both infinities—Firestore doubles like any other—so the irreversible flip could persist a headline instant of `NaN` or a count of `Infinity`, and the archived Leaderboard and Share Card the record exists to feed would render exactly that, permanently. Rules have no `isFinite()`, so `finiteArchiveNumber` stands in for it with the estate's own magnitude idiom: 4102444800000, the 2100-01-01 bound `standingsFreezeAt` and the flip's own `archivedAt` already use that way. Every comparison against `NaN` is false and each infinity fails one side, so a two-sided bound is the whole check, and the magnitude is doing that one job rather than acting as a semantic ceiling on a count or an instant. `rank` is held to more—`is int` and positive—because it is the one field the writer COMPUTES rather than copies (`holderAt + 1` over a non-negative index), the JS SDK stores an integral number as a Firestore integer (the footing `usableArchiveToken` already stands on), and a rank of 0, a negative one or a fractional one names a place in the standings that does not exist.

    **And that place has to exist in the ROSTER the record declares** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162), round 9). `rank > 0` says the place is a real ordinal and says nothing about whether the roster is that long, so `playerCount: 0` beside `standings: []`—a pairing `standingsSizeMatches` is perfectly happy with, the two agreeing the roster is empty—still admitted a First-BINGO pair whose row claimed `rank: 1`. The record then says in one breath that nobody played and that somebody came first, permanently, and the archived Share Card prints that place out of a roster it also reports as having none. `firstBingoPairComplete` therefore requires `firstBingoRow.rank <= playerCount`, which is where the comparison has to live: `firstBingoRowComplete` is handed the row alone and cannot see the count. Against `playerCount` rather than `standings.size()`, because `playerCount` is the COMPLETE ban-filtered cardinality while `standings` is only its bounded prefix—the holder's row is carried outside that prefix precisely so a place past 200 can still be printed, and asking the prefix would refuse the very record the pairing exists for. Combined with `rank > 0` it is also what forbids a non-null pair beside a roster of zero. The writer produces exactly this (`holderAt + 1` over an index into `ranked`, beside `ranked.length`), so the bound holds by construction for every record the builder can build, and `writableArchiveRecord` asks it on the near side of the quiesce.

    The bound is deliberately TWO-SIDED rather than the `> 0` those Event instants carry, and the counts are not required non-negative. This arm may only require what the WRITER guarantees, or it denies after the Event has already been shut—the same trade the character bound above declines. `players/{uid}` validates no field at all, so a Player can self-write a negative `bingoCount` or `firstBingoAt` on their own row, and `archiveCount`/`archiveInstant` COPY those through: the builder coerces what is unreadable, it does not adjudicate what is merely odd (ADR 0001). A non-negative bound would therefore refuse a legitimate record over a Player row no Admin can edit, on the one write that cannot be retried. Finiteness is exactly what the writer's coercions promise, so it is exactly what is asked for.

    **And the record's OWN two numbers were still admitting them** (Codex P2, PR [#1162](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1162)). Bounding the pair left `playerCount` and `freezeAt` on the bare `is number` the pair had just outgrown, so a direct admin flip could persist `playerCount: NaN` or `freezeAt: Infinity`—permanently, because the flip is irreversible and locks `archive`, and visibly, because the console's archived-state summary renders the count verbatim. `playerCount` is now a non-negative INT, which is more than finiteness and is exactly what the writer guarantees: it is `ranked.length`, a count the builder COMPUTES rather than copies, so it stands on `rank`'s footing rather than on the deliberately permissive one the copied counts stand on, and a fractional or negative roster size names no roster at all. `freezeAt` is `finiteArchiveNumber` or `null`—the same bound the pair's instants carry, with `null` still meaning an Event that had no Standings Freeze. It is the one number in the record that needs no Player to misbehave: it resolves from `frozenAt`, which the Admin SDK writes and no arm here constrains, which is why `src/data/eventArchive.ts` clamps it for the same reason it clamps the copied stats. `archivedAt` needs no clause of its own—`== stamp` ties it to the document's own, and clause 2 has already bounded that—and `eventName`, `standings` and `dailyHonors` carry no number at the top level, so those are every one there is. The change is confined to the FLIP arm's own helper family; the general Event arm sits at the 1000-expression cap and is untouched, and the full-sized-record case still passes.
4. It must clear `archiving`, so the freeze is carried only by the half that cannot be moved.
5. It must touch NOTHING ELSE (`affectedKeys().hasOnly(['status', 'archivedAt', 'archiving', 'archivedUnder', 'archive'])`—the fourth key is the flip-only binding, written here and nowhere else, which is why it can be required to appear, and the fifth is the record, which this arm is the only place that may introduce), which is what lets the arm skip the configuration validation without opening a hole through it: a write that also moved `days`, `bannedUids` or `standingsFreezeAt` is not an archive flip and falls through to the arm that checks those fields, where a not-yet-archived document is refused.

The record checks live HERE rather than on the general arm on purpose. That arm sits at Firestore's 1000-expression cap (#1142 item 1) and `allow` statements short-circuit, so an ordinary admin write never pays for a clause only an archive flip can satisfy. Measured against the emulator before and after: no write anywhere in the estate newly reaches the cap, and a FULL 200-row record still flips—the rules never walk the list, so the record's size costs the arm nothing.

**The general admin arm never moves the lifecycle fields, the record or the finale marker** (Codex P1, PR #1157 round 6; #1151; Codex P1, PR #1162). It keeps every other Event write and refuses any CHANGE to `status`, `archiving`, `archivedAt`, `archiveToken`, `archivedUnder`, `archive` or `finaleCompletedAt` in one `affectedKeys` check (the token is in the set because a closing Event's generation must be IMMUTABLE there—a whole-document writer restoring an abandoned token beside the current quiesce would let a stale `archiveEvent` pass `boundToStoredQuiesce` after all; Codex P2, PR #1157 round 7—and the record is in it because the flip is the one arm that may introduce it and nothing may rewrite it afterwards); a create is held to the one shape a new Event may be born in (open, not closing, no stamp, no record, no marker). `finaleCompletedAt` is the one entry in that set that is not a lifecycle field: it is server-written, and an admin able to write it could clear the very warning standing between a podium that has not posted yet and an irreversible archive that forgoes it. It carries no shape check, because a field no client may write needs no type validation and an unwritable field is cheaper to enforce than a validated one on an arm at the expression cap; none of the four dedicated arms names it either, so the flip cannot smuggle it. The create half names it for symmetry rather than reach, since no client create of an Event document can succeed at all—`isAdmin(eventId)` reads the very document being created. That single clause is what keeps the freeze write-once—an un-archive, a second stamp, a re-shut under the old generation, a rewritten record and a stray stamp or a stray record on a live Event are all a change to one of the six and are refused here first—and it replaced the shape and lock clauses it stands in for, because this arm sits at Firestore's 1000-expression cap (#1142 item 1) and the transition check the round-6 finding needed did not fit beside them. Adding `archive` to that existing list costs nothing: one more element in a literal the arm already evaluates once, measured before and after against the emulator. Restating an unchanged value is not a change, so a partial update that echoes the stored state still passes, and every other admin write (moderation, configuration, `bannedUids`) is untouched by the freeze. The four transitions live on four dedicated arms placed AFTER it, so an ordinary admin write never pays for them:

1. **The shut** (false to true): from a live, unarchived Event, by an admin, installing an `archiveToken` that is a positive integer STRICTLY GREATER than whatever number the document still carries, and touching nothing else. This is what makes every quiesce a new generation at the rules boundary rather than by client convention: `abandonArchive` leaves the old generation in place, and without this arm an admin—or a stale pre-token client—could re-shut the Event under it, after which a stale `archiveEvent(A)` passed `boundToStoredQuiesce` and could commit standings from before gameplay resumed. `>` rather than `!=` because the arm can only see one value (Phase 4b P1, PR #1157 run 4): merely-different let generation A back in after B replaced it, and an increasing counter makes a used generation unusable forever instead of for one step.
2. **The reopen** (true to false): from the quiescing state, by an admin, touching nothing else. The generation is deliberately left where it is—it is the counter the next shut has to exceed, and a conditional `abandonArchive(expectedToken)` still needs it to tell its own generation from a newer one.
3. **The flip** (§ above): from the quiescing state, bound to the stored generation through a FLIP-ONLY field—`archivedUnder`, which the flip must write and which must equal the stored `archiveToken`—carrying a numeric bounded `archivedAt` and the COMPLETE record, clearing `archiving`, touching nothing else. Comparing the incoming `archiveToken` was no binding at all: `request.resource.data` is the whole resulting document, so a flip that omitted the token inherited the stored one; a field the document does not carry until the flip cannot be inherited, and it is locked with the record afterwards (Phase 4b P1, PR #1157 run 3).
4. **The repair**: a closing Event carrying NO usable generation—usable meaning the one predicate the shut, the binding and the repair share with the client's `usableArchiveToken`: an integer greater than zero (Codex P2, PR #1157 round 8; restated for the counter in run 4)—shut out of band, or by a build that predates the generation and therefore carries a string—may be given one by an admin, touching nothing else. The replacement must still EXCEED any number the document carries, so a repair cannot reset the counter beneath a generation that has already been in force. It is the shape `beginArchive` writes when it finds an unidentified quiesce, and a closing Event that already has a usable generation never matches it.

### The server-side half—the Admin SDK writers

The second child of the epic, [#1150](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1150). `firestore.rules` stops a Player from marking a square on a closed Event. It stops none of the Cloud Functions, which run on the Admin SDK and bypass the rules entirely—so without this half "archived" would mean closed for Players and open for the estate's own writers, which is not a freeze at all.

`eventClosedToPlay(event)` in `functions/src/unlockDay.ts` is the same predicate restated at that boundary, with the same two halves and the same defaults: `status == 'archived'` or `archiving == true`, an absent field reading as open, and a missing Event document reading as open exactly as the rules' `exists()` guard makes it. It is exported and structurally typed so the daily engagement email can restate it at its own boundary rather than spelling the comparison a second time—the two must never disagree about what "this Event is over" means.

It guards every core that writes gameplay state: `stampDaySnapshot`, `manualUnlockNow`, `resnapshotDayIfNoBoards`, `runScheduledUnlock`, `runFinaleBeats`, both finale freeze transactions, `postFinaleMoment`, and the finale-complete marker `runFinaleBeats` stamps last (§ "The pending-claim drain gate"). `SnapshotResult` and `ResnapshotResult` each gain an `'archived'` member, and the daily email's `DailySendResult.reason` gains one too—reported rather than thrown, because a sweep that finds a closed Event has done its job correctly.

**Each guard is checked against the core's FIRST read and re-checked inside the transaction that writes.** The window is real in three ways. The quarter-hourly sweep selects its Events once per run and then processes them one at a time, so a run selected moments before the archive still arrives on a closed Event—and a Cloud Functions retry can arrive minutes later. The selection query is `status == 'active'`, which does not exclude the CLOSING state at all, because that state is deliberately still `'active'` (§ "Three states, two fields"). And `unlockDayNow` is an ADMIN callable, which the rules bind to the freeze for gameplay for the stated reason: an archive whose own organiser can still open a Day is not a frozen record.

The layering means no single guard is the only thing standing between a closed Event and a write, which is the point—but it also means a test that only asserts "nothing was written" would pass with any one of them removed, answered by the layer below. Each guard is therefore pinned by what it SAVES as well as by what it refuses: a sweep that stands down at its own boundary reads the Event once and stops, where one that fell through re-reads it per Day, queries the prompt pool, or opens a transaction. `tests/functions/post-sailing-archive-guards.test.ts` counts those reads, so every guard fails a test when it alone is removed.

**`postFinaleMoment` writes inside a transaction for the same reason.** It used to re-read the Event and then `set` the system Moment, and a fresh read only SHORTENS that gap—the archive can commit between the read and the write, and the beat then appends to a Feed the record has already preserved, which is the one thing an archived Feed promises it will not do. Reading the Event inside the transaction that writes the Moment closes it, because a transaction serializes against the documents it READS and the archive's own write touches that document: a commit landing in the window aborts the attempt, and the retry re-reads the closed state. That is the guarantee the snapshot, re-snapshot and finale-freeze transactions already had; this beat was the last one taking it on trust. The injected `Transaction` surface gains the `set` the real Admin-SDK transaction already has.

**The daily engagement email is stopped by the same predicate, read twice.** `runDailyEmailSweep` selects `status == 'active'` and `sendDailyEmailForEvent` checked neither half of the freeze, so an Event held in the closing state kept inviting people to play while every gameplay write the mail asks for was denied at the rules boundary. Two distinct holes: the closing state is deliberately still `'active'`, so the selection query never excluded it, and the selection runs ONCE per sweep, before any Event is processed. `sendDailyEmailForEvent` now reads the freeze off the Event document itself—once from the Event the call opens with, ahead of the admin toggle because the occasion being over is the stronger statement, and again from a fresh read taken immediately before the first send, after the hostname resolution, the roster page and the standings snapshot. That second read is what closes the selection-to-delivery window; it is exactly the elapsed time a sweep selected just before the freeze spends getting there.

### Moderation is not a gameplay write

Every `isAdmin(eventId)` arm keeps working on an archived Event: hide, restore, delete, ban, resolve a dangling claim, edit configuration, and delete proof media from Storage. A frozen Event needs a takedown path—a reported photo that can never be hidden again is the support incident ([#808](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/808)'s "an archive that locks out its own Admin").

**Which is why the moderation delete had to STOP CARRYING gameplay** (Codex P2, PR #1139). Leaving the arm open is not the same as leaving the operation open: `deleteProof` (`src/data/proofs.ts`) removed the Proof document and, in the SAME transaction, unmarked the Board cell the Proof backed, rewrote the owner's stats and dropped their Tally marker. Those are gameplay writes `eventOpenForPlay` denies for the organiser as much as anyone, and one denial inside a transaction takes the whole thing down—so the advertised takedown failed outright on exactly the Event whose play can never resume.

So the transaction reads the Event—INSIDE itself, so a quiesce committing in the window aborts the attempt rather than landing a cleanup on a Board the freeze has already shut—and skips the gameplay cleanup on an ARCHIVED Event. There is nothing for it to repair: Marks can never move again, so the cell the deleted Proof backed is exactly as frozen as everything around it. What the delete still does is what a takedown is for—the document leaves the Feed and the media leaves Storage.

**But the two halves of the freeze part company here, because one of them is REVERSIBLE** (Phase 4b P2, PR #1157 run 4). The archived skip is safe precisely because nothing can move again; the CLOSING skip made the same claim about a state an Admin undoes with one tap. Skip the Board unmark, the stat rewrite and the Tally cleanup on a closing Event and the Proof is gone while the square it backed stays marked, counted in the owner's squares and lines, with its Tally marker standing—and the moment play reopens that is LIVE gameplay, inconsistent, with nothing left to repair it: the delete that would have has already been reported as clean and the `storagePath` went with the document.

So a Proof that backs a marked cell is REFUSED while the Event is closing, with a typed `ProofBacksMarkWhileClosingError` whose message names the remedy—reopen play, then delete—and the transaction throws before it writes anything, so the document, the Board and the media are all exactly as they were. Refusing rather than attempting, because attempting is the denial that used to take the whole takedown down with it; refusing rather than skipping, because the skip is a silent success that leaves the Event wrong. A Proof that backs NOTHING—already unmarked, or never on a cell—still deletes in either state, since there is nothing to keep consistent, and the ARCHIVED skip is untouched. The refusal reaches the Admin through the affordance the delete already had (`AsyncButton`'s inline failure pill, `specs/admin-async-feedback.md`), which now takes its copy from this one error rather than from the generic "try again" that would be exactly wrong about a refusal that repeats until play reopens.

**And the Storage delete moved AFTER the Firestore commit, on BOTH paths—the owner's and the Admin's** (Codex P1, PR #1157). Main revokes the media first, on the reasoning that a failed blob delete should leave the document rather than orphan the media. The freeze is what makes that ordering wrong: an owner's delete that starts while the Event is open can revoke the media and then lose its transaction to an Admin's quiesce, leaving a surviving Proof pointing at media that is gone—on the one Event where nothing can be re-posted, and with no way back, because the owner's document delete is now denied and the object it named is already deleted. Committing first makes a lost race a NO-OP: the Proof and its media stay exactly as they were. What a committed delete can still leave is an ORPHANED blob, which is the harmless direction, and which `storage.rules` lets its owner clear in every state precisely because no document points at it.

**The retry is what makes the inversion safe, and it is written INSIDE the commit** ([#1153](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1153)). Round 1 of the #1157 review rejected the same reordering because the commit removes the Proof, its `storagePath` and the surface that offered the delete all at once, leaving a post-commit Storage failure with nothing to retry from. `deleteProof` therefore writes a tombstone—`events/{eventId}/proofStorageDeletes/{proofId}`, carrying `{storagePath, uid, requestedAt}` plus the optional `generation` below—**in the same transaction as the Proof delete**, so either both land or neither does and there is no instant at which the Proof is gone and the record of its media is not. **That row's shape is one contract, not two** (Codex round 4 P1, PR #1163). It is written by the browser and consumed by the separately-rooted Functions project, so `ProofStorageDeleteDoc` is declared in `src/domainTypes.d.ts`—the repository's single shared domain contract—and `deleteProof` annotates the object it writes with it, while `functions/src/proofStorageDeletes.ts` imports it through the same `import type` path the daily-email and finale builders use. The sweeper keeps a SEPARATE all-`unknown` input shape for what it reads off the event snapshot before validation, because the Admin SDK bypasses `firestore.rules` and a document is untrusted until checked; the two are pinned to each other's key set at compile time, so a field added to one and not the other fails `cd functions && npm run build` rather than letting the writer and the sweeper drift. A SECOND pin sits beside it (Codex round 6 P2, PR #1163), because the contract now carries two fields the client never writes: `IDENTITY_FIELDS`—the list `isSameRevocation` actually compares—is checked against the contract MINUS the sweep-lease keys, so a new field cannot join the row without an explicit decision about whether it is part of a revocation's identity or bookkeeping about it. That choice used to be made silently, by whether anyone remembered to add a line to the comparison. Writing the row is the LAST thing the client does with it: it revokes the object inline, leaves the row standing whether that succeeds or fails, and still propagates a rejection, so a takedown that did not revoke the media is never reported as clean. RETIRING the row is SERVER-ONLY (Codex round 3 P1). The client used to clear it once the object was provably gone, and that was only ever an optimisation—but the row's path is entirely predictable (`events/{eventId}/proofStorageDeletes/{proofId}`), so the arm that allowed it also let the media's OWNER blind-delete the one record still owing a revocation the moment an ADMIN takedown's inline Storage delete failed or was interrupted; denying the READ never prevented a DELETE. `revokeDeletedProofMedia` would then find no tombstone, abandon by design, and leave the reported photo reachable through its download URL. So the subject of a takedown can no longer cancel its durable half: `firestore.rules` denies every client delete on the row, and `deleteProof` issues none rather than issuing a write that is always denied. Nothing is lost, because the inline revocation leaves the object gone—which is exactly the `404` the sweeper counts as SUCCESS before retiring the row itself. It also retires the hazard the previous shape carried: a detached `deleteDoc` that never settled once left `deleteProof` pending forever, so the caller never learned a fully successful delete had succeeded and the cache purge never ran at all (Phase 4b P2). There is now no bookkeeping call left to hang on. The local cache purge (#373) runs in a `finally`, because what it follows is the commit rather than the blob delete: the Proof has left the Feed either way, so this device must stop serving the photo out of its own cache whichever half failed ([#1148](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1148)).

**The durability is not the client's, and neither is the retirement.** `revokeDeletedProofMedia` (`functions/src/index.ts`, over `revokeProofMedia` in `functions/src/proofStorageDeletes.ts`) triggers on the tombstone's CREATE, revokes the object with the Admin SDK, retires the row only afterwards—as the ONLY writer that may retire one at all—and rethrows any other failure so Cloud Functions redelivers (`retry: true`). That covers what no client-side retry can: the tab that closed, the network that never came back, the Player who reopens the app on a different device, the Player who never reopens it at all, and the Player who comes back hostile. "Already gone" counts as SUCCESS, because the ordinary outcome is that the client's own inline Storage delete won the race, and retrying a discharged revocation would hammer it until the platform gave up and strand the row. A row whose path escapes its own Event and Proof is dropped rather than retried—redelivery cannot make an unconfinable path confinable—and logged, because the only way one exists is a write that did not come through `firestore.rules`. That confinement is asked a second time there on purpose: the rules answer "is this row well-formed for its author", and this handler holds a bucket-wide delete, so "may THIS trigger delete THAT object" is a different question and is not taken on trust from the first.

**A Proof id that owes a revocation cannot come back, and the object the row named is pinned by GENERATION** (Phase 4b P1). `existsAfter` stops a tombstone being minted over a live Proof; nothing stopped a live Proof being minted over a standing tombstone. An owner could legally delete Proof P together with its own tombstone and then re-create P—same id, same `storagePath`—while the Event was still open, which reassembles exactly the pair the clause forbids: the next admin takedown's `set` is an UPDATE against the standing row, denied, taking the whole transaction with it, and the delayed sweep meanwhile revokes media the re-created Feed entry still points at, on an archived Event as readily as a live one. The Proof CREATE arm therefore refuses while `events/{eventId}/proofStorageDeletes/{proofId}` exists. It costs the app nothing—`attachProof` mints a fresh auto-id per capture and never re-posts under an id it has already used—and the hold lifts the moment the row is retired, which only the sweeper can do.

**The row names the object the PROOF stored, not the one the caller passed** (Phase 4b P2). The path pin and the owner binding together still admitted a row for the WRONG object: `{proofId}.jpg`, `{proofId}.webm` and `{proofId}.m4a` all satisfy the shape pin, and the owner check only asks that the live Proof belongs to the uid the row declares—so a `.jpg` Proof could be deleted alongside a tombstone naming the same owner's `.webm`, after which the client and the sweeper revoke a name that Proof never used while its real media stays reachable, with the document that recorded which object was its own already gone. Reaching it took nothing exotic: `deleteProof` took the path from a CALLER'S argument, read off a Feed or Review-queue snapshot that can be stale, and an authorized caller can construct the batch by hand. So both halves now read the Proof. `firestore.rules` requires `request.resource.data.storagePath` to equal the live Proof's own `storagePath`, reusing the `get()` the owner check already performs—a repeated access to the same document is served from the per-request cache, so the arm still spends three of the twenty document accesses a request gets, the same boundary as before (six distinct-Event takedowns pass and seven deny, measured in the emulator). And `deleteProof` takes the tombstone's path AND the Storage delete's from the Proof document it read INSIDE the transaction, so the fast path and the durable record can never target different blobs. A Proof that stored no object—a text Proof—is refused a tombstone by the rule and writes none from the client, which is correct: it owes no revocation.

**The `storagePath` argument stays in `deleteProof`'s signature as a HINT.** It is the only path in hand before the transaction opens, and the object's Storage `generation` has to be read there—the transaction callback must not be doing unrelated network I/O on every retry, and the value has to exist by the time the tombstone is written inside it. When the argument and the stored path disagree, the STORED one wins for the row and for the revocation, and the generation the read produced is DROPPED rather than written: it describes some other blob, and binding the sweep to it would answer `412` against the right object and retire the row with the media still in place. The key is optional precisely so it can be omitted. The argument is also the only path left when the Proof document could not be read at all—the re-run of a takedown whose Storage half failed—where there is no stored value to prefer and the behaviour is what it was before the tombstone existed.

**And the sweep is made safe against reuse on its own terms, because the rules are not its only writer.** A tombstone is a promise about the past discharged in the future, and the Admin SDK bypasses `firestore.rules` entirely while this handler holds a bucket-wide delete, so the sweeper asks five questions rather than trusting the row. FIRST, IS ANY DEBT STILL OWED: it re-reads `events/{eventId}/proofStorageDeletes/{proofId}`—the row that triggered the delivery—on EVERY delivery, and abandons the run outright when it is gone. The event payload describes the row as it was CREATED, and a delivery can arrive long afterwards (`retry: true` keeps a failed one coming back, and even a first delivery is not instantaneous), by which time an EARLIER delivery of this same event may already have discharged the revocation and cleared the row—which, retirement being server-only, is now the only way one goes. Retirement is exactly what FREES the Proof id for reuse (above), so a stale delivery that skipped this question would be revoking against an id that may already belong to somebody else's re-post—and a row whose metadata read had failed carries no generation to protect the object, so it would take the replacement media a permitted re-post had uploaded before creating its Proof document, leaving the new Feed entry pointing at nothing. Nothing is deleted on that path, the tombstone least of all: there is no row of ours left to retire, and a blind delete at that path could only take a LATER revocation's row with it. SECOND, IS IT THIS DELIVERY'S DEBT: existence is not identity, so the row that came back is compared against the event snapshot's own operation identity—`requestedAt`, `storagePath`, `uid` and `generation`, the row's whole shape, which cannot change while it stands because the arm is create-only and update-denied—and a mismatch is logged and ABANDONED with that row left exactly where it is. Without it the freed id could be re-posted and taken down again, leaving a SECOND revocation's row at the same path for a very late delivery of the first to find: it would sweep on its own stale snapshot, take `412` against the new object, and RETIRE the new row with its media still in the bucket. The standing row is not this delivery's to clear, redelivery can never make it so, and its own delivery is still owed. THIRD, AM I THE ONE SWEEPING IT: the two questions above are PRE-checks and nothing more, so the row is CLAIMED for exclusive processing before anything is deleted—see the sweep lease below—and a delivery that finds another live lease touches neither the bucket nor the row and REJECTS, so `retry: true` brings it back. FOURTH, IS THE PROOF BACK: it READS `events/{eventId}/proofs/{proofId}` and abandons the revocation if a Proof is there—media a live Feed entry points at is never revoked on the strength of a row written about an earlier one. A read failure in ANY Firestore question PROPAGATES rather than being swallowed, because "I could not tell" must never resolve to "delete it". FIFTH, IS IT THE SAME OBJECT: it deletes by generation, always—`deleteProof` reads the object's Storage `generation` immediately before the commit and records it on the row, and the sweep passes it as `ifGenerationMatch`, so a name re-occupied since keeps its bytes and answers `412`. A row that recorded none takes the generation the object carries RIGHT NOW, read under the lease, and is deleted bound to that; an object already gone at that read is the ordinary discharged revocation and retires the row without a bucket delete. The window between the client's metadata read and the commit is closed from the other side by proof-object IMMUTABILITY (§ "The enforcement"): with no overwrite possible, a `412` can only mean the name was re-occupied after a DELETE, which is exactly the case retiring the row is correct for. The fourth and fifth outcomes retire the row, because neither can be improved on by redelivery; the first three retire nothing—the first because there is no row of ours left, the second because the row there belongs to somebody else's revocation, the third because it belongs to a delivery that is still working on it—and only the third REJECTS, because only the third leaves a revocation still owed. All five are logged. **And every retirement is itself a COMPARE-AND-DELETE, not a delete** (Codex round 4 P2, PR #1163). The second question is a PRE-check: it reads the row at one instant, and the retirement it authorises happens at a later one—after a bucket round trip, on the path that matters. The gap is enough for the whole reuse sequence to run inside it: duplicate delivery A validates row A and pauses, A2 discharges and retires it, the freed Proof id is re-posted and taken down again, and row B is standing at the same path when A resumes to take its `412`. An unconditional retirement would delete B—whose own delivery would then find no tombstone, abandon by design, and leave B's media in the bucket, which is the very outcome the second question exists to prevent. So the retirement re-reads the row inside an Admin SDK transaction and deletes it only while `isSameRevocation` still holds against this delivery's own snapshot; a refusal is LOGGED AND ACCEPTED rather than rethrown, because the row standing there belongs to a delivery that is still owed and still coming, and redelivering this one cannot make it ours. **That identity is compared STRUCTURALLY** (`sameFirestoreValue`, Codex round 5 P2, PR #1163). Strict equality is right for every row the rules admit—all four fields are primitives there, and `sameFirestoreValue` is `===` for those—but the rules are not the only writer, and the malformed row the defensive branch above exists to DROP is precisely one carrying an array or a map under one of those names. Firestore deserialises a container into a fresh JavaScript object on every read, so the event snapshot and the retirement transaction's own re-read compared unequal for a row that had not changed at all: `retireTombstoneIfSame` refused, the poison row stood forever, and the Proof-create hold that lives exactly as long as it does held that id forever with it. `sameFirestoreValue` compares primitives with `Object.is`, arrays element-wise, Firestore maps by key set and value, bytes by content, and the Admin SDK's own value types (`Timestamp`, `GeoPoint`, `DocumentReference`) through the `isEqual` each of them publishes—no new dependency, and anything it cannot reason about compares UNEQUAL, which is the safe direction: a false negative leaves a row standing for the delivery that owes it, while a false positive would retire somebody else's revocation with its media still in the bucket. The generation is OPTIONAL on the row and in the rules arm: the read is one extra Storage round trip on a path whose whole design goal is that it cannot be made to fail, so a metadata failure omits the key instead of denying the write.

**But the compare-and-delete protected the RETIREMENT and only the retirement, which was not enough** (Codex round 6 P2, PR #1163). Both Firestore questions above it are reads taken at one instant, and the operation they authorise that actually destroys something—the bucket delete—sat between them and the protected retirement with nothing holding it. Run the same interleaving against a row carrying NO generation, which is the shape a failed client-side metadata read legitimately produces: duplicate deliveries A and A2 both pass the two pre-checks, A pauses, A2 deletes the object and retires the row, the freed Proof id is re-posted with new media uploaded to the very same path before its Proof document exists—so the Proof read is still false—and A resumes into an unconditioned path delete that removes the live replacement's bytes. Nothing downstream can undo that. It also contradicted this spec's own guarantee that reuse cannot make a stale delivery revoke replacement media.

**So a row is swept by exactly ONE delivery at a time, through a lease on the row itself.** After the identity match and before anything else, `revokeProofMedia` CLAIMS the row in an Admin SDK transaction: it re-reads, re-asks both pre-check questions against its own read, requires no active lease, and—atomically with that answer—stamps `leaseId` and `leaseAt` naming this delivery. Only the holder goes on to read the Proof, read the object's generation and delete it; a delivery that finds another live lease touches neither the bucket nor the row and REJECTS, which is the one abandon in this handler that does not resolve. It has to be, because `retry: true` redelivers a rejected delivery and only a rejected one: a holder that fails leaves its lease standing, its own redelivery lands seconds later inside that lease, and acking there would end the retry chain and strand a revocation nobody is discharging. Rejecting costs an extra redelivery when the holder really is still running—it acks its own delivery, and the loser's next attempt finds the row retired and resolves—and it is what makes "somebody sweeps once the lease ages out" true rather than hopeful. The two fields are SERVER-ONLY, which is what makes the claim trustworthy: the create arm's `hasOnly` does not admit either, update is denied outright, and only the Admin SDK identity that bypasses the rules can write one—otherwise the media's own owner could park an unexpired lease on their takedown's row and stall its sweep. They are deliberately excluded from `isSameRevocation` (and that exclusion is pinned at compile time, above): the lease is the one thing on the row that legitimately moves, and folding it into the identity would make every delivery's own claim look like somebody else's row.

**The lease decides LIVENESS; the generation binding decides SAFETY.** A lease must expire or a holder that crashed mid-sweep would hold the revocation forever—the media in the bucket, the row standing, and the Proof create arm's hold on that id standing with it—so `SWEEP_LEASE_TTL_MS` is **ten minutes**: an order of magnitude past the longest a holder can still be running, since the trigger takes the Gen2 event-driven default timeout of sixty seconds and the sweep inside it is one Firestore read, one transaction, one bucket metadata read, one bucket delete and one more transaction—and comfortably shorter than the hours `retry: true` keeps redelivering. The same number is the RECOVERY LATENCY after a failed sweep, which is the cost side of it: a holder that rejects leaves its lease behind, so the redelivery that actually gets to work is the first one past this window. Ten minutes of extra latency on a background revocation whose media has already left the Feed was judged a fair price for not writing a lease-release path that can itself fail; shortening it would only be safe down to the function's own timeout. It is measured in BOTH directions, so a stamp far in the future from a skewed clock expires too, and a lease the sweeper cannot read—only an Admin SDK writer could produce one—counts as no lease rather than an immortal one, which is the same safe direction the structural comparison took. Because a wall clock cannot be a correctness boundary on its own, the other half is that **no unbound delete exists anywhere**: every delete is bound to a generation, the row's own where it recorded one and otherwise the one read under the lease, so even an overtaken holder that somehow resumes can remove only the exact bytes it looked at. Retirement carries the lease too—it now requires the row to still be the same revocation AND to still carry this delivery's `leaseId`, so a holder whose lease aged out and was re-claimed cannot free the Proof id underneath the delivery that took over.

**The device-local queue child 1 shipped is RETIRED, not kept as a fast path.** `src/data/proofMediaRevocations.ts` recorded the same intent in `localStorage` before the transaction was sent, drained it on every sign-in, and verified the Proof document was gone before revoking—all of which the tombstone does strictly better: it is written inside the commit rather than speculatively ahead of it, so it needs no server read to tell a landed delete from an abandoned one; it is visible to every device rather than one; and it is discharged by an identity that bypasses `storage.rules` instead of one that can be denied. Keeping both would have meant carrying two live records of one obligation and fixing the two races the queue had—two tabs clobbering each other's read-modify-write ([Codex round 10 on PR #1157](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1153)), and a drain clearing an intent a still-running transaction had just re-asserted ([#1159](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1159))—for a record that no longer decides anything. Deleting the shared mutable state closes both races outright, which is why the module, its sign-in drain in `src/main.tsx` and its `localStorage` key are gone rather than serialized behind `navigator.locks`.

**A tombstone is written only when the transaction actually READ the Proof**, which is what keeps the new arm from becoming a new way for the takedown to fail—the failure class the reordering already closed once. A re-run after a committed delete finds no Proof, writes nothing, and behaves exactly as it did before this ticket: it retries the Storage delete inline and propagates a rejection, with the first attempt's tombstone still standing and the sweeper still owing the revocation. The client also mirrors both halves the rule checks before it writes—the path parses to a canonical object (only the FINAL extension stripped, so a dotted proof id such as `p.q` survives), and the uid it names is the Proof's own owner—so a hand-written document degrades to the pre-tombstone behaviour instead of failing the takedown. The rule's third condition needs no mirror: it IS the accompanying `tx.delete`, and the tombstone is never written without it.

**`attachProof` rolls its own upload back for the same reason.** The media is uploaded BEFORE the transaction and has to be—`firestore.rules` pins the Proof document's `storagePath`/`mediaURL` to the exact object, so there is nothing to write until the object exists—which means every rejection leaves a blob no document points at, and the freeze made one of those rejections routine: an Admin committing `archiving: true` between the upload resolving and the transaction reading denies the write, and the archive keeps the blob forever. So the object is deleted when the transaction refuses, authorised in every state by the orphan carve-out. Best effort, and the ORIGINAL error is what the caller sees: a cleanup that fails too must not replace "your proof did not post" with a Storage error the Player can do nothing with, and `ProofSheet` keeps the captured blob either way, so a retry re-uploads under a new proof id rather than depending on this object.

The archived skip and the closing refusal above are unaffected by any of it: both live INSIDE the transaction, where the freeze has to be read anyway, and both are decided before anything is written.

## Design decisions

- **Archiving is one-way from the client.** `status: 'archived'` is terminal: the rules refuse to move it back and refuse a second stamp. The alternative—a reversible toggle—hands an Admin a switch that reopens writes on an Event other people have already been told is over. Write-once is the same posture the day-meta honour doc and Moment immutability already take in this rules file. See § Recovery for the escape hatch.
- **The QUIESCE, by contrast, is reversible—and that asymmetry is the design.** `archiving` shuts gameplay without making anything permanent, so leaving it un-clearable would mean a failed second write could strand a live Event unplayable with nothing to show for it. Making it clearable costs nothing, because the moment the flip lands the freeze is carried by `status`, which is not.
- **Reversible therefore means IDENTIFIED, and identified means ORDERED.** The moment a quiesce can be lifted, two of them can exist in sequence inside one archive attempt, and a flag cannot tell an in-flight freeze that its window has been and gone. `archiveToken` is the cost of the reversibility: one integer field, incremented per quiesce, compared once in the transaction and once in the rules. It is a counter rather than an opaque id because the rules can only compare against the single value the document still carries, which distinguishes a generation from its immediate predecessor and from nothing else; a number that only ever goes up needs no memory to rule out every generation that came before. The alternative—an irreversible quiesce—buys the same guarantee by stranding Events.
- **A separate boolean, not a third `status` value.** `'closing'` as a status would have dropped the Event out of three server-side `status == 'active'` sweeps as a side effect, widened `EventDoc.status` (which `eventResolution.ts` deliberately does not share with `HostnameDoc.status`), and made the two states one field where the write-once rule must apply to only one of them. A field of its own is absent by default, needs no migration, and reads as exactly what it is.
- **The quiesce ships WITH the lifecycle, not with the snapshot it protects.** Nothing on this ticket reads a roster between two writes, so a reader could ask why the closing state is not deferred to #1151 along with the record. Because the quiesce is what makes the flip safe to take at all, and because shipping it later would mean shipping the flip twice—once without an ordering guarantee, and again with one, on rules a deployed estate is already running. The two snapshot-defining deletes are held for the same reason: their arms are the quiesce's contract, and #1151 relies on them rather than adding them.
- **Absence means OPEN, everywhere.** `exists()` guards and `'active'` / `false` defaults keep every Event document written before these fields existed playable. A missing status that read as archived would freeze the whole estate on deploy.
- **Admins are bound by the freeze for GAMEPLAY.** The Board and Player arms deny the admin branch too. An "archive" whose own organiser can still mark a square is not frozen.
- **The one exception is the WINDOW, not the archived state.** The `players/{uid}` and day-meta honour deletes are denied for the length of the QUIESCE and open again once the flip lands—the only moment at which a delete could silently edit a record nobody can rewrite.
- **The end-of-Event control lives inside Game settings**, not behind a seventh hub door. It is a once-per-Event switch, not a surface anyone visits; giving it its own card would put the most destructive control in the console at the same level as the prompt pool. The Archive action joins it in the same place rather than opening a door of its own.
- **The record SNAPSHOTS, it does not recompute** ([ADR 0001](../docs/adr/0001-honor-system-trust-model.md)). The alternative—re-deriving the standings server-side at the freeze, "to be sure"—would make the archive the one place in the product that adjudicates a Player-written stat, and it would produce a record that could disagree with the Leaderboard everyone had been watching for a week. The only things derived are the ORDER and the honour SELECTIONS, and both reuse the live selectors rather than restating them.
- **The record is BOUNDED rather than complete.** 200 rows and 100-character names against a 1 MiB document budget the archive shares with `days`, `bannedUids` and `mostLovedPhoto`. An unbounded copy is the one field a Player can make unwritable from their own row, and a permanent record that cannot be written is worse than one that keeps a prefix and states the true count beside it.
- **Every refusal is REPORTED, never thrown.** `claims-pending`, `config-changed`, `config-unreadable`, `finale-pending` and `too-large` each write nothing and each name what the Admin can do about it. A thrown error on the second write leaves an Event shut with no record and no explanation—the exact state the quiesce's reversibility exists to make recoverable, wasted.
- **The pre-finale check is an ACKNOWLEDGEMENT, not a bar.** The other three preconditions describe states the Admin can clear; this one describes a trade they may legitimately want to make. Blocking it would strand an Event that will never reach its finale, and defaulting through it silently would forgo the podium, the Most-Loved award and the freeze stamp with nothing anywhere saying so.

## What the freeze leaves open

Stated rather than assumed, because these children ship the lifecycle, its enforcement and the record, not the presentation:

- **An archived Event's Leaderboard still renders LIVE.** The record is written and locked, but nothing reads it yet: the rows cannot move—every write that would change them is denied—so what a returning Player sees is the standings as they stood, derived from the Player rows rather than read from `EventDoc.archive`. What that costs is durability against later moderation: an Admin deleting a Player row after the freeze removes that row from the LIVE standings, where the frozen record beside it keeps it. The archived presentation—the frozen podium, the hall of fame, the Share Card and the listener shutdown—is [#1152](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1152), and it is the reason the record ships first.
- **Reads are untouched.** An archived Event stays exactly as readable as it was live, to exactly the audience it was readable to. Who may read an archived Event, and for how long, is [#808](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/808)'s question; this ticket deliberately answers only the write half.
- **The server writers that keep running are the ones that SHOULD.** The gameplay writers are stopped—§ "The server-side half" is that half ([#1150](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1150))—but the moderation triggers, the admin-alert queue and the threshold sweep deliberately keep reaching a closed Event, each named with its reason in that section's audit table. A frozen Event that its own Admin can no longer moderate is [#808](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/808)'s support incident in a new place, so this is a choice rather than a gap.
- **The `apexPath` / routing half of `specs/path-addressing-and-root.md` § D8 does not ship here.** D8 requires the archival write to move every routing document, the `apexPath` field and `EventDoc.status` in ONE transaction. Path addressing ships nothing today (that spec says so itself), so there are no routing documents to move: the flip is one update on one document, which satisfies the ordering requirement vacuously and will have to be revisited when there is something to order it against.
- **The rules still leave a pending Claim resolvable ON PAPER.** The claims arm keeps `allow update: if isAdmin(eventId)` open on a frozen Event and `ReviewQueue` keeps offering Confirm and Reject—but `resolve()` writes the claimant's Board and Player row, and both are gameplay writes the freeze denies, so a Claim that somehow survived to the flip would be pending FOREVER behind a control pair that can only fail. `tests/rules/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` pins exactly that shape. The DRAIN GATE (§ above) is what makes it unreachable through the console, at both ends of the archive; the rules are deliberately not the place for it, because a rule cannot read the Claim collection from the Event document's arm.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Given** an admin shuts the Event, **when** any Player tries to Mark, Claim, Doubt, Heart, post, report or upload proof media, **then** the write is denied at the rules boundary, and the same write succeeded before the shut—`tests/rules/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` § "gameplay writes stop at the freeze", run against BOTH halves of the freeze, each case pairing a live success with a closed denial.
- **Given** a shut Event, **when** the admin reopens it, **then** gameplay writes succeed again and the generation token moves—`tests/rules/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` ("ALLOWS an admin to shut the Event, and to reopen it while the archive is uncommitted", "DENIES deleting a Player row or a Day honour while the Event is closing" — which reopens and re-checks) + `src/data/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` ("mints a NEW id for the next quiesce after an abandon").
- **Given** a caller bound to generation A, **when** the flip is attempted after play reopened and shut again as B, **then** the flip is denied—`src/data/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` ("ABORTS, and writes nothing, when play was reopened and shut again underneath it") + `tests/rules/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` ("DENIES an archive write bound to a superseded quiesce", with the in-force generation accepted as the control).
- **Given** an archived Event, **then** `status`, `archivedAt`, `archivedUnder` and the frozen record are locked, the Event document cannot be deleted from a client, and every admin moderation write still works—`tests/rules/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` ("LOCKS status, archivedAt and the frozen record once archived", "DENIES deleting the Event document itself" with the live-Event control beside it, "keeps Admin moderation available on an archived Event").
- **Given** a quiesced Event, **when** the admin archives it, **then** the document carries the COMPLETE record and the rules refuse a second stamp, a rewritten record and any un-archive—`tests/rules/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` § "the archive write must carry the whole record" (no record, an empty one, each missing key, each wrong type, the First-BINGO pair cases—mismatched, uid-only, each half's own missing and mistyped scalars, every non-finite number in either half, a `rank` that names no place in the standings, and a `rank` that overruns the roster the record declares (`playerCount: 0` beside an empty standings list and a pair ranked first, and a rank past a roster that does exist, against a holder ranked LAST and one ranked past the retained 200-row prefix)—the record's own two numbers held to their shapes (a `playerCount` that is non-finite, fractional or negative, a `freezeAt` that is non-finite or out of range, each against its own accepted control), the standings list bound to `playerCount` (a count that under- or over-counts its own list, and a list past the 200-row bound in either pairing, against a short roster whose count IS its list and a 200-row prefix of a 250-strong one), the honours list bound at `MAX_DAYS` (eleven entries refused, ten accepted), a disagreeing stamp, the complete control, the odd-but-writable counts and instants accepted beside it, a full-sized record, and a flip that smuggles configuration in beside it) + "DENIES a stray record on a LIVE Event through the general admin arm".
- **Given** a direct flip carrying more archived daily honours than an Event has Days, **then** the write is denied at the boundary and refused as `record-unwritable` before the quiesce, while an honour for every one of the ten Days goes through—and the client-side bound is stated rather than inferred, because a strictly ascending list of well-formed entries can be any length—`tests/rules/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` ("DENIES more archived honours than an Event has Days", "ALLOWS an honour for every Day the Event can have") + `src/data/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` ("bounds the frozen honours list at MAX_DAYS, which the ORDER does not imply").
- **Given** a direct flip whose First-BINGO row claims a place the roster it declares does not have—`rank: 1` beside `playerCount: 0`, or any rank past the count—**then** the write is denied at the boundary and refused as `record-unwritable` before the quiesce, while a holder ranked LAST and a holder ranked past the retained 200-row prefix both go through—`tests/rules/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` ("DENIES a First-BINGO rank that overruns the roster the record declares", "ALLOWS a holder ranked LAST, and one ranked past the retained prefix") + `src/data/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` ("binds the First-BINGO rank to the roster count, on both ends of the range", with a builder-produced record as the control).
- **Given** a Player row the rules permit but the record cannot serialise, **then** the attempt is refused before the Event is shut—`src/data/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` § "draftEventArchive — the inputs are validated BEFORE the Event is shut" + `src/components/post-sailing-archive.test.tsx` § "a record it could not store is refused before anything is shut".
- **Given** ANY roster the builder will carry—including one Player whose `dayStats` fill a row to Firestore's own document limit on an Event with no schedule—**then** the record stays under its OWN share of the budget, so the oversized-record copy's "banning a Player does not help" is true without qualification and the reachable ceiling is the projected DOCUMENT—`src/data/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` § "the record's own share cannot be filled" (the finding's fixture at ~4 KiB and the largest record the builder can produce at ~74 KiB, both against a 256 KiB share) + `src/components/post-sailing-archive.test.tsx` ("ARMS over a roster naming thousands of Days — the record ceiling is not that lever", "names the levers that actually move the size, and does not recommend a ban", "says WHICH ceiling refused it — the Event document, on an ordinary record").
- **Given** an Archive superseded by a reopen the Admin took while it was still in flight, **when** it finally resolves, **then** it neither disarms the confirmation panel it left on screen nor is credited with a phase change no commit produced—`src/components/post-sailing-archive.test.tsx` § "a superseded action keeps its hands off the surface (#1165)" ("does not close the confirmation panel from a SUPERSEDED action" with the current action closing it as the control, and "keeps a pending action's status when a render for another phase never commits", which drives a real discarded render through a suspended transition).

## Recovery

Un-archiving is not a client operation, on purpose. If an Event is archived by mistake, the fix is an Admin-SDK or console edit—the same escape hatch the write-once day-meta honour and the immutable Moment already rely on, and the same trust boundary the Cloud Functions Admin SDK already sits on (it bypasses these rules). That is a deliberate trade: a mis-click costs an operator intervention, and the alternative costs the guarantee the whole feature exists to make.

**Abandoning an archive that has not committed IS a client operation**, and that is a different thing. Until the flip lands there is nothing to un-do—only a shut Event, and shutting one is not the guarantee the write-once rule protects. `abandonArchive` is the Admin console's own way back, so the operator intervention above is reserved for the case that actually needs it.

## Residuals

- **A signed-in user can tell a missing Proof from one they may not read.** The Proof read arm answers a get of a NONEXISTENT document as not-found for any signed-in user (Codex P2, PR #1157): `resource.data.status` on a null resource errored into a denial, which the device-local revocation drain could not work with. That drain is gone with the queue ([#1153](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1153)), and the arm is kept as it stands rather than reverted—a missing document reading as not-found is the correct answer for a get, and re-narrowing it would be a behaviour change for every reader to buy back an oracle nobody can enumerate. Hidden and pending Proofs stay unreadable; what leaks is existence by id.
- **Archiving before the finale has run forgoes it permanently.** The beats are withheld, not deferred: a quiesce merely delays them, because it is reversible and every beat is self-guarded and idempotent, so a reopened Event takes its freeze stamp, its podium Moment and its Most-Loved award on the next quarter-hourly run—stamped at the SCHEDULED instants, never the reopened clock. `status: 'archived'` is not reversible, so an Event flipped before its own Standings Freeze never gets them. That is the correct reading of the freeze (a record cannot grow a podium after it is taken) and it is also a foot-gun: the Admin action that ends the Event is the same one that can end it too early. What bounds it is the pre-finale acknowledgement the drain gate now carries (§ "The pending-claim drain gate"), decided on `finaleCompletedAt` so a freeze stamp whose podium has not landed still asks. What it does NOT bound is an Admin who ticks the box: the acknowledgement states what will never arrive, and then the beats never arrive.
- **A guard refuses; it never retries.** Every stand-down returns normally—`'archived'` for the snapshot paths, `reason: 'archived'` for the mail, a plain return for the finale—so a Cloud Functions trigger is acknowledged rather than redelivered. That is deliberate: the freeze is a permanent property of the Event, so redelivering against it would loop to the platform's retention limit for an answer that cannot change.
- **A freeze landing mid-delivery still finishes the roster it had already started.** The daily email's second read is taken immediately before the FIRST send, not before each one, so an archive committing partway through a fan-out lets the remaining recipients receive the mail this run had already resolved. Re-reading the Event per recipient would put one document read on every message in the estate's largest loop to shorten a window measured in the pacing delay between two sends. The bound that holds is the one that matters: nothing those recipients are invited to do can be written.
- **A ban is the one thing that still changes what an archived Event shows.** The record itself is locked, and `ArchivedLeaderboard` renders it alone—but `bannedUids` stays editable on purpose, so a takedown after the freeze hides a stored row from the page and the Share Card while the record beside it keeps that row verbatim. That asymmetry is the point (§ "The surfaces"): moderation decides who is shown, never who won.
- **The rows and honours inside the record are not shape-checked at the BOUNDARY.** Rules cannot iterate a list, so `completeArchiveRecord` types the record's top-level keys, binds the standings list's SIZE to `playerCount` (a size is one expression; a walk is not expressible at all), and stops there — the same stated residual `bannedUids` carries. What closes the practical gap is that the only writer is `draftEventArchive`, which coerces every field of every row before it is offered, and which now asks the honours' own shape client-side (`writableDayHonor`) because a pinned holder's `uid` is the one value no arm validated. A direct Admin-SDK flip bypasses both and is bounded only by the top-level clauses.
- **The Day-meta arm's ADMIN branch still does not type-check `firstBingo.uid`.** `firestore.rules`' `meta/{metaId}` create validates `displayName` and `at` on every branch and binds `uid` to `request.auth.uid` only on the Player one, so an admin-written pin can carry a non-string holder — which is what the archive writer now discards rather than freezing. Tightening that arm is deliberately OUT of scope for this child: it is a live gameplay rule shared with the honours strip and the podium, the write is write-once so an existing pin cannot be repaired by tightening the create, and the archive's own promise is that it survives whatever the boundary admits. Worth its own ticket alongside [#1152](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1152).
- **The record keeps a bounded PREFIX of a very large roster.** 200 rows, with `playerCount` recording the true cardinality beside them. Both live Events are two figures, so nothing is dropped in practice, but an Event that outgrew the bound would freeze a record the archived surfaces have to describe as partial rather than complete.
- **The console's preview is built during RENDER, from the live subscriptions.** It is the same builder the write uses, so it cannot drift in its RULES — but it is a different roster from the one `archiveEvent` re-reads after the close, which is why the size refusal is checked twice and why the confirm row's counts are a preview rather than a promise.
- **The pre-finale acknowledgement is per-render, not persisted.** Cancelling the confirm row clears it, and an Admin who arms, cancels and re-arms is asked again. That is deliberate for an irreversible action, and it means the checkbox is not a setting an Event can be left in.
- **The proof-media delete arm can spend the whole Storage budget.** The Proof `exists()` plus the Event `get()` is two accesses on the one path that reaches both—a NON-OWNER clearing an orphan, which is an Admin taking a reported photo down or a stranger being refused one (the path was an owner deleting media a live Proof still points at until Codex round 5 P2 made `proofDocMissing` the whole arm's condition, which now denies that in one)—which is the cap, so [#806](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/806)'s membership check has no room left on that arm: it must thread its admission through the SAME Event fetch, relying on error-to-deny exactly as its own membership `get()` already does (`specs/event-membership.md` § "Storage has zero headroom"). Named here because this ticket is what spent the second access.
- **The same is true of an `attachProof` upload whose rollback also fails.** The cleanup is deliberately best-effort so it cannot mask the error the Player actually needs, and it is not queued: unlike a delete, nothing has committed, so there is no record to attach the retry to and the Player's own retry re-uploads under a new proof id. The leftover blob is an orphan by construction, and [#1153](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1153)'s sweeper is what collects it.
- **A revocation outlives the platform's retry budget as a row, not as a retry.** `retry: true` redelivers `revokeDeletedProofMedia` for as long as Cloud Functions is willing to (a bounded window, days rather than forever), and nothing re-fires a create trigger afterwards. A Storage object that is unreachable for longer than that therefore stays, with its tombstone standing as the durable statement that it was owed—which is what an operator needs to find it, and is strictly more than the device-local queue left behind. A periodic sweep of the collection is the obvious next step and is deliberately not built on a hypothetical.
- **A superseded delivery can no longer retire a newer row—the residual the `.exists` recheck left is CLOSED** (Codex round 3 P2). The round-2 recheck asked only whether a row was standing, which answers yes to a SECOND revocation the freed Proof id produced after the first was retired: a very late delivery of the FIRST create would then delete with the first row's generation, take `412` against the new object and retire that second row, leaving a real revocation undone with its media in place. It was accepted at the time on the grounds that reaching it needs the id re-created, which `attachProof` never does, and that an operation identity would mean a second field and a second rules clause. It needs neither. The row already carries its identity in the three required keys plus the optional generation, and it is CREATE-ONLY and update-denied, so those fields cannot change while it stands—the standing row is simply compared against the event snapshot's own (`isSameRevocation`) and a mismatch abandons the delivery with that row left where it is. What remains is the ordinary shape of an abandon: nothing is deleted and nothing is retired, and the standing row's OWN delivery is still owed.
- **A tombstone whose fields carry a value `sameFirestoreValue` cannot compare is still un-retirable** (Codex round 5 P2, PR #1163). The structural comparison covers everything a Firestore read can produce—primitives, arrays, maps, bytes, and the Admin SDK's own `Timestamp`, `GeoPoint` and `DocumentReference` through their published `isEqual`—so no reachable row is left stuck. Anything outside that set compares unequal unless it is literally the same object, which means a future SDK value type with no `isEqual` would reintroduce the immortal poison row the round-5 finding described. That is deliberate: the alternative is a comparison that guesses, and a false positive here retires somebody else's revocation with its media still in the bucket. The bound is that such a row can only be written by an Admin SDK caller, and the same caller can delete it.
- **A tombstone planted OUT OF BAND could still block one Proof's takedown.** The create arm admits a row only alongside its own Proof's deletion and the Proof create arm refuses that id back while the row stands, so no client can leave one standing over a live Proof—but the Admin SDK bypasses the rules, so a console or script write could. `deleteProof`'s `set` would then be an update the rules deny, and that one Proof's takedown would fail until the row is removed, which the admin delete arm allows in every state. Closing it in the rules would mean admitting an idempotent same-target update, which re-opens the sweeper-strips-live-media half of [#1147](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1147); the trade was taken deliberately. What such a row can no longer do is cost the media: the sweeper reads the Proof and abandons the revocation when one is there, so the failure is a blocked takedown an operator can clear, never a stripped Feed entry.
- **A revocation that carries no generation is bound at SWEEP time instead—it is never revoked by bare path** (Codex round 6 P2, PR #1163, withdrawing the residual this bullet used to accept). The client's metadata read is best effort—it must be, or a HEAD request could deny the write inside the takedown's own transaction—so a row can legitimately arrive without the binding, as can a row written before this existed. Such a row used to be swept by name, and the reasoning that made that acceptable ("no Proof document can exist for that id while the row stands, so what a sweep can take is an orphan blob") turned out to be false in one reachable ordering: a duplicate delivery can retire the row, freeing the id, and a re-post uploads its media BEFORE creating its Proof, so a stale delivery resuming into an unconditioned delete takes a live entry's bytes. So the sweeper now reads the object's generation under its own lease and binds the delete to that, and the lease is what closes the ordering above—A's claim finds no row of its own and abandons before it reaches the bucket at all. What remains is much smaller and is the ORIGINAL residual, correctly bounded: such a row binds against the object as it stands at SWEEP time rather than at DELETE time, so a blob re-uploaded to that name in between is still taken. It can only ever be an orphan, and for a structural reason rather than a hopeful one—the Proof create arm holds that id for exactly as long as the row stands, so no Feed entry can point at it, and the one window where the id IS free is the window the row is gone and the claim abandons in. The cost is one extra Storage round trip on the server, paid only by a row whose client-side metadata read failed.
- **Every proof-media takedown now spends one extra Storage round trip.** `deleteProof` reads the object's metadata before it opens the transaction, and only when a tombstone is actually possible—a text Proof and an unparseable path spend nothing. It is a metadata read on a rare, deliberate, single-item operator or owner action, taken so that a sweep hours later cannot delete the wrong bytes; caching it on the Proof document at upload time would have been free at delete time but would have put a server-set field on a client-created document for the sake of one path.
- **The Proof create arm spends two of the twenty document accesses a request gets with the membership switch off, four with it on, and it used to spend three.** `eventOpenForPlay` is `exists()` plus `get()` on the Event and the revocation check is one more `exists()`, which was three while the arm's leading predicate was a bare `signedIn()`—measured then at six distinct-Event creates passing and seven denying. #804 made the leading predicate `admitted(eventId)`, a `get()` on that same Event document, and the arm got CHEAPER rather than dearer: measured in the emulator, the Event document now costs one access instead of two. Firestore documents only that repeated document access calls on one path within a request may be cached, not which of `get()` and `exists()` seeds that cache for the other, so the pin records the measured count and claims no mechanism. Re-measured in the emulator at ten distinct-Event creates passing and eleven denying (`tests/rules/post-sailing-archive.test.ts` § "pins the Proof create arm's access budget"), which is the UNENFORCED figure: those Events carry no `membershipEnforcement`, so admission returns through the switch-off branch. Under `enforced`, with an active membership for the writer, the same arm spends four accesses per Event, because the membership check is an `exists()` followed by a `get()` on the membership record and the emulator counts them separately: five distinct-Event creates pass and six deny (§ "access budget UNDER ENFORCEMENT"). A future clause on this arm has two accesses of headroom under enforcement, not four, and must re-run both probes. A real capture creates one Proof in one Event and shares those Event reads with the Board, Player and Tally arms beside it, so the shape that matters has room; a future clause on this arm should re-run that probe rather than assume it.
- **Retiring the device-local queue abandons whatever it still held.** An entry written by the child-1 build survives in `localStorage` and is now read by nothing, so a blob queued there and never drained stays. The window is the interval between child 1 shipping and this change, and only for a device whose Storage delete actually failed in it; the blobs are orphans by construction and `storage.rules` lets their owner clear them, but nothing will do it automatically. A one-shot cleanup was judged more code than the exposure is worth.
- **An upload whose response is LOST now fails instead of healing itself.** `uploadBytes` retries a request that never answered, and before proof objects were immutable a retry whose first attempt had actually landed simply overwrote it and succeeded. It is now a denied overwrite, so the capture surfaces as a failed post the Player has to retry—which they can, because `ProofSheet` keeps the blob and the retry mints a NEW proof id—and the landed object becomes orphan litter of exactly the kind the residual below describes. The exposure is one lost response on a ~100–300 KB upload, against a hole where the media's owner could desynchronise a takedown's tombstone from the object it was written about, and the alternative that would preserve the self-heal is a rules clause distinguishing an overwrite that precedes a tombstone from one that follows it—a `firestore.exists` this arm has no access budget for.
- **An upload to a path whose revocation is still pending leaves a blob nothing collects** (Codex round 4 P2, PR #1163). The UPLOAD arm does not ask whether a `proofStorageDeletes` row stands for the id, so a client that re-uploads under a Proof id whose revocation has not been swept lands an object. Nothing reaches it: `firestore.rules` refuses the Proof create while the row stands, so no Feed entry can point at it, and a generation-bound sweep takes the OLD blob and leaves the new one. It is an orphan by construction, which its owner may clear in every state. Closing it in the rules would cost a third `firestore.exists` on an arm whose two-access budget `eventClosedToPlay` already spends—the same wall the residual above runs into—and the app does not produce the shape anyway, because `attachProof` mints a fresh auto-id per capture and never re-posts under an id it has used. The Storage EMULATOR is no help in measuring the trade: it enforces no access cap at all, admitting an arm that makes eighteen distinct Firestore accesses, so a green emulator run would say nothing about production.
- **An `attachProof` upload whose rollback also fails leaves a blob nothing collects.** The cleanup is deliberately best-effort so it cannot mask the error the Player actually needs, and it gets no tombstone: unlike a delete, nothing has committed and no Proof document ever existed, so there is nothing for a row to be bound to and the sweeper's own confinement has no Proof id to confine against. The Player's retry re-uploads under a new proof id. The leftover blob is an orphan by construction, so `storage.rules` lets its owner clear it in every state, and it is unreachable through every surface meanwhile.
- **A pending revocation is held until the SWEEPER discharges it, and no browser can shorten that.** The retirement arm used to be open to the admin and the named owner, on the reasoning that clearing one early could only leave behind an orphan its own deleter was already entitled to remove. That reasoning missed the ADMIN takedown: the row is the only record still owing the revocation once the Admin's inline Storage delete fails, and the media's OWNER could delete it blind at a wholly predictable path, after which the sweeper finds nothing, abandons by design, and the reported photo stays reachable. Retirement is therefore server-only (Codex round 3 P1). The cost is the one the old arm was avoiding: a row nobody clears would be swept forever—except that the sweeper clears it, counting the object's absence as success, so the only case left standing is a delivery that could not reach Firestore or the bucket at all, which `retry: true` brings back.
- **The deleting client never learns the row's fate, because it no longer has any part in it.** `deleteProof` resolves as soon as the media is actually revoked and issues no retirement at all—the earlier shape detached that `deleteDoc` so a call that never settled could not hang the takedown; removing it closes the question. Every row therefore stands until a sweeper delivery finds the object already gone, counts that as success and retires it, and until then it holds that Proof id against re-creation, which nothing can notice because `attachProof` mints a fresh auto-id per capture. The window is a trigger delivery rather than a client round trip, and the only thing inside it is an id nothing asks for.
- **A closing state shut by a build older than the `archiveToken` counter cannot be flipped directly.** The flip requires the stored quiesce to carry a usable generation—a positive integer—and `archiveEvent` refuses to bind to one that does not, so an Event left mid-archive across this change (carrying nothing there, or the opaque string an earlier build wrote) reports `quiesce-changed`. The way through is the one control the console does offer: **Reopen play**, after which the next `beginArchive` mints a generation; the repair arm also admits `beginArchive`'s in-place mint, which starts the counter above whatever number the document carries. A state that only exists if an Admin shut an Event on a previous build and never came back.
- **An Event can be left CLOSING indefinitely.** Nothing expires the quiesce: if an Admin shuts an Event and never returns, gameplay stays denied until someone reopens or archives it. A timeout would be worse—it would silently reopen an Event mid-archive, which is precisely the state the protocol exists to make impossible. The bound is the console: the closing state is stated wherever an Admin would look for it, with both exits one tap away. [#1142](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1142) item 2 asks whether an operator-facing reminder is wanted.
- **A takedown of a Proof that backs a MARKED square needs play reopened first, for as long as the quiesce holds.** That is the deliberate cost of refusing rather than skipping (§ "Moderation is not a gameplay write"): the alternative is a silent success that leaves a marked, credited square behind a Proof that no longer exists, waiting for the reopen to make it live again. The remedy is two taps in the same console—**Reopen play**, delete, close again—and it applies only to the reversible half of the freeze and only to a Proof that still backs its cell. On an ARCHIVED Event, and for a Proof that backs nothing, the delete is unconditional. What this does mean is that the moderation path is not fully independent of the lifecycle while an Event sits closed, so an Event left closing indefinitely (above) holds those takedowns with it.
- **The quiesce protects the ordering, not the Admin's own timing.** A Mark that lands one millisecond before the closing write is in; one that lands after is denied. That boundary is the point rather than a defect—the alternative is a boundary that exists nowhere, which is what this replaces.
- **An Event that closes mid-join still denies the write.** `joinAndDeal` decides on the Event read it already takes, and the freeze can land between that read and the transaction—so the identity merge is denied at the boundary and `runDeal` reports it, exactly as it does for every other gameplay write that straddles the quiesce. A CACHED closed snapshot deliberately takes the same route, because the alternative is a silent no-op on a quiesce that may already have been lifted. The window is one round trip on the visit's first startup, the remedy is the reload the retry surface already offers, and the SECOND startup routes to the standings.
