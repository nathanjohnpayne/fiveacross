# Player blocking: reciprocal, per Event, rules-enforced on the records and on Hearts, client-hidden on every shared read

A Player blocks another Player, and from then on neither sees the other in this Event. The block is reciprocal, per Event, stored per Player, self-serviceable, and reversible only by the Player who made it. It is a visibility control, never a scoring one (ADR 0001, ADR 0011), and it is the App Store and Google Play precondition for an application with a shared user-generated feed ([#689](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/689)). Ships in three parts, in order and not stacked: this spec's records, rules and data provider; then client-side hiding across every shared surface; then the block and unblock UI. Decisions are the owner's of 2026-09-24, recorded on #689 and in ADR 0016. Guarded by `tests/rules/player-blocking.test.ts` (every rules arm, both enforcement postures), `src/data/blocks.test.ts` (the batches and the pure derivations) and `src/hooks/useBlocks.test.tsx` (the provider), for the client-side hiding by `src/hooks/player-blocking-hiding.test.tsx`, `src/components/player-blocking-feed.test.tsx` and `src/components/player-blocking-standings.test.tsx`, and for the controls and analytics by `src/components/player-blocking-controls.test.tsx`.

## Glossary

**Block**: a Player's per-Event, reciprocal, self-serviceable hide of another Player. Display-only on shared reads, enforced by rules on the block records and on Hearts. Distinct from a **Ban** (Admin-issued, Event-wide hide of one Player's content, `EventDoc.bannedUids`) and from **Membership revocation** (removes admission). *Avoid:* mute, ban, ignore.

**Direction record**: `events/{eventId}/blocks/{ownerUid}_{targetUid}`, the blocker's own list entry. **Pair record**: `events/{eventId}/blockPairs/{lo}_{hi}`, the direction-free fact that a block stands between two Players. **Hidden set**: the uids a viewer hides and is hidden from, derived by that viewer's client from the pair records naming them.

## The enforcement bar, stated first

**Rules enforce the block records and a Heart aimed across a block. Every shared read is hidden client-side.** This is enforcement bar A, ruled by the owner on 2026-09-24. Firestore rules are not filters: a `list` is admitted or denied as a whole, so a per-document predicate on the author uid of the proofs, moments, markers, doubts or hearts list arms would deny every reader's whole listen (the #294/#314 "intermittent" failure), and every client listener today is a whole-collection or status/itemId/eventId query. Two rules-side alternatives were examined and rejected: a block conjunct that bites only on point gets (any document a blocked Player can list they can also read through a list, so it would spend budget to look like enforcement without being any), and query-constraint enforcement through `where(uid, 'not-in', hidden)` (`not-in` is capped at 10 values and a reader's hidden set includes everyone who blocked them, which other Players control, so eleven blockers would make one Player's Feed unqueryable: a griefing vector).

**Accepted residual, recorded here and in ADR 0016.** A blocked Player who queries Firestore directly with their own credentials still receives the other Player's proofs, moments, tally markers, doubts and hearts, and can fetch their avatar and proof media. The app never shows them. Closing this for reads needs reader-scoped, server-built projections for each surface, which is a separate epic the owner has not commissioned. #689's acceptance line "enforcement holds against a direct Firestore read" is therefore met for the block records and for Hearts, and deliberately not for the list reads.

## Data model

Two records per block, both under the Event, both at ids computable from the two uids so no rule and no client ever needs a query to find them. `BlockDoc` and `BlockPairDoc` live in `src/domainTypes.d.ts`; the refs in `src/data/paths.ts` (`blockRef`, `blockPairRef`, `blocksCol`, `blockPairsCol`, `blockPairId`).

- **Direction record** `blocks/{ownerUid}_{targetUid}` = `{ ownerUid, targetUid, eventId, createdAt }`. Readable by its owner alone (get and list; the one client query is `where('ownerUid', '==', me)`). No Admin read or delete path.
- **Pair record** `blockPairs/{lo}_{hi}` (lo < hi in string order) = `{ uids: [lo, hi], eventId }`. No direction, no timestamp, no names. Readable by either party (get, and list by `where('uids', 'array-contains', me)`); a third Player and an Admin cannot read it. The Hearts rule checks it with one `exists()`.

`_` joins both ids, as it does the doubts and hearts slot ids: it appears in no Firebase-minted uid, and the direction create arm refuses a `targetUid` containing one so the ordered pair id cannot collide across two pairs. Rejected shapes: a single pair document carrying a `blockers` field (rules cannot redact fields, so it would leak direction to the blocked Player); mirrored per-Player hidden arrays (needs a server fan-out through `functions/**`). Neither collection has a `{path=**}` rule, so a collectionGroup listen is denied.

**Invariant I: the pair record exists if and only if at least one direction record exists.** Every client write is one atomic commit (a `writeBatch` to block, a server-only `runTransaction` to unblock), and every rules arm checks the commit's result with `existsAfter`, so no single commit can break it. Concurrent commits can still leave one of two states the rules cannot rule out, because each commit is authorized against the state before the other: a pair with no direction (two concurrent direction-only unblocks, each authorized while the other direction still stood), which the pair delete arm lets either party remove, `unblockPlayer` tries to at once and `reconcileOrphanPair` removes durably; and a direction with no pair (a block batch racing a pair delete that was authorized before the direction existed), which the pair create arm lets the direction's owner restore and `repairMissingPairs` restores durably. Reading the pair inside the deleting transaction would not serialize the second race: the block's pair write is content-identical, and Firestore does not advance a document's update time for a write that changes nothing. Both reconcilers run from the provider: the orphan delete once per pair per app session and again whenever that pair reappears within a subscription, the missing-pair repair once per subscription, after each reconnection and whenever a pair disappears, with a bounded retry timer after a failure (see Data layer). The arms:

- Direction create: `ownerUid == auth.uid`, a non-empty `targetUid` that is not the caller, is not the reserved `system` author of server-written Moments (a pair naming it would deny the blocker's Hearts on every one of them), and contains no `_`, `eventId` equal to the path, the id bound to `auth.uid + '_' + targetUid`, `createdAt` a number no more than 60s ahead of server time with deliberately no lower bound (unlike the shared −24h window: a block queued offline for longer than a day must still land when the device reconnects, per ADR 0006, rather than roll back unseen, and a back-dated stamp only orders the owner's own list), and `existsAfter(pair)`.
- Direction update: only an idempotent re-block. `affectedKeys().hasOnly(['createdAt'])`, `createdAt` under the create arm's clock bound, and `existsAfter(pair)`. This is what lets `blockPlayer` be a blind set that never fails "already blocked" when the own-blocks listener has not loaded or another device blocked first.
- Direction delete: owner only, and `existsAfter(pair) == existsAfter(the other direction)`.
- Pair create/update: `auth.uid in uids`, `uids == [lo, hi]` matching the id, keys exactly `{uids, eventId}`, `eventId` equal to the path, and `existsAfter(the caller's own direction record)`. The content is deterministic, so a re-set is a no-op update.
- Pair delete: `auth.uid in uids`, and neither direction `existsAfter`.

Every arm conjoins `admitted(eventId)`, consistent with `specs/event-membership.md` § The enforced-path inventory; a blocked Player remains a member, and the block predicate sits beside `admitted()`, never inside it. There is deliberately no `eventOpenForPlay` conjunct: blocking is a safety action and stays available on a closing or archived Event. The target need not be a member or a Player (checking would spend a `get()` and buy nothing); self-block is denied; a Player may block an Admin (decision 4), which is a social hide only.

**Rules `get()` and `exists()` read documents regardless of their read rules.** The blocked Player cannot read the direction record that names them, yet their Heart on the blocker's post is still denied. `tests/rules/player-blocking.test.ts` pins this.

## Client write flows (`src/data/blocks.ts`)

`blockPlayer({ me, target, eventId })` commits `{ set direction, set pair }`. Idempotent under the arms above. `unblockPlayer({ me, target, eventId })` sends every attempt as a server-only commit (a `runTransaction` with no reads, which the SDK never applies to the local cache before the server accepts it, unlike a `writeBatch`), so an unblock needs a connection and offline rejects instead of queueing. It first tries `{ delete direction, delete pair }`; on `permission-denied`, which the rules issue when the other direction still stands (or when the pair is already missing), it retries `{ delete direction }`, then makes one best-effort `{ delete pair }`; when that is denied (the ordinary mutual case: the other direction still stands) or fails, it reports whether the pair still stands, as described below. If the direction-only retry is itself denied, the other party unblocked between the two attempts, so the rules now require the pair to leave with the caller's direction: it retries `{ delete direction, delete pair }` once and returns `{ stillHidden: false }`. Any other error, and any error on that last full batch, rethrows. The best-effort pair delete covers a truly concurrent mutual unblock: both first attempts are denied, and if both direction-only retries are evaluated while the other direction still stands, both can land and leave the pair with no direction. The pair delete arm lets either party remove a pair once neither direction exists, and whichever retry commits last runs its cleanup after both directions are gone, so the orphan is removed and that caller gets `{ stillHidden: false }`. If that cleanup is denied or fails, `unblockPlayer` lists the caller's pairs from the server and reports `stillHidden` as whether the pair is among them, because a missing pair is denied too (a direction that had lost its pair to a concurrent delete). It lists rather than gets: the pair read arm tests the caller against `resource.data.uids`, so a direct get of a missing pair is denied exactly like its delete, while the caller's own `array-contains` listing is allowed whatever it contains. An unreadable answer reports `true`, so `stillHidden: true` is conservative: the pair still stood, or its absence could not be confirmed. The earlier caller in a concurrent unblock may still be told `stillHidden: true`; the provider reads the committed pair, so what renders is correct either way. That cleanup is best-effort, so if both parties' cleanups fail (a dropped connection, a lost acknowledgement) the orphan would outlive them; `reconcileOrphanPair` is the durable half. The provider offers every server-confirmed pair to it once per app session, and again whenever a pair appears after a subscription's first server answer (a new block, or a repair write that landed after an unblock and recreated an orphan), as one server-only `{ delete pair }`: the rules allow it only when neither direction exists, so an ordinary pair is simply denied and nothing changes on the device, while an orphan is removed by the next session of either party. The cost is one denied write per pair per session, plus one each time a pair reappears within a subscription. `repairMissingPairs` is the mirror for a direction that lost its pair to a concurrent delete: on the first server-confirmed pair snapshot of each subscription (so a pair lost while the viewer was signed out or in another Event is caught on return), on the first server-confirmed snapshot after any cache snapshot (a reconnection, when a pair created and deleted during the outage never shows as disappearing), and again on the first server-confirmed snapshot after any snapshot (pending, cache or settled) from which a pair disappeared, so a loss seen only on a pending snapshot is still owed a repair (the only way that race shows on the listener; an ordinary unblock also triggers it, at the cost of one listing), it lists the caller's own directions from the server and re-sets, server-only, the pair for every target missing from that snapshot. The pair arm allows it only while the caller's direction exists and the content is deterministic, so a stale view costs at most a no-op write; an offline listing or a transient re-set failure rejects and is retried on the next server snapshot and, while the listener stays server-backed, on a doubling timer (from 5s, at most five retries), because a failed write produces no snapshot to wait for. Accepted residual: until that repair commits (one server listing and one transaction after the first server snapshot), a direction that lost its pair to the race does not hide on either side. Readiness is deliberately not held for the repair, because that would add a server round trip to every cold start for a state only a concurrent block and delete can produce. A pair confirmed still standing after the caller's direction left is how the unblocker can infer the block was mutual, but `stillHidden: true` alone is not proof: an orphaned pair or an unanswered follow-up listing reports the same. Reciprocity makes that disclosure inherent, and the unblock copy (§ Block and unblock controls) says so without overclaiming. Both capture the acted Event once, as `setHeart` does, so a hostname change cannot move the write.

## Direction disclosure is inherent, not a solved requirement

#689 asks that the block list not be readable by the blocked Player. The direction records are owner-only, so the list itself is not. But the blocked Player's client must learn the blocker's uid in order to hide them reciprocally, so it receives every pair record naming them, and a pair whose counterpart is not among the viewer's own direction records identifies someone who blocked them. No client-side reciprocal design avoids this; the only rules-level alternative is the same projection epic as the read residual. Accepted by the owner. The block confirmation copy does not overclaim: "They won't get a notification, but their app will stop showing you, so they may be able to tell." (§ Block and unblock controls).

## Per-path enforcement

| Path | Mechanism | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| `blocks`, `blockPairs` | Rules (this spec) | The pair is readable by both parties (direction disclosure above) |
| Hearts create and update | Rules: `heartTargetAccepts` reads the target once for the incarnation stamp and adds `!exists(pair)` on its author | Only inside the two concurrent-commit states (Invariant I): a direction that lost its pair allows a Heart until `repairMissingPairs` restores the pair, and an orphaned pair denies one until `reconcileOrphanPair` removes it. The predicate deliberately follows the pair, the same record that drives hiding, so enforcement and hiding answer from one record; the rules read the server's pair, and a client's hidden set can lag it until that client's pair snapshot arrives. A server-authored Moment (`uid: 'system'`) never names a pair |
| Doubt create | Unchanged, open by decision 5 | A blocked Player can raise a Doubt on the blocker through a direct write; the blocker's app never shows it while third parties' do |
| Feed, proofs, moments, tally markers, who-lists, doubts, hearts counts | Client-hidden (part 2) through `useHiddenUids()` | Direct list reads return everything |
| Leaderboard rows, podium, last-call line, share cards, archived standings | Client-hidden at render, after ranks are computed (part 2); rank gaps kept, podium entries withheld without promotion (decision 6) | Rank gaps and server-aggregate counts reveal presence |
| Most-Loved award, `draftEventArchive`, Functions aggregators, the tally aggregate, Day snapshots | Stay raw: server-parity mirrors must never receive a hidden set | Hiding is display-only and retroactive at render |
| Avatars, `users/{uid}`, proof media and its download URLs | Display-only (decision 3): D9 in `specs/event-membership.md` keeps them globally readable, Storage has no spare access budget, and a minted download URL is a bearer capability | Reachable by a direct read |
| Community Prompts and `suggestedBy` | Not filtered (filtering would change dealing) | Attribution visible |
| Admin console, moderation surfaces, Notices, the banned roster | Never filtered (decision 4): moderation reach is unchanged, a Notice is never hidden | None |
| Old installed clients | Nothing until they update | They never write blocks and do not hide |

**Rules budget deltas.** Hearts create/update: the existing target `get()` is hoisted into `heartTargetData` and the pair `exists()` rides beside it, so in the accounting of `specs/event-membership.md` § The rules-evaluation budget the arm's own reads go from one to two and its worst enforced case from 4 / 3 to 5 / 4 (textual / distinct), well inside the ten-per-operation limit; the incarnation-stamp check is byte-equivalent to the inline pair of reads it replaces, and `tests/rules/player-blocking.test.ts` lands a Heart under `'enforced'` with no pair present. Doubts and the Mark batch are unchanged, and the Mark budget pins in `tests/rules/membership-mark-batch-budget.test.ts` stay green unmodified. The new arms spend at most the admission read plus two `existsAfter` calls.

## Doubts and ADR 0001

A Doubt is the honor-system verification channel. If a block denied Doubt creation, a Player who blocked every co-member would become un-doubtable, because blocking is a few taps each with no cap. #689 asks only that doubts not be *seen* across a block. So Doubt create stays open in the rules (decision 5); part 2 hides the Doubt button across a block and hides the pair's doubts from both parties while third parties still see them. ADR 0016 states the interaction and ADR 0001 points at it. `raiseDoubt` keeps its benign permission-denied branch unchanged.

## The hidden-set provider (`src/hooks/useBlocks.tsx`)

A leaf module that imports nothing from `useData` or `AuthContext`, so it can be mounted in the app shell without an import cycle and the suites that close-mock `../hooks/useData` keep working unmodified. `HiddenUidsProvider({ uid, enabled })` owns one `onSnapshot` with `includeMetadataChanges` on the array-contains pair query, keyed on `eventScopeKey(EVENT_ID, 'block-pairs', uid)` so an Event or account switch drops the old set first. `useHiddenUids()` returns `{ hidden, ready }`; the context default is `{ hidden: ∅, ready: true }`, so a tree without the provider renders exactly as today. `useMyBlocks(uid)` is the ownerUid-equality listener the Blocked players panel reads, with an `error` flag so a failed listener is never shown as an empty list. `AuthContext.tsx` mounts the provider around `children`, inside the attestation gate, with `uid` from the signed-in user and `enabled` following `admissionAllowsEventWatchers`, the same admission gate `App` requires before it renders any Event content: every admitted viewer gets a listener whose first answer makes the content hooks ready, so no content hook waits on a provider that will never answer. The shell-level watchers (`ConfirmWinMoments`, `RetractWinMoments`, `PoolRecoveryWatcher`) sit outside it and read the context default, which is right: they read the raw roster and never render another Player's content.

**Readiness.** Signed out: ready and empty, no listener. Signed in and not enabled: not ready, no listener, so a late admission flip cannot render a counterpart before the first pair snapshot lands; with no listener the answer is derived from the uid on the same render, so a sign-in before admission is never ready for even one render. Signed in and enabled: ready on the first snapshot, cache or server. Content hooks (part 2) report loading until ready, so a blocked Player never flashes in before the pair listener's first answer on a cold start. Readiness is deliberately not gated on a server-backed snapshot: ADR 0006 makes the app usable offline from the persistent cache, and a server-gated provider would hold every content hook in loading for as long as the ship's wifi is down. The accepted residual is the staleness ADR 0006 already accepts for every read: a returning device whose cached pair query predates a block made elsewhere (the other party's, or the viewer's own on another device) renders from that stale cache until the server snapshot arrives, and then hides the counterpart.

**The optimistic-unblock flash.** In the mutual case the first unblock attempt is denied. Had it been an optimistic local delete, the pair would have left the viewer's query at once, and the SDK derives a query snapshot's `hasPendingWrites` from the documents still in the result, so that empty snapshot would read as settled and reveal the counterpart until the server's denial rolled it back, indefinitely while offline. That is why `unblockPlayer` never deletes locally: its server-only commits mean a pair leaves the query only once the server has accepted the delete, and a denied attempt changes nothing on the device. Blocking stays an optimistic batch on purpose, so a block made offline hides at once and queues durably (ADR 0006); the asymmetry is deliberate, since hiding early is safe and revealing early is not. As defence in depth the provider also keeps `lastCommitted`, the set from the latest snapshot with `hasPendingWrites == false`, and publishes `current ∪ (hasPendingWrites ? lastCommitted : ∅)` (`computeHiddenSet`, pure and unit-tested), so no snapshot carrying a pending write ever reveals anyone.

**Fail mode.** If the pair listener errors, the provider logs `console.error` and resolves ready with the last set it published, never blank: a listener that fails before its first answer renders unfiltered (the same admission failure would deny the content listeners too), while one that had already answered keeps its last hidden set until a remount or reload resubscribes. The listener uses an explicit error callback rather than `useColSub`, which swallows errors.

## Where hiding applies

Every rule below is display-only and per viewer. It reads the viewer's hidden set through `useHiddenUids()` and the pure helpers in `src/data/moderation.ts`: `isHiddenFor(uid, hidden)`; `withBlockExclusions(bannedUids, hidden)`, the ban roster plus the hidden set, which returns the ban roster itself when nothing is hidden; and `withRanksKeepingGaps(rows, hidden)`, which numbers rows first and drops hidden ones after.

- **Content hooks** (`src/hooks/useData.ts`). `useProofFeed`, `useMoments`, `useTallyCards`, `useTally`, `useProofsForItemText`, `useAllDoubts` and `useDoubts` drop the hidden set's content beside the ban filter. Until the set is `ready` each reports loading and returns no rows. The Feed, the Tally Cards and their names line, the who-list sheets on the Feed and the Board, the Square's Tally badge, the proof pills and the Leaderboard's proof chips all read these hooks, so a counterpart's Proof, Moment, Mark and avatar never reach them. Tally Card positions are derived after the filter, and the bump history restarts whenever the hidden set changes, so a hidden Mark never bumps a card or keeps a position it earned before the block. While the Tally Card listener resubscribes on a new hidden set, the cards already in hand are served with the new set's Marks removed rather than blanked, so the Feed does not drop to its loading state. A who-list sheet open on the Feed re-reads the live card, and when a block removes the card it scrubs its tap-time snapshot against the hidden set, closing the sheet when no visible row remains. `useLeaderboard` stays raw (ADR 0001, ADR 0011).
- **Doubts** (decision 5). A Doubt with either party in the viewer's hidden set is hidden, so the pair's Doubts vanish for both parties while a third Player, whose set names neither, still sees them. A hidden Player has no row in either who-list, so neither sheet offers the Doubt button against them. Doubt create stays open in the rules.
- **Hearts.** `heartState` receives the union in place of the ban roster, so a counterpart's Heart leaves every count; the viewer is never in their own set, so the own-heart exception holds unchanged.
- **Counts.** Every count shown beside a list is derived from the filtered list: the Tally badge, a Tally Card's count, the who-list header, the open-Doubt summary and the proof pills. A count that comes from a frozen record still includes a hidden Player, which is the presence residual in the table above: the archived standings' "top N of M players" footnote reads the record's `playerCount`, and the Most-Loved award's heart count is the frozen award's.
- **Honours, withheld without promotion** (decision 6). The podium Moment and the last-call line (`ProofFeed`), the Board's pinned Day honour, the live Leaderboard's Day-honour strip (`pinnedOrDerivedDailyHonors`), the farewell podium (`buildPodium`) and the archived hall of fame each withhold a hidden holder exactly as they withhold a banned one: the honour goes blank and nobody inherits it. The Event-wide ⭐ is computed over the raw roster, so a hidden holder's ⭐ simply disappears with their row. The last-call line is re-derived over the unbanned Players as before, but when it would name a hidden leader the line is withheld for the generic Moment copy rather than naming the runner-up (the identity-free wide-open and neck-and-neck lines stand); a hidden runner-up stays in the field, since the line names only the leader. A legacy last-call Moment that carries only a pre-rendered line is dropped while anyone is hidden, as it already is while anyone is banned.
- **Standings rows keep their rank gaps** (decision 6). The live Leaderboard, the archived standings, both Leaderboard share cards and the farewell podium's standings number their rows first and hide the counterpart after, so the ranks read 1, 3 rather than closing up. A ban still closes its gap, as before. Share cards cut the counterpart after the top-N slice, so nobody below moves up into view, and never append a hidden First to BINGO holder. These surfaces read the hidden set directly rather than through a content hook, so each waits for it to be `ready`: the live and archived Leaderboards render their loading state, the farewell podium renders nothing and holds its share card, and the Board withholds its pinned Day honour, so a cold start never paints a counterpart before the pair listener answers. When a block hides every remaining archived row, the archive shows its empty state without attributing the rows to moderation.
- **Most-Loved.** The award record, `proofFeedVisible` and `buildMostLovedPhotoAward` never receive the hidden set, since they mirror `functions/src/finaleContent.ts`. Hiding reaches the award only through `useProofFeed`, and `mostLovedDisplayWinners` then drops a hidden winner without promoting anyone. The farewell share card's credit line keeps counting a hidden co-winner but never names them ("shared with another player").
- **Avatars and profiles.** They are hidden with the row, card or sheet that carries them. `users/{uid}` and the media stay readable (D9); this is the display-only residual recorded above.
- **Never filtered.** The Admin console and every moderation surface, Notices, Community Prompts (`useItems`), the viewer's own content (`useMyProofs`) and the shell watchers above.

## Block and unblock controls

Part 3. No new route and no `App.tsx` change; every control is display-only glue over `blockPlayer` and `unblockPlayer`.

- **Where a block starts.** `BlockPlayerButton` (`src/components/BlockPlayerButton.tsx`), a compact icon button labelled "Block {name}", sits on another Player's Proof card beside Report (which is unchanged, so reporting still takes one tap), and on every other Player's row in the who-list sheets on the Feed and the Board, beside Doubt. It renders only for a signed-in viewer and never on the viewer's own Proof or row; a Proof card shows either Block (someone else's) or Delete (the viewer's), never both. MomentCard and the Leaderboard rows carry no control (the owner's decision). A Player may block an Admin (decision 4).
- **The confirm sheet.** "Block {name}?", then: "You'll stop seeing each other's proofs, marks, hearts, doubts and moments for this {occasion}. Scores and standings don't change." and "They won't get a notification, but their app will stop showing you, so they may be able to tell. You can unblock any time from More." `{occasion}` is the Edition's lexicon (cruise, trip, event). Cancel is the prominent button. The sheet is portalled to `document.body`, because the who-list sheet it opens from carries a transform during its entrance animation, and its Escape and Tab handling runs in the capture phase and stops there, so it never also closes the who-list beneath it. A backdrop click also stops at this sheet: React bubbles a portalled event through the component tree, so without that the host who-list's own backdrop would close too. The trigger and the portalled sheet both carry `ph-no-capture`, so autocapture and session replay never record whom a Player blocked. On close, focus returns to the Block trigger; when the block has unmounted it (its card or row is now hidden), focus lands on the who-list sheet the trigger sat in (the Feed's `role="dialog"` or the Board's bare `.sheet`), if that is still open, rather than dropping to the page. A block started from a Proof card has no host sheet, and it hides every other card by the same Player in the same render, so no nearby anchor is certain to survive; there focus follows the browser default, an accepted residual on the Feed page, which is not a modal and has no focus trap to escape.
- **Confirming.** The block batch is optimistic and durable (ADR 0006), so the sheet closes at once and the counterpart disappears immediately, online or offline, usually taking the card or row the button sat on with it. An online rules rejection is logged and self-corrects when the listener rolls the optimistic pair back, the same posture as Hearts and Marks; a synchronous refusal (a missing uid, a self-block, the reserved `system` author) keeps the sheet open with "Couldn't block {name}. Try again."
- **Where a block is reversed.** More → Support → "Blocked players" (`src/components/BlockedPlayersPanel.tsx`, a `MorePanel` mounted only while open). It lists the viewer's own direction records (`useMyBlocks`), newest first, named from the raw roster (`useLeaderboard`, which the block never filters) and "A player" for a target with no usable name on a server-confirmed roster; until the roster resolves, an unnamed target reads "Loading…" and its Unblock stays disabled, so nobody reverses a block without knowing whose it is. The name is read defensively, because the Player row's rules check ownership and not the field's type: a non-string or blank `displayName` counts as no name, so it neither crashes the panel nor unlocks a row before the roster confirms. Only the blocker can reverse a block: a block made on the viewer is unreadable to them and never listed. The panel root carries `ph-no-capture`, so session replay never records who a Player has blocked. A failed own-blocks listener reads "Couldn't load your blocked players", never the empty state, and so does an unanswered one: `useMyBlocks` listens with metadata changes and reports `confirmed` once a snapshot has come from the server, and an empty list reads "Loading…" until then, because a first open while offline can deliver an empty cache-only snapshot that is no proof the viewer has blocked nobody.
- **Unblocking.** Each row's Unblock opens an inline confirm: "Unblock {name}? You'll see each other again. If they've also blocked you, you'll stay hidden from each other." Unblocking is deliberately not optimistic (`unblockPlayer` sends server-only transactions), so it needs a connection: while `useOnline` reports offline (a trustworthy `false`), Unblock is disabled with "You're offline. Unblocking needs a connection." rather than started. A row that is still a queued block batch (`useMyBlocks` reports it in `pendingTargets`, from the document's `hasPendingWrites`) reads "Saving this block…" with Unblock disabled until the server commits it: the unblock's server-only transactions would not see the uncommitted record, so they would fail and the queued block would land after them. Opening the inline confirm moves focus onto its Cancel, and cancelling returns focus to that row's Unblock. Started online, the row reads "Unblocking…" and every Unblock is disabled until the server answers; the panel can be closed meanwhile, and the listener shows the outcome either way. The in-panel message and that guard last only while the panel is open, an accepted residual: reopening it while the request is still in flight offers Unblock again, and a second attempt can at worst report a failure while the listener removes the row the first one reversed, since a delete of a record already gone is denied rather than applied twice; after a failure, reopening shows the row still in place, which is the outcome. The outcome is reported at the panel level, because a landed unblock removes its row (at once, locally, keyed to that record, so a listener slow to deliver the deletion never re-offers an unblock that already landed): "Unblocked {name}.", or for `stillHidden: true` "Unblocked {name}. You're still hidden from each other for now—usually that means they've blocked you too." (worded as likely, since an orphaned pair or an unanswered listing reports the same). Any error `unblockPlayer` rethrows (a dropped connection, a timeout, a denial on its last attempt) leaves the row in place with "Couldn't unblock {name}. Check your connection and try again." Because confirming unmounts the focused "Yes, unblock" and a landed unblock removes its row, the panel keeps keyboard focus inside itself: on the panel while the request runs, then on the outcome message; the `MorePanel` Tab trap also pulls any focus that has fallen outside the panel back in.
- **Guidelines.** The community guidelines sheet (`AcceptableUse`) says how to block and where to undo it.

### Analytics

Two catalogued events (`GA4_EVENTS`, both sinks), neither carrying a uid, a display name or anything else that identifies either Player, since a block is a private safety action. `block_player { surface }` (`'proof_card' | 'feed_wholist' | 'board_wholist'`) fires once the block batch commits, which for an offline block is on reconnect. `unblock_player { stillHidden }` fires once `unblockPlayer` resolves. Both go through `trackIfCurrentEvent` with the Event captured when the Player acted, so a write that settles after another Event activated is never reported as that Event's.

## Frozen and denormalised content

Day snapshots, podium and last-call Moments, `DayMeta.firstBingo`, the tally aggregate, archive standings and the Most-Loved award record stay exactly as written. Hiding happens at render, so a block that lands mid-Event hides content retroactively and an unblock restores it. No record is rewritten, and no server aggregator becomes block-aware.

## Rollout

Deploy `firestore.rules` first: the new collections are additive and the Hearts conjunct denies nothing until a pair exists. Deploy hosting after: a new client on old rules would get `permission-denied` on every block write. Old installed clients never write blocks and keep working; they simply do not hide until updated. No backfill and no migration. Rollback: reverting hosting removes the UI and the hiding, but leftover pair records are not inert while these rules stay deployed: `heartTargetAccepts` keeps denying Hearts across every standing pair, and the hosting revert also removes the UI that would unblock. A hosting-only rollback therefore deliberately keeps that Hearts enforcement; restoring pre-feature behavior reverts the rules too. Reverting the rules alone re-opens cross-block Hearts, and while the blocking client stays deployed its block and unblock writes and its pair listener get `permission-denied` (a running listener then keeps its last hidden set until a remount or reload, and a fresh one renders unfiltered), so revert hosting first, or hosting and rules together. `firestore.indexes.json` is unchanged: both client queries are single-field.

## Out of scope

Reader-scoped server projections; any change to the read posture of `users/{uid}`, avatars or proof-media Storage (#806 territory); a report-a-Player flow or a combined Report and Block action (follow-up [#1306](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1306)); a per-Player block cap or an Admin-visible block-count signal; hiding Community Prompts by block; block-aware Functions aggregators; deleting block records on membership revocation or account deletion; any per-Moment control on MomentCard.
