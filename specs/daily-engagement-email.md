# Daily themed engagement email (#616)

One email per participant per Event-local calendar date that has a Day, up to the Event's Standings Freeze, sent that morning in the Event's timezone, carrying the same identity the app shows that morning: the Day's Theme name, its palette, its context line. The original contract was one email per DAY at that Day's unlock; [#723](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/723) superseded it at the repository owner's request, because an Event's `days` array does not map one-to-one onto its calendar dates and mailing each Day in turn put two emails on one Bodega Bay Sunday. § Scheduling below is the full rule, and `plans/daily-cards-wireframes.html` § "Daily engagement email" carries the same revision. The job order is fixed—**nudge participation, show standings, make the photo expected, point at the Feed**—and the photos message is load-bearing: every BINGO claim is a photo opportunity, and the email is where that becomes a norm rather than a feature. It also carries the standing reminder that the most-loved photo takes an award at the finale ([#534](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/534)).

The design source is `plans/daily-cards-wireframes.html` § "Daily engagement email"—frames `#fx-email-anatomy` (annotated template anatomy and the per-module email-client constraints), `#fx-email-vacay` (a fully-rendered Bodega Day 1), `#fx-email-gcb` (mid-cruise Day 4), and `#fx-email-registers-tri` (the per-brand register strip). This spec records the implementation of those frames; where the two disagree about what the email LOOKS LIKE—module anatomy, order, copy registers, client constraints—the frames are the design of record and this file is the bug. Send CADENCE is not a frame: the wireframes render a single email and say nothing about which mornings produce one, so § Scheduling below is the contract of record for who is mailed and when, and a cadence change updates the frames' prose to match rather than being overruled by it.

Delivery is the existing transactional stack—Resend via `functions/src/email.ts`, sender identity resolved by `resolveEmailFrom` (`functions/src/dailyEmail.ts`), which this feature READS rather than duplicates. That resolution is Edition-aware ([#671](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/671)): it reads the Event's Edition off the same `resolveEventOrigin` lookup the Feed CTA uses, looks up the matching `EMAIL_FROM_GCB` / `EMAIL_FROM_VACAY` / `EMAIL_FROM_FIVEACROSS` param, and falls back to the project-wide `EMAIL_FROM` param when no override is configured for that Edition (e.g. its sending domain is not yet verified in Resend). The honor system is untouched ([ADR 0001](../docs/adr/0001-honor-system-trust-model.md)): the email recomputes no stats, gates no play, and a mail failure never blocks anything.

## Modules

Seven, in a fixed order, in both the HTML and the plain-text part. Editions change the WORDS, the Day changes the PALETTE, the order never moves.

1. **Preheader**—hidden body text clients show beside the subject. The Day plus one hook, ~85 characters, never a second sentence.
2. **Theme header**—the Day's Theme emoji + label in display type over a two-token gradient band, with the context line (`Day N of M · weekday, date · Place`) and the Theme's five-swatch palette strip. This is the module that makes the email resemble the Day.
3. **Standings snapshot**—the top three through YESTERDAY (today's card has only just unlocked), plus the Event-wide First-to-BINGO holder appended when their rank has fallen outside that slice, plus one personalized "You're #N" line. On the opening Day, or any Day nobody has played, it renders its empty state and sells the two open honors instead of showing a podium of ties—in two variants, because "the cruise starts today" is true on Day 1 and plainly false on Day 4. A board that is all zeros because every Mark so far was ceremonial is NOT that case: somebody holds the ⭐, so the snapshot renders with honest zero rows rather than an empty state advertising an honor that is already taken.
4. **Participation nudge**—the greeting, the arrival line in the Edition's register, today's unlock time, and the Day's "Tonight:" line when it publishes one.
5. **Photos + award**—the two content requirements ride together, the same module every Day, because norms are built by repetition.
6. **Feed CTA**—one bulletproof button, "Open the Feed", deep-linking the Event's canonical host ([#599](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/599)). The Feed, not the Card: the email's job is the social loop.
7. **Footer**—Edition sender identity, the why-you-got-this line, and the visible unsubscribe + preferences links.

- **Given** a mid-cruise Day with a played schedule **then** the model carries the Theme headline, a `Day N of M · weekday · Place` context line, the top three with the ⭐ Event-wide First-to-BINGO pin on the earliest first bingo, and the register's arrival line. (Test: "renders the mid-cruise Day 4 case from the wireframe".)
- **Given** the opening Day of a Vacay Event **then** the standings module renders its empty state naming the trip-wide honor, the subject tail is "your card is live", and the award line reads "most-loved photo of the trip". (Test: "renders the Bodega Day 1 empty state in the trip register".)
- **Given** two recipients of the same send **then** the ONLY differences are the greeting and the "You're #N" line; every other field is identical. (Test: "personalizes exactly one line".)
- **Given** an address that is not on the roster **then** no rank line is rendered rather than a rank nobody holds. (Test: "omits the rank line for an address that is not on the roster".)
- **Given** a later Day on which nobody has played **then** the empty state says the honors are wide open rather than that the occasion starts today. (Test: "does not claim the occasion \"starts today\" on a LATER empty Day".)

### Ranking parity

Ranking is `compareFinalePlayers`, so the email, the podium and the in-app Leaderboard can never disagree about who is ahead. Two subtleties carry the same parity:

**The ⭐ holder is never dropped from the snapshot.** The module shows the top three, and appends the First-to-BINGO holder—carrying their true rank—when they sit below third. Without the exception an honor holder who has since slipped to 4th vanishes from the rows entirely and the email renders no ⭐ at all, while the in-app Leaderboard still shows one. `buildShareStandings` in `src/components/Leaderboard.tsx` makes the same exception for the same reason (`specs/w2-leaderboard.md`: the pin can never silently drop off the card), and this mirrors it.

**A ban hides the holder; it never reassigns the honor.** The historical First-to-BINGO identity is computed from the raw roster before the presentational ban filter, exactly as the in-app Leaderboard does (`specs/w2-ban-console.md`). The banned row is then absent from the rendered/send roster, so no visible Player carries the ⭐; the next-earliest Player is never retroactively promoted.

**The standings totals, the standings tie-break and the ⭐ are three policies, not one.** [ADR 0011](../docs/adr/0011-scoring-policy-stated-not-inferred.md) makes a Day's Pool identity, its Tutorial framing and its Scoring Policy three independent facts, and this module reads a different combination of them for each of the three answers it prints ([#1052](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1052)):

- **Totals exclude ceremonial Days.** A `ceremonial` policy promises its Marks never move the standings, so its bingos and squares are dropped from the sum. Its per-Day bucket is untouched and its own daily honour still stands—the exclusion is of the summed roots, never of the Day.
- **The tie-break excludes Tutorial Days OR ceremonial Days.** `compareFinalePlayers` separates equal scores on the earliest first-bingo, so a timestamp that still counted a ceremonial Mark would let that Mark decide a ranking its score was excluded from. This mirrors `rankingExcludedDay` (`src/game/logic.ts`) and `podiumStandingRow` (`finaleContent.ts`).
- **The ⭐ excludes Tutorial Days alone, and honours the Standings Freeze.** A Tutorial bingo is real play and is summed, but an onboarding or send-off card is framed as non-competition so its bingo never takes the Event-wide honour—without that exclusion an embark-Day winner would take the ⭐ in the email while the in-app Leaderboard gave it to someone else. A ceremonial, non-Tutorial bingo stays *eligible*: inert for score, still something somebody was first to. The cutoff is the Event's resolved freeze (`standingsFreezeAtFor`—the configured `EventDoc.standingsFreezeAt`, else the first ceremonial Day's `unlockAt`), inclusive at the instant itself, because a ceremonial Day keeps recording Marks afterwards and those must not mint a ⭐ the frozen podium does not carry.

The ⭐ is therefore derived by its own selector (`eventFirstBingoUid`) from the raw roster rather than read off the ranked rows: one mutated `EmailPlayer.firstBingoAt` cannot carry both the tie-break and the honour, and making it try is what let an Event with an early ceremonial, non-Tutorial Day mail the wrong rank order while displaying the correct headline winner elsewhere. The two packages stay decoupled; `tests/functions/finale-parity.test.ts` feeds one roster and one schedule to the email, the client podium and the Functions podium and fails if any of the three moves alone.

**`dayStats` is untrusted runtime shape.** `players/{uid}` is self-writable by design ([ADR 0001](../docs/adr/0001-honor-system-trust-model.md)—stats are client-authoritative), so a row carrying `{ dayStats: { 0: null } }` is reachable by any participant. It is sanitized at the read boundary (`sanitizeFinaleDayStats`, the one function `readFinaleRoster` reads its roster through too) and skipped defensively inside `standingsThrough`, because one malformed row throwing would suppress the entire Event's send. A non-object bucket is dropped; a bucket whose COUNTS are malformed is kept, with each invalid count read as `0` and its `firstBingoAt` left usable, because that is what the in-app Leaderboard and the frozen record do—dropping it left the row with no breakdown at all, and the ⭐ selector falls back to the ROOT for exactly those rows, so the email could lose a holder the app still shows (#1152, CodeRabbit on PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165)).

**The roster is READABLE before it is ranked.** `players/{uid}` validates no field, so a Player can self-write a count or an instant far outside the magnitude `firestore.rules` accepts, and `useLeaderboard`, `draftEventArchive` and `readFinaleRoster` all bring those inside `MAX_ARCHIVE_NUMBER` before they rank. This path did not: `standingsThrough` and `headlineFirstBingoAt` accepted any FINITE value, so two counts that tie once clamped ranked by their raw difference in the morning email, and an out-of-bound `firstBingoAt` gave its ⭐ to a different Player than the one the in-app pin and the podium name—the parity this section exists to state, in the one surface that cannot be corrected after the fact. `readEmailRosterPage` therefore maps every row through `withReadableFinaleRanking` (`functions/src/finaleContent.ts`), once, at the read boundary, so every recipient's model is built from the same numbers every other surface reads (#1152, Codex P2 on PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165)).

**And the RE-AGGREGATED total is bounded, not only its addends.** `standingsThrough` reports a WINDOW, so it adds the window's buckets back up—and a sum of counts that are each inside `MAX_ARCHIVE_NUMBER` is not itself inside it. Two buckets at the maximum gave their row twice the bound in the morning email while the in-app Leaderboard and the frozen record read that same row's clamped ROOT, so a second row at the bound with more squares led the app and trailed the mail—the one divergence this section exists to forbid, in the one surface that cannot be corrected afterwards. The finished totals therefore go back through `clampReaggregatedTotal` (`functions/src/finaleContent.ts`), the same helper both `podiumStandingRow` implementations re-aggregate through, applied ONCE to each total rather than per addend so that a negative count's position in the map cannot decide the answer. It is also the second line the read boundary already has: an unnormalised bucket reaching `standingsThrough` no longer reorders the window (#1152, Codex P2 on PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165)).

## Edition register

Per-brand copy is a table in `functions/src/dailyEmailContent.ts`, one field per row of the wireframe's register strip. The modules the strip marks brand-invariant—Theme header, standings structure, CTA, unsubscribe—carry no field at all, which is how the invariance is enforced rather than merely asserted. An unknown or absent Edition degrades to the legacy Edition (`gcb`), the same direction `setActiveEdition` degrades in the app, and the lookup is an own-property check so an inherited `Object.prototype` key can never pass as a register ([#597](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/597)).

- **Given** each Edition **then** the occasion noun, subject tail, arrival line, photos nudge, award line and why-you-got-this line follow the #608 lexicon, and every Edition **of** the platform carries the `· by Five Across` endorsement in its footer brand line — Vacay from #616, Gay Cruise Bingo from #698 — while `fiveacross` goes without, being the platform itself. That is the same partition the in-app lockup uses (`wordmarkByline`, specs/event-resolution.md), so the sign-off and the header cannot disagree about which Editions the platform endorses. (Tests under "Edition registers".)

## Day-Theme tokens

The app keeps a Theme's label and emoji in `src/theme/themes.ts` and its palette in `src/theme/themes.css`. The email needs both, as literal hex, in the functions package—and a mail client resolves no CSS variable—so `functions/src/dailyEmailTheme.ts` mirrors the seven tokens the email paints with. The app and functions packages are deliberately decoupled (the same posture `autohide.ts` keeps toward `moderation.ts`), so this is a mirror, not an import.

A mirror without a parity test is how mirrors drift, so the suite parses both app sources and asserts the table matches them id-for-id and hex-for-hex. It is intended to fail if a Theme is added, removed or re-palettised on either side alone.

- **Given** the app Theme registry **then** the mirror covers exactly the same ids and carries each label, emoji and palette hex verbatim. (Tests under "Day-Theme token mirror".)
- **Given** an unknown, absent or inherited Theme id **then** the tokens fall back to that EDITION's default Theme—`the-birds` for Vacay, `marquee` for Five Across—rather than to grey, and rather than to Gay Cruise Bingo's default, which would put another product's visual identity on a degraded Day. (Tests: "falls back to the default Theme for an unknown id", "falls back to the EDITION default", "mirrors the app's per-Edition default Theme".)

**Dates are calendar labels, not instants.** `DayDef.date` is already the Event's local calendar date, so `formatDayDate` renders it in UTC and takes no timezone argument at all. Pinning it to noon UTC and then re-rendering it in the Event's zone applies the offset twice—invisible inside ±12h, and wrong past it: in `Pacific/Kiritimati` (UTC+14) `2026-07-18` would print as Sunday, Jul 19. The unlock TIME is a real instant and is formatted in the Event's zone, falling back to UTC for a bogus IANA string.

- **Given** an Event east of UTC+12 **then** the context line names the weekday on the Event's own calendar. (Test: "renders the Day date as the calendar label it already is".)

## Email-safe rendering

Every constraint below is a real mail-client limitation, not a stylistic preference, and each is asserted:

- **600px single-column table layout**, no flexbox or grid—Outlook 2016-2019 renders through Word, which supports neither.
- **Literal hex on `bgcolor`/`style`, never a CSS variable**—Gmail resolves no custom properties.
- **An explicit background AND ink on every module**, plus `color-scheme`/`supported-color-schemes`—Gmail and Outlook dark modes invert colors they consider unmanaged, so every module leaves them nothing to grab. The light Theme (`fog-froth-farewells`) is the case that proves it and is rendered under test.
- **Display type declared `'Bebas Neue','Arial Narrow',Arial`** and designed to survive the fallback, because the webfont does not load in mail.
- **A bulletproof CTA**—a padded, bordered cell plus the Outlook-only VML fill—and no `<img>` anywhere, so the primary action survives image blocking.
- **A plain-text mirror** in the same module order, so `multipart/alternative` degrades to something still worth reading.
- **Every interpolated string escaped**, and every `href` restricted to `https:`—the model is built from Firestore data (Event name, Player display names, the canonical host), so a `javascript:` scheme reaching an anchor is the one templating mistake that would matter.

- **Given** a rendered email **then** it is a 600px table with no flex/grid, contains no `var(--`, declares both color schemes, includes the VML CTA and no `<img>`, and carries the visible unsubscribe and preferences links. (Tests under "renderDailyEmailHtml".)
- **Given** a display name containing markup **then** it is escaped rather than interpolated raw; **given** a non-https CTA URL **then** the anchor renders `#`. (Tests: "escapes Firestore-sourced copy", "refuses a non-https link".)

## Scheduling

`dailyEngagementEmail` is an `onSchedule` trigger on `*/15 * * * *` UTC, and the cadence is deliberate for the reason `unlockDay` runs the same one ([#552](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/552)). The send has to land at the Day's unlock in the EVENT's timezone, and a Cloud Scheduler cron is one fixed schedule for every Event a deployment serves: a daily UTC cron would fire at an arbitrary local hour, and a per-Event cron would mean a deploy every time an Event is created somewhere new. So the schedule is a frequent, dumb sweep and `dueDayForDailyEmail` is the real clock. Fifteen minutes rather than sixty because real IANA offsets include `:30` and `:45`.

**One email per Event-local calendar date that has a Day, sent that morning in Event time** ([#723](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/723)). Send an email before any day that exists, regardless of what sort of day it is. Eligibility reads each Day's own `date` label and nothing else: `date` is a required `DayDef` field—the schedule's own statement of which calendar day a Day belongs to—so deciding which dates have Days needs no clock and no inspection of `unlockAt`. There is deliberately no taxonomy of unlock values in the eligibility decision; the sentinel opening Day, an ordinary Day and a Day whose `unlockAt` is corrupt are all simply Days on a date. Sorting them into kinds and skipping two of the three is what left Bodega Bay's Friday unmailed.

**One Day owns a date, and it is the lowest-indexed Day on it.** An Event's `days` array does not map one-to-one onto its calendar dates. Bodega Bay puts two Days on Sunday—the 06:00 competitive card and the 11:00 ceremonial wrap-up whose unlock IS the Standings Freeze—and mailing each in turn put two emails five hours apart on one Sunday, the second announcing the wrap-up Day's `place` ("The drive home") through an arrival line built for a port name. A later Day sharing a date is not a new day and gets no email of its own; its content still reaches players in the app. This collapse is the central rule, not an edge case.

**A Day with no usable `date` can never be due.** That is the only disqualifier. It is what keeps a Day with nothing to anchor to from looking permanently overdue and mailing forever ([#289](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/289)), and it is the whole of the fail-safe—a malformed `unlockAt` no longer disqualifies anything. "Usable" means a real calendar day, not merely ten characters shaped like one: the label is parsed and round-tripped, so `2026-99-99` and `2026-02-30` are both no-date. Shape alone would let a nonexistent label own a date, and because it can never equal today's it would surface only through the carried-Day path—putting the Sunday wrap-up back in a second evening email, the very regression the ownership rule removes.

`unlockAt` decides only WHEN in the morning the email goes out. A Day with a valid finite positive `unlockAt` keeps the absolute window `[unlockAt, unlockAt + SEND_WINDOW_MS)` it has had since [#616](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/616), so the email carries the same identity the app shows that morning. Ownership picks which Day may speak for a date; it never shortens that window—a Day unlocking at 23:00 local is still due at 01:00, after the date has rolled over, or an outage in those two hours would lose that Day's email permanently. Any other `unlockAt`—the `0` sentinel, missing, `NaN`, negative, non-numeric, or outside the range a JavaScript `Date` can represent—is one case, "names no hour", and gets the same span measured on the Event's wall clock from the fallback morning hour on the Day's own `date`, bounded to that one local morning on that one date. The fallback hour is the EARLIEST hour any Day in the Event really unlocks, defaulting to `DEFAULT_SEND_LOCAL_HOUR` (08:00, the convention `src/domainTypes.d.ts` documents) when the Event has no real unlock at all: a fixed 08:00 would have mailed Bodega's opening Friday two hours out of step with its own 06:00 mornings, and taking the earliest means this hour is never later than the Event's own wake-up. The Event zone is load-bearing for that window—08:30 in Los Angeles is 15:30 UTC, so a UTC reading would call the morning long over. In the rare case where yesterday's Day and today's are both inside their windows, today's owner wins, so the send is never a stale card. Out-of-range is bounded in `hasScheduledUnlock`, the single predicate both the sender and the copy consult, because `Number.MAX_VALUE` is finite and positive yet every `Intl` call on it throws: unbounded, one corrupt future Day would abort the Event's whole due check and suppress that morning's valid email—mailing nothing, the failure this rule exists to remove.

**The Standings Freeze stops the mail** ([#1121](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1121)). Nothing is due at or after the Event's resolved Standings Freeze (ADR 0011, `standingsFreezeAtFor` — the CONFIGURED `EventDoc.standingsFreezeAt`, else the first ceremonial Day's `unlockAt`). The freeze is where scoring permanently ends: no later Mark can move the standings, the ⭐ is settled, and the finale is the mail that speaks for what is left. A daily *engagement* email sent afterwards invites people into a competition that has closed, which is exactly the state that was caught shipping — an Event whose only bingo landed post-freeze on a ceremonial Day renders an all-zero board with no ⭐ holder, so the next morning's email advertised two open honours nobody could still win. Answering it here rather than in the copy makes that empty state unreachable instead of reworded, and keeps `buildDailyEmailModel` clock-free: the cutoff lives in `dueDayForDailyEmail` with the rest of the clock, reading the SAME resolved instant the headline ⭐ is settled against, so the freeze this sweep stops at and the freeze it quotes can never be two different moments — one variable, resolved where the cutoff is finally applied, serves both. An Event with neither a configured freeze nor a ceremonial Day resolves `null` and mails exactly as it always has.

**The cutoff is on the send instant, not on the freeze's calendar date.** The two disagree on the Event it matters most to: Bodega Bay's freeze is the 11:00 Sunday check-out, which it both CONFIGURES and derives — `scripts/seed-data/bodega-bay-2026.mjs` pins `EventDoc.standingsFreezeAt` to the ceremonial wrap-up Day's own `unlockAt`, so the configured and the derived instant agree to the millisecond — and that is five hours after the same Sunday's 06:00 email, which is a morning of open scoring and still mails. Suppressing the freeze's whole date would drop the trip from three emails to two and silence a playing morning. The residual is deliberate: a Day whose six-hour window is still open when the freeze lands stops being due mid-window, so an outage spanning the boundary loses that morning's email rather than delivering it late into a closed Event — sent mail is irreversible, an unsent one is not. A post-freeze sweep reports `not-due`, the same answer as any other morning with nothing to send; `archived` remains the post-Event freeze's own reason.

**And the cutoff is re-evaluated immediately before delivery, against the Event re-read there** (Codex P2, PR [#1202](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1202)). The due check is a single clock reading taken when the sweep opens an Event, and the hostname resolution and the roster page sit between it and the first send — so a run that starts moments before the freeze can cross the boundary during that preparation, and nothing downstream stops it: an Event is frozen long before it is archived, so the `status`/`archiving` read that already guards delivery (§ the post-Event freeze) sees a perfectly live Event and lets the mail out at or after the instant this rule promises nothing is sent at. So that same fresh read resolves the freeze as well, and the clock is read again against it. The value that decides is therefore the CURRENT one on both sides — a freeze configured, moved or removed between the due check and delivery is honoured rather than the stale one the call opened with — and the answer is `not-due`, the same as the due check's. **Everything freeze-dependent the send quotes is resolved from that same value, below the check that used it** (Codex P2 round 2): the headline ⭐ in particular, which used to be settled up in the preparation block against the freeze the call opened with. Split across two values, an EXTENSION was the case that escaped — the fresh value permits the email, the superseded one decides its honour, and a bingo landing between the old cutoff and the roster read is valid under the extended freeze yet missing from the headline the email prints. Recomputing it from the delivery-time value rather than refusing the send keeps the promise by construction and still mails the morning, where deferring to the next sweep would risk a window that has since closed. The residual matches the archive guard's exactly: a freeze landing after delivery has begun still finishes the roster already in flight, because the check is per Event, not per recipient.

Running 96× a day is safe because every beat is self-guarded rather than schedule-timed: the Event-level toggle is off by default, the due window closes six hours after unlock so a recovered outage never back-fills an already-played Day, each recipient's `lastSentDayIndex` makes a second sweep a no-op, and the Resend idempotency key (`daily-email/{eventId}/{dayIndex}/{uid}`) collapses any duplicate that slips through a failed marker write.

## The winner announcement (#1192)

The last mail an Event sends, and the only one at or after its Standings Freeze. The section above stops the daily card there because nothing it says is still true; this is what speaks in its place, so the last thing in a participant's inbox is the final standings rather than a card nobody can play. It is the same email FAMILY — the same opt-in, the same unsubscribe, the same shell and the same Edition registers — not a new category, so a participant who unsubscribed from the daily card is not mailed this either.

**It is its own quarter-hourly sweep, due off the `podium` Moment.** `podiumAnnouncementEmail` (`functions/src/index.ts`) is `dailyEngagementEmail`'s twin — same cadence, same service account, the same `RESEND_API_KEY` binding and the same generous timeout — and `runPodiumEmailSweep` (`functions/src/podiumEmail.ts`) is the sweep. The Event is due when the finale beat has posted its `podium` Moment WITH a payload, the Event-level `settings.dailyEmailEnabled` is on, it is neither archived nor closing, and it carries no `podiumEmailAt`. The ticket asked for the send to fire from inside the `postPodium` arm of `finaleActions`; it does not, and the three reasons are all properties of that scheduler rather than preferences (Codex on PR [#1207](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1207)). `unlockDay` binds no `RESEND_API_KEY`, so every transport setup would have failed silently and retried forever. It takes Firebase's 60-second default timeout, which a paced per-recipient fan-out exhausts at roughly a hundred recipients. And its Event loop is SERIAL, so one Event's mail would have delayed every later Event's Day snapshot and finale beats. The beat still owns the trigger in the sense that matters — nothing is mailed until it posts the Moment.

**The Moment is the source, not the roster.** The `podium` Moment is written once at a deterministic id (`events/{eventId}/moments/podium`) and never amended, so the champion and the ⭐ are read out of its stored payload. That is what makes the email quote the frozen record BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by a recomputation that happens to agree — and the alternative was measured: an email that rebuilt the payload from the roster on a retry could name a different champion than the Moment already posted, because `players/{uid}` is client-authoritative and validates no field (ADR 0001), so a post-freeze display-name or count edit moves values the freeze cutoff does not bound (it bounds timestamps). [#1052](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1052) is the record of what it costs when several readers of one honour each derive it themselves.

**Row one is the Moment's champion, placed by identity.** The live ranking fills only the rows BELOW it, with that uid removed so nobody appears twice, and the recipient's own placing indexes that same ordering. Leaving row one to the roster was a real defect rather than a tolerable residual (Codex P2 round 2): the subject names the Moment's champion, and a post-freeze self-write — `players/{uid}` validates no field (ADR 0001), and the freeze cutoff bounds timestamps, not counts — could put somebody else at the head of the rebuilt ranking, producing an email whose headline and whose first row named two different people.

**The ranking is cut at the RESOLVED freeze, the same instant the Moment was built at.** `standingsFreezeAtFor` prefers the configured `EventDoc.standingsFreezeAt` and falls back to the first ceremonial Day's `unlockAt`, and the sweep resolves it the same way the finale beat does. Reading the configured field alone would leave the cutoff null for every Event written before that field existed — which is both live Events — so ranks 2 and 3 would have counted post-freeze marks the Moment's own champion excludes, and the tie-break instant would differ too, reordering rows. That is a systematic disagreement with the frozen record rather than the per-Player-edit residual below.

**The residual is ranks 2 and 3 only.** Those rows come from the roster through `podiumStandings` — the same function whose head `buildPodiumPayload` took its champion from — so they can still drift from what the Moment would have shown. Closing that means persisting a top three onto the Moment, which was considered and declined: it changes an immutable Feed payload and the in-app podium would have to render it to stay honest.

**An empty board is its own input, not an absent champion.** `boardWasEmpty` is read off the Moment's payload BEFORE the ban filtering below, because the filter gave `champion: null` a second meaning: an Event whose frozen champion is currently banned would otherwise have told every recipient that nobody marked a square, directly above a personal placing line reporting their own non-zero result.

**The honours are ban-filtered for the email, though the Moment's payload is not.** `readFinaleRoster`'s contract states why the record is unfiltered — ban filtering "is applied only to the rendered view/copy, so reversible bans do not permanently erase finale data" — and this email is a rendered view. So a champion or ⭐ holder who is currently banned is withheld from the email rather than named in its subject while row 1 shows somebody else, and a banned participant is not a recipient. **The Most-Loved award is filtered the same way** (Codex and CodeRabbit, round 2): a banned winner is dropped, the next visible co-winner becomes the hero, the "shared with N others" tail counts only Players the reader could see, and an award with no visible winner left renders no module.

**And the stored award is validated, not cast.** `EventDoc.mostLovedPhoto` arrives as a raw Firestore map, and the content module indexes into it — an award missing `winners`, or a winner without a string `promptText`, threw inside the renderer. Because that throw landed in the per-recipient catch it counted as a failure for EVERY recipient, so the Event never drained and the sweep retried the identical crash every quarter hour. `visibleMostLovedAward` normalises anything malformed to `null`, which renders as no module.

**The fresh guards run BEFORE the empty-roster branch, not after.** Ordering is load-bearing: with the recount first, an Event disabled or archiving since the due check could have its completion marker written by the zero-recipient path, after which re-enabling answers `already-sent` forever and an archive transition recorded completion instead of stopping. The archive check, the toggle check and the ban comparison all precede it now, and it still precedes the origin and sender resolution so nothing is looked up for an Event with nobody to mail.

**The ban roster is compared at delivery too.** Every ban-filtered thing the snapshot carries — the recipient list, the podium honours, the Most-Loved winner — was filtered against the roster the due check read, so a ban landing during preparation would mail the newly banned Player and keep naming them in everybody else's copy. The send aborts rather than rebuilding: the snapshot is internally consistent, and returning undrained sends the Event through the whole due check again next sweep, which produces a correctly filtered snapshot by construction while every recipient already mailed is skipped by their own marker. The residual matches the daily card's — a ban landing after delivery has BEGUN still finishes the roster already in flight, because the check is per send, not per recipient.

**The send waits for the freeze stamp, not just the podium Moment.** `runFinaleBeats` posts the Moment under its own guard, independently of the freeze — that decoupling is deliberate (#228) — so a run whose freeze transaction failed can leave a posted podium beside an Event carrying no `mostLovedPhoto` at all. The field's contract is explicit that ABSENCE means "not yet computed" while `{ winners: [], heartCount: 0 }` means "computed, no award", and collapsing the two would mail the Event, stamp completion, and permanently omit an award the next unlock retry was about to persist. `frozenAt` is the gate rather than the award itself because one transaction writes both: waiting on the award would hang forever on the defensive freeze path that writes none, while waiting on the freeze is bounded, since that beat retries until it lands.

**The award is re-joined against the LIVE Proof before a hero is chosen.** `buildMostLovedPhotoAward`'s contract states that display always re-joins the live Proof doc so a later-hidden photo can never render from a stale record, and this email is a display surface. A Proof deleted, hidden, pushed over the report threshold, or re-created as a different incarnation since the freeze is dropped, and the next surviving winner becomes the hero; an award with no surviving winner renders no module. The join reads in award order and stops at the first survivor, so the common case costs ONE document read. A read failure drops that winner rather than naming it — a failed read is not evidence the photo is fine.

**The completion recount compares the UID SET, not the count, and it happens INSIDE the transaction that writes the marker.** Reading the roster and then writing the Event document left a window in which a Player could create their row: the marker still landed, every later sweep answered `already-sent`, and that participant was never mailed. The transaction reads the players collection, so Firestore serializes the write against any creation in it — a row added during it aborts and retries, and the retry sees it. The window was narrow, but the loss is silent and permanent, which is the combination worth paying a transaction for. An admin deleting one Player while another joins leaves the total unchanged while an unexamined recipient has replaced an examined one, and both writes are permitted during a fan-out that runs for minutes. Only "is every visible uid now one I examined" is the question worth asking.

**Delivery re-reads the Event immediately before the first send.** The due check's Moment and roster reads, the hostname resolution and the sender lookup all sit between the archive guard and the first message, and archival is terminal for this send — so an archive landing in that window would otherwise mail an Event the spec promises nothing is mailed at. The same pre-delivery re-read `sendDailyEmailForEvent` performs, for the same reason.

**The roster read is bounded at the query, and re-counted before the marker.** The ceiling plus one, so overflow is detected from the raw page rather than from the ban-filtered roster — a corrupted `players` collection would otherwise be materialised and sorted in full before any loop guard applied. And because `firestore.rules` permits `players/{uid}` creation until archival while a paced fan-out runs for minutes, the roster is re-counted before `podiumEmailAt` is stamped: a Player who joined mid-send is absent from the list this run walked, and stamping on it would mean every later sweep skips the Event and they are never mailed. A mismatch simply withholds the marker; the next sweep examines the new member while everybody else is skipped by their own `podiumEmailSentAt`.

**Exactly one send per Event per recipient, on its own marker.** `podiumEmailSentAt` on `events/{eventId}/emailPrefs/{uid}`, deliberately not the daily card's `lastSentDayIndex`. The two answer different questions — "have I mailed this Day" versus "have I sent the one winner mail for this Event" — and the podium fires on the farewell Day, whose index the daily card may also have stamped, so one field serving both would let a card send suppress the winner mail or the reverse. The Resend idempotency key is `podium-email/{eventId}/{uid}` with no Day in it, because the Event and the recipient are the whole identity of the send; a retry after a failed marker write dedupes there rather than arriving twice.

**An unban after completion does not reopen the fan-out.** The membership recount compares the visible roster against the SAME ban list the send walked, carried rather than re-read: a fresh read would make an unban register as an arrival, and an Event that has ever banned anyone could then have its completion marker deferred indefinitely by ordinary moderation — which is precisely the defect that made the marker unwritable in the first place. The question the guard asks is "did anybody join while I was sending", and the ban roster the send used is the right baseline for it. The cost is that a Player unbanned after the Event is marked finished does not receive the winner email; that is the deliberate price of the marker being writable at all on an Event with any moderation history. It differs from the reversible-toggle case below because a ban does not stop the fan-out from completing for everyone else, whereas a disabled toggle means the fan-out never ran.

**A disabled Event is never marked finished.** `settings.dailyEmailEnabled` is reversible, so neither the due check nor the delivery-time re-check writes a completion marker when it is off: an owner who turns the daily email on after the podium has posted still gets the winner announcement, and a marker written while it was off would make that permanently impossible. The toggle is re-read immediately before the first send for the same reason the archive state is, and an empty visible roster is re-counted before completion too — `firestore.rules` permits `players/{uid}` creation until archival, so the zero-recipient path can gain a member between the due check and the send exactly as a populated one can.

**`podiumEmailAt` on the Event is the sweep's quiet-down, and it is not the duplication guard.** It is stamped only by a run that gave every recipient a PERMANENT answer — mailed, or suppressed for a reason no retry would change — so a partial or failed fan-out leaves it absent and the next sweep resumes. Per-recipient duplication is prevented by `podiumEmailSentAt` alone, which is what makes a resumed run safe. It is written with a MERGE: a one-argument Admin SDK `set()` replaces the document, which would have reduced the Event to `{ podiumEmailAt }` and dropped it out of every later sweep. It is server-written and refused to every client in `firestore.rules`, alongside `finaleCompletedAt` and for the same class of reason: an admin able to stamp it could silently suppress the Event's last email for everyone on the roster.

**A failed marker write keeps the run open.** A send that Resend accepts while `podiumEmailSentAt` fails to persist is the one way a duplicate escapes: the recipient is mailed, nothing records it, and Resend's idempotency key only dedupes for 24 hours. Counting the failure as `blocked` withholds `podiumEmailAt`, which brings the retry forward to the next quarter hour — well inside the window, where the key still collapses it.

**A permanently undeliverable recipient is recorded durably too.** `podiumEmailSkippedAt`, its own field rather than a `podiumEmailSentAt` value, so "we decided not to mail you" and "we mailed you" stay distinguishable to anyone reading the doc in support. Without it, every sweep repeats a Firebase Auth lookup for each address-less Player, and a long leading prefix of them could consume a whole invocation and starve the deliverable tail behind it indefinitely.

**A permanent answer and an open question are different outcomes.** `skipped` counts recipients no retry would help — opted out, already sent, no address on file. `blocked` counts recipients this run could not decide about, because a dependency failed: the prefs doc could not be read or minted, or the address lookup threw. Only `skipped` is compatible with draining. Collapsing them is how a transient Firestore or Auth outage could skip an entire roster, report the fan-out finished, stamp the marker, and mean nobody was ever mailed. The address lookup therefore does NOT swallow its own failure the way the daily card's copy does: `auth/user-not-found` is a permanent absence and stays a skip, and every other error propagates to be counted as blocked.

**The pacing queue is per-invocation, not account-wide.** Each family owns its own in-memory queue, so a daily sweep and a podium sweep running at once can each pace independently and together exceed Resend's account-wide rate; so can two overlapping instances of either. The two schedules are staggered seven minutes apart (`*/15` against `7-59/15`) so the common case — a short podium burst against a daily sweep — does not overlap at all, and the residual is stated here rather than papered over: a long-running sweep can still span into the other's slot. A shared durable limiter is the real fix and belongs to both families rather than to this ticket.

**Archival is terminal for this send**, the same posture the daily card takes: an archived or closing Event is not due, so a fan-out still owed when the archive lands is abandoned rather than retried. That is a deliberate residual, not an oversight — the archive is the point at which the Event's record is taken, and `finaleCompletedAt` deliberately does not wait on this email, so a permanently failing transport cannot block an irreversible flip.

**A failed send blocks nothing.** The sweep is a separate function from the finale beat, so the freeze and the Moment have already committed before it runs and nothing about the email can fail them. A per-recipient failure is counted and logged with the error's NAME and the ids only, never the address or the payload.

**Content is the wireframe's frame `#fx-email-finale-gcb` and its numbered legend.** Module order: Theme header (the farewell Day's own Theme, with "Final standings" where the daily card says "Day N"), final standings, the ⭐, the most-loved photo, where you finished, sign-off + CTA, footer. Three modules are CONDITIONAL and are OMITTED rather than emptied — the ⭐ and the award when the Event has no holder, the placing line for an address that is not on the roster — because an empty state that advertises a withheld honour is the defect #1121 was filed for.

**The subject names the champion.** `Final standings 🏆—{register.finaleSubjectTail(name)}` — "Zacaria Arab takes the cruise" / "takes the trip" / "takes it" — because that is the strongest true thing the mail has to say. An Event whose board closed empty has no name to print and falls back to the occasion close, "the cruise is done". The ⭐ is its own module rather than a row pin, because the Event-wide honour excludes Tutorial Days and so frequently belongs to someone outside the top three.

**The subject is the one place participant text reaches a header, and it is sanitised there.** The daily card's subject is built entirely from the Theme registry and the Edition register, so no send in this family has previously carried participant content in a header at all; naming the champion changes that. `players/{uid}` validates no field (ADR 0011's sibling rule, ADR 0001), and `sendEmail` passes `subject` to the provider untouched, so `subjectSafeName` strips control characters and newlines — the header-injection shape — and bounds the length so one participant cannot push the register's own words out of every inbox preview. The provider is called over a JSON API rather than by writing SMTP, so a CRLF is unlikely to split a header in practice; "unlikely, because of how a dependency happens to serialise" is not a property this send rests on. The body needs none of it: every rendered field goes through `esc` in the template.

**A truncated tie is not counted whenever any ban is in play.** `winners` is a bounded prefix while `winnerCount` preserves the full cardinality beyond it, so a truncated award cannot prove its hidden remainder is ban-free and no derived number is trustworthy. The test is whether a ban roster exists at all, not whether a ban was found inside the prefix — that narrower test was the first version of this rule and it covered precisely the case that does not need the guard, leaving the ban-only-beyond-the-prefix case reporting an unfiltered total. With no bans configured, no filtering happened and the stored cardinality stands. Reporting one would overstate the visible tie and name hidden Players by implication, which is what the ban filter exists to prevent. The copy drops to a non-numeric tail — "Shared with others on the same count." — rather than dropping the tail entirely, which would distort in the other direction.

**A tie on the Most-Loved Photo prints the hero and says so.** `buildMostLovedPhotoAward` returns every proof at the maximum eligible Heart count with `winners[0]` the share hero, so printing only the hero would silently award one of several and printing all of them would have no bound. `winnerCount` is absent on records written before the bounded format, where `winners` WAS the complete tie, so the retained prefix's length is the fallback. The explicit no-award record (`winners: []`, `heartCount: 0`) renders no module rather than a zero.

## Consent

Non-negotiable, and applied before the send rather than filtered after it.

**Recipients** are signed-in participants only—the Event's `players` roster, ban-filtered exactly as the podium filters it, mapped to verified Firebase Auth emails by the same verified-only policy `notify.ts` uses. A participant with no verified address is skipped.

**The Event-level admin toggle** is `events/{eventId}.settings.dailyEmailEnabled`, read as OFF unless explicitly `true`. It is the only thing that decides whether anyone is emailed at all, it ships off, and it is admin-writable through the settings gate that already exists in `firestore.rules`—no new write path. (The Admin-console CONTROL for it is not in this change; the field is set through the console's existing settings write path or a seed until a follow-up adds the switch.)

**The per-user opt-out** lives at `events/{eventId}/emailPrefs/{uid}`: `{ optedOut, token, lastSentDayIndex, updatedAt }`. Event-scoped because that is what the unsubscribe link in a given Event's email actually promises, and because the Event is already the schema's isolation boundary. The collection is SERVER-OWNED—`firestore.rules` denies clients read and write outright, and the Admin SDK bypasses rules. Both directions matter: `token` is an unguessable per-user capability, so a readable token would let any signed-in reader unsubscribe someone else, and the doc is the record of a consent decision, so a client write path would let a Player forge another Player's suppression.

**The token is stored randomness, not an HMAC.** An HMAC would need a Secret Manager entry set out of band, and an unresolved secret would break the one part of this feature that must never break. 256 stored random bits are equally unguessable with no key management, and revoking one is a field write rather than a rotation.

**Unsubscribe** is the `emailUnsubscribe` HTTP endpoint. It is HTTP rather than a callable because RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is a bare POST issued by the mail client itself, with no Firebase SDK, no session and no callable envelope—and because the visible link is a URL a mail app opens in a browser. Every send carries both the visible link and the `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header pair that makes a client surface its own native control.

**GET confirms; POST acts.** Corporate link scanners and client prefetchers issue GETs against every URL in a message, so a GET that unsubscribed would silently opt people out of mail they never opened. The GET renders a one-button form; the POST—which is also what one-click sends—performs the change. A wrong token and an unknown uid get the identical answer, so the endpoint cannot be used to enumerate participants. The confirmation form carries through every non-reserved parameter the request arrived with, so a deployment whose `EMAIL_UNSUBSCRIBE_URL` selects the endpoint with its own router parameter does not lose it on the POST.

**The anonymous capability is unsubscribe-only.** A forwarded or leaked old email must never be able to reverse the recipient's suppression indefinitely. `resubscribe` is therefore rejected and is never rendered as a link; turning mail back on belongs to a future authenticated preference flow (or explicit support action), where the actor can be tied to the Player rather than merely to possession of an old message.

**A read failure is not an absence**, anywhere in this module. The preference doc is only ever created from a CONFIRMED absence, and only through an atomic `create`. Collapsing "cannot read" into "not there" would let a transient Firestore error mint a fresh opted-in document over a real opt-out and mail somebody who asked not to be mailed; `create` closes the remaining window, where a concurrent unsubscribe lands between the read and the write.

The same distinction decides the endpoint's STATUS CODE. `applyOptOut` reserves `invalid` (404) for a confirmed refusal—no such document, or a token mismatch—and answers `error` (500) when the read itself failed. A 500 is retryable and a 404 is not, so answering a transient blip with 404 tells an RFC 8058 one-click client the unsubscribe is gone rather than that it should try again: the request is dropped and the reader keeps getting mail.

**Verification and update are one transaction.** `applyOptOut` reads the token, compares it and writes inside a single transaction, so a token rotated or revoked between the read and the write forces a retry whose re-read sees the new token and answers `invalid`. Split across three steps, a request bearing the OLD token would still land its write on the newly-secured document—defeating the field-write revocation this module relies on.

**The token back-fill is transactional**, and it returns the PERSISTED token rather than the one that call minted. A document that exists without a token gets one written inside a transaction, so two concurrent sweeps cannot each write their own and each return their own—the loser re-reads and mails the winner's token. An unconditional merge would produce an email whose `List-Unsubscribe` link carries a token the endpoint rejects: an unsubscribe that looks fine and silently does not work. The write names only the token on an existing document, so a stored opt-out survives it.

- **Given** a matching token **then** the opt-out is recorded; **given** a wrong token or an unknown uid **then** the answer is the same `invalid` and the stored state is untouched. (Tests under "applyOptOut".)
- **Given** an anonymous `resubscribe` action **then** it is rejected and the stored opt-out remains true; the confirmation page exposes no turn-mail-back-on link. (Tests: "refuses anonymous re-subscribe", "does not expose a bearer-token path".)
- **Given** a read that FAILED **then** the answer is `error` (a retryable 500), not `invalid` (a permanent 404). (Tests: "answers \"error\", not \"invalid\", when the read FAILS", "still answers \"invalid\" for a CONFIRMED absence".)

## Runtime identity and config

Both new triggers pin `ADMIN_SDK_SERVICE_ACCOUNT`, derived per-project as `functions/src/index.ts` documents ([ADR 0008](../docs/adr/0008-five-across-second-firebase-project.md), #593): they read Events, rosters and hostnames and write opt-out docs through the Admin SDK, which the default Gen2 compute identity cannot reach. The scheduled sender binds `RESEND_API_KEY` like the moderation notifiers.

Every param above is declared in the committed template `functions/.env.example`, including the two this feature added. `EMAIL_UNSUBSCRIBE_URL` in particular is listed there with per-project guidance: a second Firebase project that leaves it unset mails unsubscribe links pointing at the FIRST project, whose `emailPrefs` collection holds no document or token for that participant—so every unsubscribe is answered "that link is no longer valid" and the recipient cannot opt out at all.

`EMAIL_REPLY_TO` (`functions/src/params.ts`) is the address replies land in, applied to EVERY transactional send rather than to this feature alone—added at Nathan's request while this PR owned the email path. It defaults to empty, meaning no `Reply-To` header at all, so a project that sets nothing is byte-identical to before. It is a separate param from `EMAIL_FROM` because the two answer different questions: `EMAIL_FROM` must be an address on a Resend-verified sending domain, while replies want a mailbox a human actually reads. As of [#1102](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1102) they are the same address on every project: `hello@fiveacross.app` is the `From:` for every Edition and receives through Cloudflare Email Routing, so the param is left empty and a reply goes to the `From:` by default. A value would be actively wrong—it is project-wide while [ADR 0008](../docs/adr/0008-five-across-second-firebase-project.md) splits projects by cohort, so one address cannot be right for every Edition a project serves. The Resend Node SDK spells the send field `replyTo` (camelCase), verified against the installed `resend@4.8.0` typings; the `reply_to` that appears elsewhere in those typings is the response shape.

`EMAIL_UNSUBSCRIBE_URL` (`functions/src/params.ts`) is the endpoint's public address. A param rather than a derived string because the address is a deployment fact, not a code fact—the same source deploys to two Firebase projects and may sit behind a rewrite or custom domain. The default sits behind a Firebase Hosting rewrite (`gaycruisebingo.com/unsubscribe`, rewritten in `firebase.json` to the `emailUnsubscribe` function) rather than the bare `*.cloudfunctions.net` address, which is a first-party link and better for deliverability—but the rewrite by itself is cosmetic, not what makes the endpoint reachable. The `gaycruisebingo` GCP project's Domain Restricted Sharing org policy (`constraints/iam.allowedPolicyMemberDomains`) refuses to grant `allUsers` the Cloud Run invoker role on the backing Cloud Run service, and a Hosting rewrite forwards the unauthenticated request into that SAME gated service rather than bypassing it, so both addresses 403 equally until `scripts/set-email-unsubscribe-invoker.sh` disables the invoker IAM check on the backing service post-deploy (the same mechanism `submitBugReport` uses—`docs/app/bug-reports.md` § Repeat-deploy hardening, #158; runbook step in `docs/app/phase-1-deploy.md` § 1a-i). **Any other project must set `EMAIL_UNSUBSCRIBE_URL` in `functions/.env.<projectId>`** to its own canonical host plus `/unsubscribe` (the same rewrite ships from this same `firebase.json`), or its emails will carry an unsubscribe link pointing at the wrong project.

The fan-out is paced (default 550ms between sends) because Resend's default account limit is a couple of requests a second and an unpaced blast would be throttled into dropped mail; the trigger's timeout is raised to match. Active Events start concurrently so a large or slow first Event cannot starve every later one, but every actual delivery passes through one shared pacing queue, keeping the account-wide request rate unchanged.

**The runaway guard bounds EXAMINED players and the roster query itself**, not just send attempts. A cap counting only real sends does nothing for the case it exists to catch—a roster that is entirely opted out, already sent, or missing verified addresses increments nothing and runs to the end, each iteration still costing a preference read—and an unbounded roster `.get()` materializes the whole collection before any in-loop cap can apply. The query is therefore limited to the ceiling plus one (so truncation is distinguishable from landing exactly on it, and is logged as an error), and the loop counts every player it examines. Continuation for legitimate large Events is unaffected: any roster at or under the ceiling is examined in full on every sweep.

- **Given** a roster that is entirely suppressed **then** the loop stops at the ceiling rather than running to the end. (Test: "bounds EXAMINED players, not just send attempts".)
- **Given** a roster larger than the ceiling **then** the query returns at most ceiling-plus-one and the overflow is logged. (Test: "bounds the roster QUERY too".)
- **Given** a banned row inside a truncated query page **then** overflow is still logged from the raw query count. (Test: "logs roster overflow even when a banned row makes the filtered page look within the ceiling".)
- **Given** one Event has a slow send **then** every other active Event can still reach the shared paced delivery queue. (Test: "lets every active Event reach the shared delivery queue".)

## Shared domain contract

`EmailDay` / `EmailEvent` / `EmailPlayer` are `Pick` / `Partial` views of the canonical `DayDef` / `EventDoc` / `PlayerDoc`; they do not restate field types. The stable app import remains `src/types.ts`, backed by declaration-only `src/domainTypes.d.ts`. Functions imports that same declaration file directly, so its `rootDir: "src"` compiler consumes the contract without trying to emit an app source file outside the Functions package.

Three fields are the deliberate exception, and each names the same reason: this boundary reads RAW Firestore documents, so a value the canonical type forbids is nonetheless reachable and must RESOLVE rather than fail to typecheck. `port` / `portEmoji` are the pre-#566 persisted names for `place` / `placeEmoji`. `scoring` and `pool` are typed as bare `string`—the same widening `FinaleDay` applies for the same reason—because a stored `scoring` that is neither policy resolves through `scoringForDay`, and both live Events persist their pools under the pre-#565 `embark` / `farewell` spellings that `normalizePool` folds. Neither is ever compared directly; the resolvers in `scoringVocab.ts` are the contract.

`tests/functions/daily-engagement-email-contract-parity.test.ts` keeps whole-object and field-level compile assertions as documentation of the intended views, including `PlayerDoc.dayStats`; the production types themselves now make silent retyping impossible.

- **Given** a canonical `DayDef` / `EventDoc` / `PlayerDoc` **then** it is assignable to the email's local shape, `settings.dailyEmailEnabled` included. (Tests in `daily-engagement-email-contract-parity.test.ts`.)
