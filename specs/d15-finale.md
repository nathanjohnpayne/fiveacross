# Two-beat finale: last-call standings + freeze + podium (`d15-finale`)

Implements `plans/daily-cards-spec.md` § "Scoring and social surfaces" → "The finale—two-beat finish" and § "Farewell view". The Event ends on two scheduled beats, timed by `finaleTimes` (`d15-scheduler-unlock`, #202): a last-call Moment posts the going-into-the-final-night standings at `lastCallAt`, and at the Event's **Standings Freeze** (`finaleTimes`' `standingsFreezeAt`—the configured `EventDoc.standingsFreezeAt`, else the first ceremonial Day's own `unlockAt`; ADR 0011) the standings **freeze** (`EventDoc.frozenAt` set), the closing Day unlocks, and the finale view opens with the **podium**—Event champion, Event-wide First to BINGO, and the ten daily honors—also posted as a final Moment. In the STANDARD shape those instants are **20:00 on Day 9** and **08:00 on Day 10**; a schedule whose closing Day would otherwise leave an empty last-call window instead derives `lastCallAt` backwards from the close (#784), so this ticket's content functions take the boundary from `finaleTimes` rather than assuming a fixed wall-clock time. `d15-scheduler-unlock` (#202) owns firing the two beats on schedule; this ticket owns their CONTENT—the standings/podium copy and computation—and the client-side farewell podium banner + freeze semantics.

## Contract

- `functions/src/finaleContent.ts` (new, needs-phase-4)—pure, injectable content functions the scheduler's last-call / freeze+podium triggers call into (the STANDARD shape fires those at 20:00-D9 / 08:00-D10; #784's same-date fallback fires last-call earlier, relative to the close, instead). No `firebase-admin`, no live backend (mirrors `unlockDay.ts`'s decoupled-pure posture):
  - `lastCallStandingsCopy(players, opts?)`—the going-into-the-final-night line ("Jess leads by 2 bingos—standings freeze at 8 a.m."), naming the leader and their margin; degrades to a generic line on a tie or an empty board. `opts.freezePhrase` overrides the trailing "standings freeze at …" clause; `DEFAULT_FREEZE_PHRASE` ("standings freeze at 8 a.m") is only the fallback for an Event with no usable freeze instant, never the normal case (#800 — see `freezePhraseForUnlock` below).
  - `freezePhraseForUnlock(freezeAt, timeZone)` (#800)—derives the ACTUAL freeze phrase from the Event's Standings Freeze, formatted in the Event's timezone ("standings freeze at 11 a.m" for an 11:00 freeze, not a hardcoded 8). `runFinaleBeats` (`functions/src/unlockDay.ts`) computes this once per last-call beat, passes it as `lastCallStandingsCopy`'s `opts.freezePhrase`, and persists it on the posted Moment (`lastCall.freezePhrase`) so the client's reconstruction reads the SAME string rather than reformatting the instant itself. Falls back to `DEFAULT_FREEZE_PHRASE` when the instant is missing/non-finite. **Which instant** (#800 shipped before ADR 0011, and the answer changed): it is `times.standingsFreezeAt`, the RESOLVED freeze, not a closing Day's `unlockAt`. Identical for both live Events—Bodega pins the two equal to the millisecond—but load-bearing for an Event that plays competitively until a configured check-out freeze, where no Day's unlock equals the freeze and quoting one would announce the wrong deadline.
  - `normalizeTimezone(raw)` / `DEFAULT_TIMEZONE` (#800 Codex P2)—mirrors `src/data/converters.ts`'s `normalizeTimezone`/`DEFAULT_TIMEZONE` exactly (`Europe/Rome`), restated here because `runFinaleBeats` reads the raw Firestore Event doc directly, bypassing `eventConverter`. A malformed/missing `timezone` must resolve to the SAME zone on both sides, or the freeze phrase reintroduces the very two-rendering-paths-disagree bug #800 exists to close, one layer down.
  - `buildPodiumPayload(players, days, dayHonors)`—the podium payload: Event champion (top of the aggregated standings, EXCLUDING every CEREMONIAL Day), Event-wide First to BINGO (non-Tutorial Days only—a Tutorial Day's mark never takes the honor). Excluded by `DayDef.tutorial` ALONE, never by pool: pool identity and Tutorial framing are independent (ADR 0011), and a curated pool can be real competitive play, and the ten daily honors read from each Day's `meta.firstBingo`. **Plus `playRecorded`: whether ANY Marks exist across the Event, ceremonial and Tutorial Days included** ([#1192](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1192)). That fourth field is NOT derivable from the other three, which is why it is carried: each of them answers a different question under a different exclusion, so an Event whose only play sits on a ceremonial, `tutorial: false` Day has a `null` champion and a real First to BINGO at once. A consumer reading the first as proof nobody played is reading a scoring answer as a Marks answer—the winner-announcement email did, and told every recipient nobody marked a square beside the ⭐ naming the person who bingoed (`specs/daily-engagement-email.md` § "An empty board is its own input"). Computed over the RAW roster via the shared `anyMarksRecorded`, never over the re-aggregated rows where a ceremonial Day's Marks have already been dropped, and from roots OR buckets so the claim it gates is refused unless every signal a row carries agrees nothing was marked. **`PodiumMomentPayload.playRecorded` is OPTIONAL**: a podium Moment is written once and never amended, so every Moment posted before the field existed lacks it and absence means "unknown", not `false`—each consumer states its own frozen-record-only fallback, and that fallback may only ever raise the answer to TRUE (Codex P2 [`4058610372`](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1242#discussion_r4058610372)), including over a stored `false`, which only a Moment contradicting itself can carry. A record naming a champion, the Event-wide ⭐ or a pinned honour proves the Event was played; a record naming none of them proves nothing, because the ceremonial-only Event this field exists for names none of them while having been played all morning. No consumer may turn that silence into "nobody played", which is the inference `playRecorded` was added to retire. **And the value the SCHEDULER posts is the one captured at the freeze, not this builder's derivation** ([#1218](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1218)): the builder is pure and answers for the roster it is handed, which is exactly what the podium beat cannot be trusted to hold. The beat is retried until its Moment lands and a ceremonial Day keeps recording Marks after the freeze by design, so a delayed sweep or a retry after a failed write reads live totals—and unlike `champion` and `firstBingo` this field is a COUNT, carrying no instant for the `freezeAt` cutoff to bite on. `runFinaleBeats` therefore reads the fact off `EventDoc.frozenPlayRecorded`, stamped by the freeze transaction below, and OMITS `playRecorded` from the Moment when the Event carries none rather than substituting the live answer—which by then means a freeze that predates the field or one whose roster read failed, never a freeze this run could not get, because the beat defers rather than posting ahead of the stamp it quotes (below).
  - `ceremonialDayIndexes(days)`—the standings exclusion, keyed on each Day's stated Scoring Policy via `scoringForDay` (`functions/src/scoringVocab.ts`), never on its pool (ADR 0011). A SET, not the single index the superseded `farewellDayIndex` returned: a schedule may state as many ceremonial Days as it means, and a competitive final morning states none.
  - `withReadableFinaleRanking(player)` + `ARCHIVE_NUMBER_BOUND` / `MAX_ARCHIVE_NUMBER` / `clampArchiveNumber` (#1152, Codex P2 on PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165))—**the podium, the live board and the archive rank by ONE normalisation.** `players/{uid}` validates no field ([ADR 0001](../docs/adr/0001-honor-system-trust-model.md)), so a Player can self-write a count or an instant far outside the magnitude `firestore.rules` accepts; `useLeaderboard` and `draftEventArchive` both clamp those before they rank, through `withReadableDayStats` (`src/data/eventArchive.ts`). This side asked only whether a value was FINITE—`readFinaleRoster`'s coercions—and `buildPodiumPayload` then compared the raw numbers, so two oversized `bingoCount`s collapsed to a tie and reordered on squares on the two client paths while the scheduled podium Moment, which is written once and never amended, kept their original count order; an out-of-bound first-bingo instant likewise ties under the clamp, where the shared uid tie-break can hand the honour to the other Player. So this is the mirror of that normaliser—the root three fields AND the per-Day buckets the honour resolves through—and `readFinaleRoster` maps every row through it, exactly as `useLeaderboard` maps the live roster, so every finale beat reads one roster of one set of numbers. The bound constants are restated here rather than imported for the reason every mirror in this file is (`functions/tsconfig.json` sets `rootDir: "src"`), and `tests/functions/finale-parity.test.ts` pins them, the clamp and the normaliser's output against the client's own `src/data/eventLimits.ts`. A well-formed row is returned by identity and no count is recomputed: no real stat is within nine orders of magnitude of the bound, so this decides nothing about who won.
  - `sanitizeFinaleDayStats(value)` + `canonicalDayStatsKey(key)` (#1152, CodeRabbit on PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165); [#1168](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1168))—the READ BOUNDARY every Functions roster reader shares, and **its entry rule is `withReadableDayStats`'s exactly, key for key and shape for shape.** `dayStats` is untrusted runtime shape rather than a contract (ADR 0001), so the map is rebuilt by ONE rule both sides state (`readableDayStats` here; `withReadableDayStats` in `src/data/eventArchive.ts`, with the key predicate in `src/data/eventLimits.ts`): a map that is not a non-null, non-array object—absent, `null`, a string, an array—reads as absent; an entry survives only under a key `Number(key)` round-trips (a safe integer whose `String()` is the key itself, so `"07"`, `" 7"`, `""` and `"-0"` are dropped exactly as `"7.5"` and `"seven"` are, never read as Days 7, 7, 0 and 0 and merged over the real bucket under that Day) and only with a non-null, non-array object for a bucket (plainness is not asked—a `Date` or a `Timestamp` written where a bucket belongs is KEPT, with every field unreadable, on both sides); a surviving bucket's three fields get the root's own coercions (an unreadable count reads `0`, an unreadable instant `null`, a finite one is clamped—an array-valued FIELD is just an unreadable value); a map in which nothing had to be dropped or coerced passes through by IDENTITY on both sides, a field beyond the three the rankers read included, and is rebuilt to those three fields only around a dropped or coerced entry (fix round 1: the sanitiser here used to rebuild unconditionally, so it stripped a foreign field the client kept); and a map left with nothing reads as ABSENT, so the row ranks as a legacy row by its roots on every surface. #1165 left the first two shapes out of scope—the client kept a non-integer key verbatim and kept an array bucket, coercing its fields, while this side canonicalised the key and dropped the bucket—and both sides now DROP, because dropping is the stricter contract, the one under which no two entries can collapse into one Day, and the one this boundary already promised; the empty-map corollary is aligned with it, because `podiumStandingRow` re-aggregated a client `{}` to 0/0 while passing this side's absent map through to the roots. No row a real client writes carries any of these shapes (`foldDayStat` and `foldEchoStats` in `src/game/logic.ts` file every bucket under its numeric `dayIndex`, and the seed and post-freeze ceremonial writes in `src/data/api.ts` and `src/data/admin.ts` key the same way), so nothing rendered moves. What is NOT dropped is a bucket whose counts are malformed—the client keeps that bucket, reads each invalid count as `0` and leaves its `firstBingoAt` usable, while both roster readers here discarded it before the normaliser above could coerce it. A row whose only bucket was malformed therefore reached the scheduler and the morning email as a row with NO breakdown at all, and `effectiveFirstBingoAt` falls back to the ROOT for exactly those rows—so the Event-wide First to BINGO the live board shows could be a holder the podium Moment and the email had already lost, and `podiumStandingRow` crowned a champion from roots the client had stopped reading. One function rather than the two byte-identical copies `unlockDay.ts` and `dailyEmail.ts` each kept, because the drop was one defect written twice.
  - `clampReaggregatedTotal(total)` (#1152, Codex P2 on PR [#1165](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1165))—**a total RE-AGGREGATED from a row's per-Day buckets is clamped to the same bound the buckets are.** Clamping each bucket is not enough, because two of the three ranking surfaces do not rank by the buckets: `podiumStandingRow` here and its `src/data/finale.ts` twin re-sum the non-ceremonial buckets, `standingsThrough` (`dailyEmailContent.ts`) re-sums the window's, and a sum of bounded counts is not itself bounded. `players/{uid}` validates no field (ADR 0001), so two buckets at `MAX_ARCHIVE_NUMBER` are reachable—and they gave their row a `2 * MAX_ARCHIVE_NUMBER` podium and email score while the live Leaderboard and the frozen record read that same row's ROOT as `MAX_ARCHIVE_NUMBER`, so a second row tied it on the board and lost to it on the podium. The clamp is applied ONCE to the finished total rather than per addend: a count may be negative for the same reason it may be oversized, and clamping as the sum accumulates would let the order the buckets happen to be visited in decide the answer. A non-finite total reads `0`, the floor `readableRankingCount` already gives an unreadable count. Mirror of `clampReaggregatedTotal` in `src/data/eventLimits.ts`, pinned against it by `tests/functions/finale-parity.test.ts`.
  - The ranking + tutorial-exclusion semantics MIRROR `src/game/logic.ts`'s `comparePlayers` / `eventFirstBingoAt` (the app and functions packages are deliberately decoupled, like `autohide.ts` from `moderation.ts`).
- `src/data/finale.ts` (new)—the client mirror:
  - `buildPodium(players, days, dayMetas?, dayMetasLoaded?, freezeAt?, bannedUids?)`—the same podium the scheduler posts, computed client-side from the live `PlayerDoc` aggregates + per-Day honors, AS OF `freezeAt`. Carries `playRecorded` with its own `anyMarksRecorded` mirror (#1192): no client surface reads it yet—the farewell view OMITS the champion block rather than asserting anything about who played, which is why it never had the bug—and it moves with its twin anyway, because a fact present on one side of this mirror and absent on the other is the shape the drift the parity test exists to catch takes. The cutoff is load-bearing rather than cosmetic: a ceremonial Day keeps recording Marks after the freeze (its bucket is retained so its own daily honour still renders), so without it a post-freeze bingo on a ceremonial `tutorial: false` Day mints a First to BINGO the scheduler's already-posted, immutable podium Moment does not have—the Card and the Feed naming different winners. `buildPodiumPayload` takes the same parameter so the parity test can feed both post-freeze data. The champion/standings EXCLUDE every ceremonial Day's contribution, so a post-freeze goodbye mark never moves the podium (the podium is "as of `frozenAt`", not live). A schedule with no ceremonial Day excludes nothing, and a Player row with no `dayStats` keeps its root totals—in both cases re-summing the buckets would rewrite a legacy/hybrid row rather than leave it alone.

    **It takes the RAW roster and hides by `bannedUids`, and returns honours and positions under their two different rules** (`specs/w2-leaderboard.md` § Design decisions, "a position closes the gap; an honour vacates"). `Board` used to ban-filter the roster on the way IN, which is indistinguishable from a roster the banned Player was never on: `standings[0]` became the runner-up, `eventFirstBingoWinner` picked the next-earliest bingo, and `perDayHonors` derived a Day's honour for whoever was left—so the closing Day's banner and its share card crowned a champion, a ⭐ and a daily honoree the Feed's own podium Moment for the same Event withheld, three reassignments the ban policy forbids in one render. The hiding is applied to the OUTPUT instead: `champion` / `firstBingo` / `dailyHonors` go through `withholdBannedHonours`—the single statement of that rule, shared with the Feed Moment's renderer—while `standings` (the top `PODIUM_STANDING_ROWS` rows, replacing the champion-plus-`runnersUp` pairing) is numbered 1..n over the rows a reader can see. Each row carries its own `rank`, so the share composition marks the champion's row **by uid** rather than assuming it is row 1: the two coincide on an unmoderated Event and come apart exactly when the honour is withheld. With an empty ban roster the withholding step is the identity, which is what keeps this byte-identical to `buildPodiumPayload` and the parity test meaningful—the Moment is written unfiltered by contract, so a ban is the one input the two builders are never handed together.
  - `finaleDayIndex(days, freezeAt)`—the ONE definition of which Day the finale lives on: the LAST Day still open at the Standings Freeze, else `-1`. Read by both the default-view pin and the podium's mount gate in `Board.tsx`, so those cannot disagree. Keyed on the FREEZE rather than on the first ceremonial Day: the two coincide whenever the freeze is derived (a derived freeze IS that Day's unlock), but a schedule with an early ceremonial Day, later competitive Days and an end-of-Event freeze would otherwise strand the podium mid-Event. A Day's Scoring Policy says whether its Marks count; it does not elect the finale's host.
  - `finalePinIndex(days, frozenAt, now)`—the default-view pin: `finaleDayIndex`'s Day once `frozenAt` is set AND that Day is unlocked, else `null` (fall back to the normal "today" default). Never pins before the freeze. Renamed from `farewellPinIndex` (ADR 0011): an Event whose final morning is competitive has no ceremonial card to pin, and falling back to "today" after the Event ends lands on a Day the schedule no longer has.
- `src/lastCallCopy.ts` (new, #800; extracted from `src/components/ProofFeed.tsx`)—`lastCallLineFromPlayers(players, freezePhrase?)`, the client's ban-aware reconstruction of the last-call line: `ProofFeed.tsx` cannot simply display the scheduler's posted `line` string, because a Player banned AFTER the beat posted must still be removable from a still-live leaderboard read, so the Feed re-derives the sentence from the Moment's `lastCall.players`, filtered to exclude any now-banned uid. It takes `moment.lastCall.freezePhrase` as its freeze-phrase argument (falling back to the historical literal only for a legacy Moment posted before #800) rather than an independently hardcoded string, mirroring `lastCallStandingsCopy`'s leader/margin phrasing (a parity test, `tests/functions/lastcall-copy-parity.test.ts`, pins the two together).
- `src/components/FarewellPodium.tsx` (new)—the closing view's podium banner (champion + Event-wide First to BINGO + ten daily-honor rows), rendered ABOVE the goodbye banner (`d15-tutorial-banners` owns the goodbye copy/mount; this ticket owns the podium banner and their stacking order). Issue #449 later added the podium's own share affordance—a "Share final standings" button at the bottom of the section rendering the same frozen `Podium` payload as an on-device Share Card; the card and share mechanics are owned by `specs/w2-share-cards.md` § Final standings.
- `src/components/Board.tsx` (modify)—mount the podium above the goodbye banner on the FINALE Day once frozen (gated on `finaleDayIndex`, not on the viewed Day's pool), and pin that Day as the default view once the Event has ended (`finalePinIndex`). It passes `useLeaderboard`'s RAW roster and lets `EventDoc.bannedUids` reach `buildPodium` through the `event` prop; filtering the roster here is what promoted (above).
- `functions/src/unlockDay.ts`—`finaleTimes(days, standingsFreezeAt?)` resolves the beat clock. The freeze/podium anchor is the Event's Standings Freeze: the configured `EventDoc.standingsFreezeAt` when the doc carries a usable one, else the first ceremonial Day's own `unlockAt`—which on both live Events is the closing Day's 08:00, exactly the instant the superseded `pool === 'closing'` lookup produced. `null` when the Event has NEITHER, so callers skip the finale entirely; the finale is a stated freeze or a stated policy, never merely the schedule ending. `podiumDayIndex` is the ceremonial Day when there is one, else the LAST Day, and `lastCallDayIndex` is clamped at 0 so a one-Day schedule cannot file a Moment under Day -1.
- `EventDoc.frozenPlayRecorded?: boolean` ([#1218](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1218))—the podium's "did anybody play" fact, CAPTURED AT THE FREEZE. `runFinaleBeats` computes it with the shared `anyMarksRecorded` over the roster read at the freeze arm and writes it in the SAME transaction as `frozenAt` (both freeze paths: the atomic Most-Loved one and the compat stamp), so the fact and the stamp land together or not at all. The roster read is taken just before that transaction rather than inside its read set, which is the one deliberate difference from the Most-Loved award: `players/{uid}` is the hottest collection in the Event at exactly 08:00, and adding it to the read set would let every concurrent Mark abort the one transaction the finale cannot lose. Nothing is given up—a count carries no instant, so an in-transaction read would be no more "as of the cutoff", and what the fix needs is that the value be FIXED at the first successful freeze rather than recomputed by each later retry. The witnesses are the roster AND the Days' pinned honours, read together (Codex P2 [`4058671218`](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1242#discussion_r4058671218)): a `players/{uid}` row is client-authoritative and validates no field (ADR 0001), so its holder can clear the counts that earned a Day's honour, while `meta.firstBingo` is server-written and stands—a roster-only capture therefore stored `false` for an Event whose own Moment printed the pin beside it. A pin counts exactly as the podium payload counts one, over the same Days, so the two halves of one Moment cannot contradict each other; no cutoff is introduced by reading them, for the reason the capture takes no cutoff at all. **READ COMPLETENESS IS PART OF THE ANSWER** (CodeRabbit Major on [PR #1242](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1242)): `readDayHonors` turns a Day whose `meta` doc could not be read into no entry, which is right for the payload—one unreadable Day costs one honour line—and wrong for a fact that gates a claim, because it makes an unread pin indistinguishable from a Day that pinned nothing. The capture therefore reads the pins through `readDayHonorsWithCompleteness`, which reports both what it got and whether that is everything, and resolves three ways: `true` on ANY successful signal proving play (a Mark on the roster, or a pin that WAS read), since evidence in hand does not need the remaining reads to agree; `false` only when the roster read AND every honour read succeeded and none proved play, because that is the answer carrying a claim; UNKNOWN otherwise. The two witnesses are SETTLED INDEPENDENTLY ([#1263](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1263), Codex P2 [`4074863795`](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1242#discussion_r4074863795)): a roster read that fails does not discard a pin that WAS read, which still answers `true`, and only a capture in which no read proved play and some read failed comes out unknown. Every read here is best-effort in the same direction: a failure costs the Event its `playRecorded`, never its freeze. UNKNOWN IS WRITTEN AS AN EXPLICIT `null`, not as an omitted key (Codex P2 [`4058671215`](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1242#discussion_r4058671215)): because this field is admin-writable (below), an UNFROZEN Event can already carry a value, and a freeze that wrote only what it learned would stamp `frozenAt` beside that guess and publish it as the frozen answer on this run and every podium retry after it. The freeze therefore states the unknown in the same update, and `null` and absence read identically everywhere downstream. The beat still omits `playRecorded` from the Moment for both. **A frozen `false` keeps every post-freeze honour off the board** ([#1263](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1263), Codex P2 [`4074863801`](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1242#discussion_r4074863801)): `false` is stored only when the freeze read the roster and every Day's pin and found nothing. The beat's own honour read carries no cutoff—and a ceremonial Day pins after the freeze by design—so a podium RETRY could find a pin acquired after the snapshot and post it beside the frozen `false`, and the email, which lets a named honour outrank a stored `false`, would print a played board the freeze had ruled out. When the frozen answer is `false`, the beat therefore drops every daily honour pinned at or after the freeze cutoff (the same `>= freezeAt` bound the builder applies to the First to BINGO); the champion and the ⭐ are already resolved as of the freeze by the builder and stand as built. It FILTERS BY INSTANT rather than blanking the board ([PR #1268](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1268), Codex P1 [`4088048309`](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1268#discussion_r4088048309)): the field is admin-writable (below), so a `false` rewritten between the freeze and a podium retry must not be able to erase a champion, a ⭐ or a pin the freeze itself could have seen from the write-once Moment. `true` and unknown keep the payload as built, since neither makes a claim a later honour could contradict. **A FAILED FREEZE is not, and the podium waits for it** (CodeRabbit on [PR #1242](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/pull/1242)): `runFinaleBeats` posts the podium Moment only when the run either stamped `frozenAt` itself or found the Event already stamped, and on any other outcome—the transaction throwing, a missing Event document, an archive that closed it first—it logs and defers the beat to the next sweep. The two stay decoupled in the direction Codex #228 asked for, a freeze that landed on an EARLIER run never holding a podium retry back, and only in that direction: the Moment is written once and never amended, so a run that posts it with nothing captured leaves the later, successful freeze with nowhere to put what it learned—the empty-board message restored permanently, for exactly the ceremonial-Squares-and-no-honour Event this field exists for. Deferring costs nothing, because the podium beat is guarded on its Moment's absence and `finaleCompletedAt` on the same Moment: the finale stays honestly unfinished until it lands. Unlike `finaleCompletedAt`, it is NOT in `firestore.rules`' no-client-writes set—the Event arm sits at Firestore's 1000-expression cap ([#1142](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/fiveacross/issues/1142))—so an Event admin can still write it, exactly as they can `frozenAt` beside it. The exposure is one sentence of email copy, plus the post-freeze ceremonial honours a frozen `false` drops, rather than a gate on the podium the freeze could see, which is why it rides the existing admin gate rather than waiting for room on that arm.
- `src/types.ts` / `src/data/converters.ts`—`EventDoc.frozenAt?: number` (ms epoch) is the freeze stamp; a missing field reads as `undefined` (unset) through `eventConverter`'s passthrough. (Landed with the #212 schema contract.) `LastCallMomentPayload.freezePhrase?: string` (#800) is the scheduler's computed freeze phrase, persisted on the `last_call` Moment. `EventDoc.standingsFreezeAt?: number` (ADR 0011) is the CONFIGURED freeze—the schedule, where `frozenAt` is the stamp—and `DayDef.scoring?: 'competitive' | 'ceremonial'` is the Day's Scoring Policy. Both are OPTIONAL, because no doc written before ADR 0011 carries either, and both are resolved on READ rather than read directly: `scoringForDay` (`src/game/scoring.ts`) falls back to the closing-pool derivation and `standingsFreezeAtFor` (`src/game/logic.ts`) to the first ceremonial Day's `unlockAt`. `eventConverter` fills both, but the RESOLVERS are the contract—several data paths hydrate raw documents no converter has touched, the same reason `normalizePool` exists.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Given** `finaleTimes.lastCallAt` has arrived (20:00 on Day 9 in the standard shape; earlier, relative to the close, when #784's same-date fallback applies), **when** the scheduled trigger fires, **then** a last-call Moment posts naming the current leader and their margin. (Test: last-call-copy.)
- **Given** the Event's Standings Freeze, **when** the last-call Moment's freeze phrase is built, **then** it quotes that ACTUAL instant formatted in the Event's timezone (not a hardcoded 8 a.m.), and the client's ban-aware reconstruction quotes the SAME phrase from the persisted Moment rather than an independently formatted one. (Test: freeze-phrase, #800.)
- **Given** the Event's Standings Freeze has arrived (08:00 on Day 10 in the standard shape, where it is the ceremonial Day's own `unlockAt`), **when** the scheduled trigger fires, **then** `frozenAt` is set, the closing Day unlocks, and a podium Moment posts with the champion, Event-wide First to BINGO, and ten daily honors. (Test: podium-payload.)
- **Given** an Event whose FINAL Day is competitive and which states its own `standingsFreezeAt`, **when** the beats are resolved, **then** the finale still runs, that Day's marks count right up to the configured instant, the podium files under the last Day, and the last-call copy quotes the configured freeze rather than any Day's unlock. (Test: competitive-close.)
- **Given** the Event has ended (`frozenAt` set) and the finale Day is unlocked, **when** a Player opens the app, **then** that Day (podium included) is the default view AND the podium actually mounts there; before the freeze it is never pinned. (Test: default-view-pin.)
