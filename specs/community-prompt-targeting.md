# community-prompt-targeting—an intended Day carried through submission, approval and snapshot

Part of the Community Prompts epic (#533); implements #557. A **Community Prompt** (CONTEXT.md § Community Prompt) is a Prompt a Player suggested during an Event, approved by an Admin, and dealt onto a later Day Card with the submitter attributed. Player-facing copy is **"put it on tomorrow's card"**—never "bingo moment", which would collide with **Moment**.

Before this ticket a suggestion had no Day. `addItem` wrote a `pending` row (`d15-approvals`), an Admin approved it to `active`, and the next Day to freeze picked up every active item in its pool—so an approved suggestion entered **every** not-yet-snapshotted Day's snapshot. The suggestion was placed everywhere and therefore nowhere in particular. This ticket gives it one Day and keeps it there.

## The shape of the change

The whole feature is one optional field plus one predicate. A Prompt names the Day it is meant for; the snapshot admits a named Prompt to that Day alone. Everything else—roll-forward, retention, the never-mutate-a-frozen-Day invariant—falls out of that pair rather than being enforced separately.

**Nothing here mutates a Day.** A Day's snapshot is frozen once, at its `unlockAt`, and `stampDaySnapshot` refuses to overwrite an existing stamp. Targeting is therefore only ever a statement about the future: routing a Prompt is a write to the **Prompt**, and a Day learns about it only when that Day freezes and reads the pool. The invariant "an already-snapshotted or dealt Day is never mutated" holds by construction, not by a guard that could be forgotten.

### Why the eligibility bar is `unlockAt`, not "has a snapshot yet"

A Day whose `unlockAt` has passed but which the scheduler has not stamped yet looks open and is not. `activeSnapshotIds` filters the frozen pool **as of `day.unlockAt`**, so a Prompt approved after that instant is dropped by the cutoff filter even though the stamp had not landed when it was approved. Treating such a Day as eligible would promise a placement that silently never happens—the organiser is told "scheduled for Day 4" and Day 4 deals without it. A Day is targetable only when it is **both** unstamped **and** still ahead of its own `unlockAt`. That is also why an empty `snapshotItemIds: []` counts as closed: it is a real stamp (`isDueForSnapshot`'s idempotency rule), not an absence.

### The clock routing trusts, and how far that trust reaches

Routing compares `unlockAt` against the approving Admin's **device** clock, and stamps that same instant as `approvedAt`. That is the pre-existing approval contract (#210) and the app-wide posture (ADR 0001: the client is authoritative; nothing here writes a `serverTimestamp`), not something targeting introduces—and a server-authoritative instant is not available to this decision anyway: `serverTimestamp()` is a write-time sentinel whose value the client cannot read *before* the commit that routing has to decide. Genuinely server-authoritative approval means moving the write into a Cloud Function, which is a change to the approval architecture rather than to this feature.

What bounds the exposure is that eligibility does not rest on the clock alone. `isDayTargetable` also requires `snapshotItemIds == null`—a fact written by the **scheduler**, server-side, and read inside the approval transaction. So a skewed clock can never route a Prompt into a Day that has already frozen: the never-mutate-a-dealt-Day invariant is carried by the stamp, not by wall time. The residual window is exactly the one this section already names—a Day past its real `unlockAt` that the scheduler has not stamped yet—and within it a slow clock can keep a Day targetable slightly too long (the Prompt lands on the Day it was aimed at, or is retained) while a fast clock rolls forward one Day early. Both are bounded by the skew, both stay inside the "which Day" question, and neither can mutate a frozen Day, leak a suggestion outside its Event, or deal anything before an organiser approves it.

### And why the Day must also deal the main pool

A Day is targetable only if it deals the **main** pool. Every Community Prompt begins as a main-pool submission—`addItem` writes `pool: 'main'` and `firestore.rules` admit nothing else from a non-admin—while #558 lets the approving Admin atomically classify it as Easy (`embark`) or Exploratory (`main`). A main Day freezes both classifications, whereas a curated Day freezes only its own pool (`snapshotPoolsFor`: main → main+easy, easy → easy, closing → closing). A suggestion aimed at a Tutorial or closing Day would therefore pass every Day, timing and moderation check and then be dropped by the snapshot's **pool** filter. That is the same broken promise the `unlockAt` bar exists to prevent, arriving by a different route: on the med-2026 shape, every suggestion made in the run-up to the closing Day would have been reported as scheduled and then dealt nowhere. Such a suggestion is retained instead, which is the honest outcome. Legacy persisted pool spellings normalize first, so a Day storing `embark`/`farewell` behaves identically.

This bar has a second effect worth naming. `bulkApproveItems` stamps one shared `approvedAt` across the batch, and the snapshot cutoff key is `approvedAt ?? createdAt`; because routing only ever selects Days whose `unlockAt` is still in the future, every row of a bulk approve lands on the same side of every Day's cutoff. A bulk approve cannot split across a freeze.

## Contract

- `src/domainTypes.d.ts`—`ItemDoc.targetDayIndex?: number` is the intended Day. **Absent** is the untargeted case and means every not-yet-snapshotted Day: the organiser/seed pool, and every Prompt written before this field existed, so the existing Event needs no backfill and its behaviour is unchanged. **Present** means a Community Prompt aimed at that one Day. `ItemDoc.retainedAt?: number` is stamped at approval when no eligible Day remained.
- `src/data/communityPrompts.ts` (new, pure—no Firestore)—`isDayTargetable(day, now)` (unstamped, still ahead of its `unlockAt`, and dealing the main pool), `targetableDays`, `defaultTargetDayIndex` (the earliest still-open Day: "tomorrow's card"), `routeApprovalToDay(days, intendedDayIndex, now)`, and `isUsableTarget` (a non-negative integer, matching what the rules admit).
- `src/data/api.ts`—`addItem(uid, text, spicy, targetDayIndex?)`. An omitted target is resolved from the Event's schedule to the earliest still-open Day, so a suggestion carries its intended Day from the moment it is written. An explicit value is the override seam for a Day picker (#559), and an explicit value that is **malformed throws**: omitting the argument means "resolve the default", whereas passing a bad one is a caller bug, and quietly dropping it would write an untargeted row—every future main Day, the exact failure this ticket removes, arriving through the one path that is supposed to *set* the target. `NaN`, `-1` and `1.5` are all valid TypeScript `number`s, so only a runtime check catches them; `firestore.rules` would reject the value at the door anyway, and throwing turns a silent mis-placement into an obvious programming error at the call site. Resolution of an *omitted* target writes **no** target when the read SUCCEEDS and there is simply no Day to aim at—a schedule-less legacy Event, a Day-less schedule, or no Day that can still take one. Those are known states, and untargeted is the honest record of them. A **failed read is not**, and it propagates rather than falling back to untargeted: an untargeted row does not lose the targeting, it means *every* future main Day, so a transient offline blip would put one suggestion on every card of the cruise. That is the same mistake as silently dropping an explicit malformed argument, and it fails closed for the same reason—costing the player a retry with their text still in the box, rather than costing the organiser every Day. The field is **omitted**, never written as `null`: absent is the state every pre-#557 Prompt already has, so the snapshot filter needs no third case.
- `src/data/admin.ts`—`approveItems(rows, adminUid)` is the approval write, with `approveItem` (one row) and `bulkApproveItems` (the queue's full list) as its two entry points. Per Prompt: keep the intended Day when it is still open; otherwise roll **forward** to the earliest open Day after it; otherwise retain. A **malformed persisted target retains too**, and is reported as retention rather than as ordinary untargeted content: the snapshot already excludes such a row from every Day, so it is dealt nowhere, and describing it as untargeted would tell the organiser it is live on every Day while it is live on none. Only a strictly **absent** field is the untargeted case—matching `targetsDay`'s rule, so a stored `null` retains here as well. The malformed value is left in place rather than repaired: the write is a merge, and guessing which Day was meant would be inventing one. It returns an `ApprovalPlacement[]` (`{ itemId, dayIndex, retained, outcome }`) so the console can say where each Prompt landed. `dayIndex`/`retained` describe where the Prompt now **stands**; `outcome` describes what this call **did** (`placed` / `untargeted` / `retained` wrote; `stale` / `missing` did not). Keeping the two apart is what stops a consumer announcing "scheduled for Day 3" for a row that was actually rejected, or for one that no longer exists. #558 adds the Admin's `main`/`easy` classification to the caller row and atomically persists it only when this same transaction actually approves a still-pending row; routing continues to use the authoritative stored target.

A **pending** row with no target is a player submission that lost one, not a Prompt meant for every Day, so approval **resolves** the Day it should have had. That holds by construction: organiser and seed Prompts are created `active` through the rules' admin-active-create arm and never enter this queue, so everything approval sees is a suggestion aimed at one Day. It also closes the gap the create rule cannot cheaply close—`validTargetDayIndex()` must permit an absent field, since "no Day was available" is a legitimate state, and it cannot tell that apart from a cached or crafted client simply declining to say. Left alone, such a submission would be approved onto every Day: the feature's central failure mode, reached around the front door. If the schedule has nothing left to resolve to, the Prompt is retained, exactly as a targeted one with nowhere to go. The single exception is an Event with **no schedule at all**, where untargeted is the honest record: there are no Days, so "every Day" is the one legacy board.

Approval reads **authoritative** state: each item is read inside the transaction, only a still-`pending` row is approved, and routing acts on the **stored** `targetDayIndex` rather than the one the caller passed. The caller's row is a client snapshot and can be stale, which is not a theoretical worry—two organisers holding the same queue row would otherwise double-approve it: the first places the Prompt on Day 2, Day 2 freezes with its id, and the second finds Day 2 closed, rolls forward, and rewrites the Prompt for Day 3, which then freezes with it too. The Prompt would be dealt on **two** Days—the one outcome this ticket exists to prevent—and because no Day is ever mutated on the way there, nothing downstream would catch it. A row that has moved on is a no-op reported where it actually stands, so a double-click or a stale queue is harmless; a row that has vanished is reported, not invented. `approveItem` still takes the queue row because that is what the queue holds. Its `id` remains the only caller field load-bearing for routing; #558 additionally makes its validated `pool` load-bearing for the Admin's approval-time difficulty decision.
- `functions/src/unlockDay.ts`—`SnapshotFilter.dayIndex` and the `targetsDay` predicate inside `activeSnapshotIds`, applied alongside the existing pool / free-space / report / ban / cutoff filters, plus a `retainedAt` exclusion. Retention is honoured from the **stored marker** rather than re-derived from the schedule: an unreachable target only means "dealt nowhere" for the schedule as it stood at approval, and adding a Day, repairing one, or switching a curated Day to the main pool would silently resurrect a Prompt the organiser was already told was retained. `retainedAt` is written only by a retention and cleared by any placement, so honouring it makes the recorded decision the thing that binds. `snapshotItemsFrom` projects `targetDayIndex` **raw**, so a malformed value stays distinguishable from an absent one. Both call sites (`stampDaySnapshot`, `resnapshotDayIfNoBoards`) pass the Day's own `index`. `dayIndex` is optional, so a caller that omits it keeps the pre-#557 no-targeting behaviour. The predicate is a **local mirror** of the client rule rather than a shared import, exactly as `isReportHidden` and `isBanned` already are—this module deliberately imports nothing from the app package.
- `firestore.rules`—two helpers on `items/{itemId}`. `validTargetDayIndex()` on **create**: `targetDayIndex` is optional but, when present, must be a non-negative `int`. `targetDayIndexUnchangedOrValid()` on **update**, applied to the admin arm: *who* may move a Prompt's Day already followed from the pre-existing non-admin bound `hasOnly(['reportCount'])`, but *what* they may move it to was unbounded, so the rules could still mint the malformed target every other layer fails closed on. No legitimate admin operation needs to—approval writes a routed integer, and a repair writes a real Day or removes the field. The check is conditioned on the field actually **changing**, which is load-bearing: retaining a row that already carries a malformed target stamps `retainedAt` *without* touching the value, and an unconditional check would deny exactly the write that retains it.
- `src/components/admin/ReviewQueue.tsx`—the Approvals queue passes the row to `approveItem`; #558 overlays the Admin's explicit Easy/Exploratory classification on that row so the approval transaction can persist it atomically.

### Rolling forward, and rolling nowhere

Roll-forward is strictly **forward**. A Prompt written for Day 7 is never dealt onto Day 5, even when Day 5 is still open: rolling backward would place it somewhere it was never meant to be, and "the organiser was late" is not a reason to change what the Player suggested it for.

`retainedAt` is an **approval-time** fact, and only a retention writes one: a placement **clears** any marker already on the row rather than merely not writing one, because `tx.update` is a merge and a survivor would describe an active, dealt Prompt as one that was retained and dealt nowhere—the mirror of the malformed-target misreport, and just as misleading. The route that put a stale one there is a submitter supplying it on create—`firestore.rules` now refuses that on the non-admin arm, it being the one forged field a normal approval would *not* overwrite, unlike `approvedAt`. (Re-approving an already-retained Prompt is not a second route: the stale-approval guard only approves a still-`pending` row, so that flow does not exist.) The clear is therefore the second line behind the rules bar rather than the only one. The rules bar and the clear are two halves of one guarantee: only a retention stamps it, and anything else unstamps it.

When nothing remains, the Prompt is **retained**: still `active`, still in the pool for the recap or a reusable pack, with its original—now unreachable—`targetDayIndex` left in place and `retainedAt` stamped. It is never dropped and never deleted. Leaving the original target is load-bearing: **clearing** it would make the Prompt untargeted, which reads as "every Day"—the one outcome this ticket exists to prevent. So retention needs no new status value; an unreachable target already means "admitted nowhere", and `retainedAt` exists only so the state is a stored fact rather than something every consumer re-derives from the Day schedule.

### A malformed target fails closed

`targetsDay` admits a Prompt whose target is absent to every Day, one whose target is a **non-negative integer** matching this Day to that Day, and **nothing else**—including a Prompt whose `targetDayIndex` is present but malformed (a string, `NaN`, a fraction, or an explicit `null`). "Absent" means strictly `undefined`: a stored `null` is a value, and a malformed one. The rules reject `null` on create, but the admin update arm is deliberately unconstrained and an imported or repaired row can carry one, so reading `null` as absent would put that Prompt back on every Day. That is deliberately against `unlockDay.ts`'s usual fail-open posture, which the moderation predicates follow. The asymmetry is the point: failing open on moderation risks showing one item that should have been hidden, whereas failing open on targeting would put an unresolvable Prompt on **every single card**, which is the exact bug being fixed. An excluded Prompt is retained, not lost—it stays `active` and admin-visible, and no Day is harmed by its absence. The rules reject malformed targets at the door anyway; this is the second line.

The other two ends of the path fail closed the same way, so "malformed" never decays into "every Day" anywhere. At **submission**, an explicit malformed argument to `addItem` throws instead of being dropped into an untargeted write. At **approval**, a malformed persisted target is reported as **retained** rather than as untargeted: the snapshot excludes it from every Day, so retention is what actually happens, and saying otherwise would misreport a Prompt that is live nowhere as live everywhere. All three ends agree that only a strictly absent field means "every Day".

### Why approval runs in a transaction

`approveItems` writes only to items, yet reads the Event doc inside a transaction. The read exists to put the **schedule** in the transaction's read set. The scheduler stamps snapshots by updating that same doc, so if a Day freezes while an approval is in flight, Firestore retries and the routing is recomputed against the schedule that actually won. Without it an approval could commit "scheduled for Day 4" microseconds after Day 4 froze, and the Prompt would be silently retained while the organiser was told it was placed. The already-frozen Day is left strictly alone either way.

It is worth stating what that does **not** buy, since the boundary is easy to overstate in both directions. The snapshot side is not the loose half: `stampDaySnapshot` reads its active-item query *through* its own transaction, in the same read set as the `tx.update` that stamps the Day, so the frozen ids and the committed pool always describe one Firestore state. What neither transaction can promise is the phantom edge—a row flipping `pending`→`active` is not a change to a document the scheduler's `status == 'active'` query matched when it ran. So the residual risk is that an approval landing in that instant is reported as placed on a Day whose snapshot does not list it: a **misreport of which Day**, and that is the worst case. The Day itself is safe by construction—the stamp is written once, re-confirmed absent inside the scheduler's transaction, and never overwritten. Making even the misreport impossible means approving on the server clock (#813), not a stronger client-side transaction.

## What this ticket does not do

- **The quota** (#558)—reserving 2–4 of the 24 squares for community Prompts inside the easy/exploratory ratio. Targeting decides *which Day* a Prompt can be dealt on; the quota decides *how many* get dealt.
- **Pool vocabulary**—writes keep emitting the legacy persisted values through the existing `persistedPool` / `normalizePool` coercion seam (CONTEXT.md § Pool). `targetDayIndex` is born canonical: it is a new field with no legacy spelling, and it is compared against `DayDef.index`, which was never renamed.

## Entry point, submitter states, and attribution (#559)

The entry point, submitter states, and attribution built on top of the targeting model above. Player-facing copy stays "put it on tomorrow's card"—never "bingo moment" (CONTEXT.md § Community Prompt).

- **The entry point**—`src/components/TomorrowsCardInvite.tsx`, ONE component mounted on the Card (`Board.tsx`) and the Feed (`ProofFeed.tsx`) rather than forked between them. Hidden whenever `targetableDays(days, now)` is empty—the same predicate the submission default already reads—so the invitation never promises a Day that does not exist. Mounted in EVERY render branch that could otherwise show it, not just the happy path: Board.tsx splices it into every one of its early returns (locked/waking preview, deal error, thin pool, cached fallback, the "Dealing…" spinner) alongside `daySwitcher`, and ProofFeed.tsx's empty-Feed early return carries it too (Codex P2 + CodeRabbit Major, PR #845 round 1—the entry point used to live only in each caller's happy-path return). **Known gap, deliberately deferred (Codex P2, PR #845 round 4):** `App.tsx`'s own Card-tab `dealError`/cached-fallback branch—reached on a join/deal failure or an offline start, BEFORE `Board.tsx` ever mounts—has no invite of its own, so Board's coverage above does not reach it. Fixing it means giving `App.tsx` (a deliberately frozen shell file, `./components/tabs`'s own mount-point map) its own live `useEventDoc` subscription, which broke `App.test.tsx`'s existing Board-is-mocked isolation by pulling in `../firebase`'s real module-load-time `getAuth()` call. Left as a follow-up rather than widening this PR's blast radius into the shell's test boundary for a non-blocking (discretionary-tier) finding. Each caller's own `now` is a ticking clock, not a per-render `Date.now()` (CodeRabbit Minor, PR #845 round 1), clamped to the 32-bit `setTimeout` max (Codex P1, PR #845 round 4—an Event whose next unlock is more than ~24.9 days out would otherwise overflow the delay and re-arm a near-instant timer forever; `admin/SchedulePanel.tsx` already carries the same clamp). Tapping it hands off through a tiny intent bridge (`src/hooks/useOpenSuggestPanel.ts`, the same in-memory one-pending-intent shape `useOpenSquare.ts` already uses for Feed → Board) to `More.tsx`'s existing "Suggest a square" panel (`ItemPool.tsx`, #203/#208)—never a second, forked submission form. The actual navigation is deliberately NOT performed by `Board.tsx` itself: `SuggestPanelBridge.tsx`, mounted once from `App.tsx` alongside `<Nav />`, does it instead, because `Board.tsx` is kept react-router-free by convention (its unit tests mount it standalone, with no `<Router>` wrapper) while `App.tsx` is always inside one. That bridge reads `navigate` through a "latest ref" rather than depending on it directly (Codex P2, PR #845 round 4): `useNavigate()`'s identity changes with the pathname under `BrowserRouter`, so a direct dependency re-ran the effect the instant the `/more` push landed—while the intent could still read pending—and pushed a SECOND history entry.
- **Submitter states**—`submitterStatus` (`src/data/communityPrompts.ts`) derives `'pending' | 'scheduled' | 'approved'` from a Prompt a submitter can actually READ: `pending` while `status === 'pending'`; `scheduled` once active with a usable `targetDayIndex` naming a Day that is STILL targetable (a live promise); `approved` for everything else active—untargeted (every Day), a target whose Day has already stamped or passed its cutoff, or retained (the `retainedAt` case is deliberately never re-derived as `scheduled` from its now-unreachable target). There is no `'rejected'`/"not selected" arm in this function: `firestore.rules`' item read rule carves out `status == 'pending'` only for a non-admin submitter, NOT `pending || rejected` (see this spec's own contract above), so a rejected row is unreadable by its own submitter and this function never sees one. "Not selected" is therefore a CLIENT-SIDE inference, not a read—`src/data/mySuggestions.ts`. `trackSuggestion`/`loadTrackedSuggestions` persist a per-device, per-Event, per-uid list of this browser's own submissions (id/text/submittedAt, `gcb.mySuggestions.{eventId}.{uid}`-keyed localStorage, capped at 20, fail-open on a storage error—the established `NoticeBanner`/`explicitTagSeenKey` pattern). `deriveMySubmissions` merges that local list against TWO LIVE queries, both the submitter's own and both unfiltered by the public pool's presentational hides (Codex P2, PR #845 round 4): `useMyPendingItems` and the sibling `useMyActiveItems` (`src/hooks/useData.ts`), which reads `where('createdBy','==',uid) + where('status','==','active')` directly rather than client-filtering `useItems()`'s public pool—a Prompt the community auto-hide threshold, the adult-content posture, or a since-banned author has presentationally dropped from that pool is still genuinely `active`, and a submitter checking their OWN status must see that, not `'not_selected'`. Every row the live pending query returns is `'pending'`, cross-device, exactly as before; a tracked id found in the active-mine query resolves through `submitterStatus`; a tracked id found in NEITHER, once BOTH queries have delivered a server-confirmed (`hasServerData`) snapshot—not merely cleared `loading`, which an offline/cold-cache start also does—falls back to that entry's `lastKnownStatus` rather than jumping straight to `'not_selected'` (Codex P2, PR #845 round 7). Two different gaps can otherwise misreport a genuinely-approved submission that way: (1) `pendingMine`/`activeMine` are independent listeners, so if an admin approves while the panel is open, the pending-removal snapshot can land one render before the active-addition snapshot, leaving the id in neither query transiently; (2) an admin can hard-hide an ALREADY-approved Prompt later (`hideItem`), which drops it from the `status == 'active'` query permanently—hidden is unreadable to the submitter the same way rejected is, but is a different fact the client cannot tell apart from the id alone. `TrackedSuggestion` therefore also carries optional `lastKnownStatus`/`lastKnownDayIndex` fields, stamped by a new pure `refreshLastKnownStatuses(tracked, activeMine, days, now)` (`mySuggestions.ts`) every time `ItemPool.tsx`'s own effect sees an entry it is tracking show up active, and persisted as one normalized/capped snapshot through `refreshAndPersistLastKnownStatuses` only when something actually changed (the refresh function returns the SAME array reference otherwise, so the effect skips the write with a plain `===` check). `lastKnownDayIndex` mirrors whatever `targetDayIndex` the active row currently carries, independent of the derived status—the same convention `deriveMySubmissions`' own live-`dayIndex` field already follows—so it can still read a Day number after a scheduled submission's Day stamps and the status moves on to `'approved'`; only the UI's own render gates the "· Day N" suffix on `status === 'scheduled'`, not this module. A tracked id truly never observed active still falls all the way to `'not_selected'`, unchanged. Two more gaps in that same fallback surfaced in round 8, both against a submission this device has NEVER before seen active (round 7's cache only helps once something has actually been observed). (a) The FIRST-time version of round 7's cross-listener race: an admin approving a submission for the first time has no `lastKnownStatus` to fall back to at all, so the SAME pending-removal-before-active-addition overlap still read `'not_selected'` the instant the pending listener fired. `ItemPool.tsx` now keeps a bounded `APPROVAL_GRACE_MS` (5s) window per id that just vacated `myPending` with no active arrival yet, reporting `'pending'` for that window (`deriveMySubmissions`'s `graceIds` parameter) rather than a guess. Round 8's FIRST cut tracked this window as a plain ref-diff, with no timer of its own—correct for the race it targeted, but Codex P2 on PR #845 round 9 caught the deeper bug: mutating a ref never itself schedules a re-render, so a submission that is GENUINELY rejected—no active snapshot ever arriving to trigger one—had nothing left to age the window out at all, and could report `'pending'` forever. The fix is a REAL `setTimeout` per graced id (`graceIds` is now `useState`, not a ref): the timer's own fire is what closes the window if nothing resolved by then, and a second effect clears the timer EARLY the moment the id actually turns up in `activeMine`, so a fast, genuine approval does not have to wait out the full window either way—only a submission with no active arrival ever ages out, and only after the window, never sooner. (b) A cached `'scheduled'` fallback is a promise about a SPECIFIC Day, true only while that Day is still targetable—the same rule a live `submitterStatus` read enforces. A submission observed scheduled and then hard-hidden BEFORE its Day's cutoff has no way to be re-observed and self-correct, so `deriveMySubmissions` now re-derives a cached `'scheduled'` fallback against the CURRENT `days`/`now` rather than replaying the label verbatim: once the named Day stops being targetable (stamped, past cutoff, or no longer in the schedule at all), the fallback downgrades to `'approved'` with no `dayIndex`—still-approved is the honest last-known fact, exactly as a live read would report for any other Day-cutoff-passed row.

One more fallback gap, round 10 (Codex P2, PR #845): the fallback TEXT had the same staleness bug the round-7 status/target fix already fixed for status. An admin may edit a submission's wording before or after approval, and once observed active the fallback already preferred that edited text over the locally-tracked original—but only while `active` was directly available. The instant the same submission is hard-hidden, `deriveMySubmissions` fell straight back to `t.text`, silently reverting to the PRE-EDIT original wording even though the status/target fallback kept reporting the correct (still-approved) state. `TrackedSuggestion` now also carries `lastKnownText`, re-stamped by `refreshLastKnownStatuses` alongside `lastKnownStatus`/`lastKnownDayIndex` every time the entry is observed active, and preferred over `t.text` in the fallback—so a hard-hidden submission's displayed wording stays the last text this device actually saw live, not whatever was originally typed.

`addItem` now returns `{ id, targetDayIndex? }` (`AddItemResult`, `src/data/api.ts`; was `Promise<void>`) so `ItemPool.tsx` has both an id to track AND the target the write actually committed, for its `prompt_suggestion_submitted` analytics payload. `ItemPool.tsx` also carries its own ticking clock (the same pattern as `TomorrowsCardInvite`'s callers, clamped to the 32-bit `setTimeout` max), so a panel left open across a `scheduled` submission's target Day `unlockAt` re-evaluates to `'approved'` on its own. Scoped deliberately to the submitting device: a submission made elsewhere is still visible while `pending` (the live query), but its later follow-up is only as durable as this one device's `localStorage`—honest to lose on a device the player never reopens, rather than promising a cross-device guarantee the read rule does not support.
- **Attribution**—"Suggested by [player]" lives in Prompt detail (`ProofSheet.tsx`, the sheet opened by tapping a Square), never on the card tile. `Cell.communityPrompt`/`Cell.suggestedBy` (`domainTypes.d.ts`) are denormalized onto the dealt Cell at DEAL TIME by `dealBoard` (`src/game/logic.ts`, `DealItem.targetDayIndex`/`createdBy` carried through from every one of `data/api.ts`'s three deal paths—`joinAndDeal`, `dealDayCard`, `reshuffleBoard`)—the same idiom the pre-existing `echo`/`echoGeneration` Cell fields already use—so Board never needs to re-enable its live item-pool subscription post-deal (`useItems(!board)` stays disabled once a Board exists) just to know which Squares are Community Prompts. `dealBoard` mirrors `isUsableTarget` locally (`isUsableDealTarget`) rather than importing it, matching `functions/src/unlockDay.ts`'s own local-mirror rationale: `src/game/**` is the app's pure layer and every existing import edge runs `data/** → game/**`, never the reverse. `ProofSheet.tsx` resolves `suggestedBy`'s uid into a display name with ONE one-shot read (`fetchDisplayName`, `data/api.ts`) fired only while the sheet is open on a community Square—never a live subscription, mirroring `resolveDisplayName`'s saved-name-then-`'Anonymous'`-fallback rule.
- **"New from the group"**—a tile-level `.cell.community` accent ring (`Board.tsx`/`index.css`), not a corner glyph: every corner of a Square is already claimed (✓ top-right, echo ⟲/Doubt top-left, proof ＋ bottom-left, tally bottom-right), and a Square can carry several of those states at once, so a corner badge would contend for the same pixel. The ring composes with all of them instead. `squareLabel`'s screen-reader text carries the same note through every marked-state branch.
- **Analytics (`src/analytics.ts`'s `GA4_EVENTS` catalog, #33 catalog convention)**—`prompt_suggestion_submitted` (`ItemPool.tsx`, on a successful `addItem`), `prompt_suggestion_approved` (`admin/ReviewQueue.tsx`, once per `ApprovalPlacement` whose `outcome` is not `'stale'`/`'missing'`—wrapped around the approval call itself rather than chained onto the 18+ flip-confirm's `guard()` return, because `guard` can defer the real call into its own confirm-dialog handler and resolve its OWN promise with `undefined` immediately in that branch), and `community_prompt_dealt` (`data/api.ts`, fired once per genuinely NEW deal—AFTER each of the three transactions commits, never inside the retried callback—with an aggregate `count` of Community Prompt Squares on that card, mirroring `most_loved_photo_frozen`'s aggregate-not-per-item shape). None carries Prompt text—counts, ids, uids, and booleans only, per `specs/posthog-analytics.md` § "Payload hygiene".

### Persisted tracker normalization (#909)

Every tracker write stable-deduplicates by id: the first occurrence keeps its array position and later copies are discarded, then the 20-entry cap is applied by array position without sorting or trusting `submittedAt`. For a single-entry write, an existing id is replaced at its first occurrence's position; a genuinely new id is appended before normalization and capping. A multi-entry status refresh merges only its changed last-known fields into the latest persisted snapshot, preserving a newer append from another tab, then persists the complete normalized/capped result in one write. This prevents both a newly appended entry from being overwritten by a stale tab and entries evicted by the cap from being re-appended as apparently new by a later write from the same stale refresh pass.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Given** a Player submits a suggestion during an Event with an open Day ahead, **when** the write commits, **then** the row carries the submitter (`createdBy`), the submission time (`createdAt`), the intended Day (`targetDayIndex`), and moderation status `pending`; an organiser's edit through `adminUpdateItemText` is the final text that is dealt. (Tests: "stamps the earliest still-open Day when no target is given"; "honours an explicit target (the Day-picker seam, #559)".)
- **Given** an approved suggestion targeted at Day N, **when** any Day freezes, **then** Day N's snapshot contains it and no other Day's does; an untargeted Prompt still enters every Day's. (Tests: "admits a targeted Prompt to its OWN Day"; "admits a targeted Prompt to NO other Day—the whole point of the ticket"; "admits an untargeted Prompt to EVERY Day—the pre-#557 contract, unchanged".)
- **Given** an organiser approves after the intended Day's cutoff, **when** the write commits, **then** the Prompt is re-aimed at the next still-open Day that can actually deal it, never at an earlier one and never at a Day whose pool would drop it. (Tests: "rolls FORWARD to the next open Day when the intended Day has closed"; "never rolls BACKWARD onto an earlier Day"; "rolls PAST a curated Day to the next Day that deals the main pool"; "RETAINS rather than promising a closing Day when only curated Days remain".)
- **Given** no eligible Day remains, **when** the organiser approves, **then** the Prompt is retained—`active`, `retainedAt` stamped, original target intact, in no snapshot, and not deleted. (Tests: "RETAINS a Prompt with nowhere left to go, keeping its original target"; "a Prompt retained past the last Day is frozen into NO snapshot and still exists".)
- **Given** a Day that has already frozen or dealt, **when** anything in this feature runs, **then** that Day's `snapshotItemIds` is unchanged. (Tests: "leaves an ALREADY-STAMPED Day untouched—targeting never re-opens a frozen Day"; "never writes to a Day—an approval touches only the Prompt".)
- **Given** a pending suggestion, **when** anyone outside its Event reads it—including an Admin of a different Event—**then** the read is denied; only the submitter and the Event's own Admins can see it, and nothing is dealt until an Admin approves. (Tests: "DENIES an admin of ANOTHER Event reading this Event's pending suggestion"; "DENIES a non-admin approving it, with or without a target change".)
